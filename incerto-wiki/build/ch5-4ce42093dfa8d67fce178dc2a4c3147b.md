# Chapter 5 - Scalability and Power Laws

## What This Chapter Is Doing

Chapter 5 moves from the qualitative warning about fat tails to the mechanics
of scalable tails. The central question is: once a tail behaves like a power
law, what follows for moments, sums, extremes, and estimation?

This guide is a router. It links to concept pages for the mathematical atoms
and keeps the chapter-specific examples at the level of orientation rather than
reproducing Taleb's prose or figures.

## 5.1 - Scalability and Power Laws

The chapter's main formal bridge is regular variation:

$$
\bar F(x)=x^{-\alpha}L(x),
\qquad
\frac{\bar F(tx)}{\bar F(x)}\to t^{-\alpha}.
$$

Existing concept pages:

- [Regular Variation](../../concepts/theorems/regular-variation.md)
- [Karamata's Theorem](../../concepts/theorems/karamata.md)
- [Pareto Moment Existence](../../concepts/theorems/pareto-moment-existence.md)

The point is not merely that power laws are straight lines on log-log plots.
The point is that regular variation gives stable asymptotic rules for
integration, moments, and extremes.
