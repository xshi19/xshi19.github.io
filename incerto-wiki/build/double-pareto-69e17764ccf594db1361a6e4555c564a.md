# Symmetric Shifted Double Pareto Distribution

## Statement

We use the symmetric shifted convention implemented in `incerto`; the
name "double Pareto" is overloaded in the literature and can also refer to
positive-valued size distributions with different left and right exponents.
Let $Y$ have a [Pareto Type I distribution](pareto.md) with lower cutoff $1$ and
tail exponent $\alpha>0$.  Let $B$ be independent of $Y$ with
$\mathbb P(B=1)=\mathbb P(B=-1)=1/2$.  The shifted symmetric double Pareto
variable is

$$
X = B(Y-1).
$$

Its density on the real line is

$$
f(x;\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{2}(1+|x|)^{-(\alpha+1)},\qquad x\in\mathbb R.
$$

The CDF is

$$
F(x)=
\begin{cases}
\frac12(1-x)^{-\alpha}, & x<0,\\
1-\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha}, & x\ge 0.
\end{cases}
$$

For $x\ge0$, each one-sided tail has probability

$$
\mathbb P(X>x)=\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha},
\qquad
\mathbb P(X<-x)=\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha}.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\mathbb P(|X|>x)=(1+x)^{-\alpha}.
$$

More generally, a location-scale version $Z=\mu+sX$ with $s>0$ has density

$$
f_Z(z)=\frac{\alpha}{2s}
\left(1+\frac{|z-\mu|}{s}\right)^{-(\alpha+1)}.
$$

The ordinary mean exists and equals $0$ exactly when $\alpha>1$.  For
$\alpha\le1$, the positive and negative parts are both infinite, so symmetry
gives a center but not an ordinary expectation.  The variance is finite exactly
when $\alpha>2$, in which case

$$
\operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac{2}{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)}.
$$

The absolute moments unify these thresholds:

$$
\mathbb E|X|^p
=\frac{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(\alpha-p)}{\Gamma(\alpha)},
\qquad 0<p<\alpha.
$$

The symbols $F$, $f$, $\alpha$, $\mathbb E$, and $\operatorname{Var}$ follow
the shared [notation table](../../notation/index.md); $B$ is local to this
construction.

## Shape and two-sided tail intuition

The ordinary [Pareto distribution](pareto.md) is one-sided: the rare extreme is always on
the right.  The double Pareto keeps the same power-law magnitude but gives the
shock a sign.  This makes it useful for toy return models where both large gains
and large losses are possible, while preserving a transparent tail exponent.

```{code-cell} python
:label: double-pareto-density-plot
:tags: [hide-input]

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

from incerto.distributions import double_pareto
from incerto.figures import FIGURE_SIZES, set_theme, style_axes

set_theme()
x_grid = np.linspace(-8, 8, 500)

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=FIGURE_SIZES["single"])
for exponent in (0.8, 1.5, 3.0):
    ax.plot(
        x_grid,
        double_pareto.pdf(x_grid, exponent),
        label=fr"$\alpha={exponent}$",
    )

ax.set_xlabel("x")
ax.set_ylabel("density f(x)")
ax.set_title("Symmetric shifted double Pareto density")
ax.legend()
style_axes(ax)
plt.show()
```

**What to notice.** Smaller $\alpha$ keeps much more mass away from zero on
both sides.  The center remains symmetric, but the probability of a large gain
and the probability of a large loss decay at the same power rate.

## Derivation from signed Pareto magnitude

We derive the symmetric shifted construction used here from the signed
Pareto magnitude $X=B(Y-1)$.  The tail, density, and CDF formulas follow
directly from symmetry.  The moment thresholds use the same Pareto moment
logic as [Pareto Moment Existence](../theorems/pareto-moment-existence.md).

For $x\ge0$,

$$
\mathbb P(X>x)
=\mathbb P(B=1, Y-1>x)
=\frac12\mathbb P(Y>1+x)
=\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha}.
$$

The negative tail is identical by symmetry.  Differentiating the CDF on either
side gives the density.  The expectation is zero only when the first absolute
moment exists.  When $\alpha\le1$,
$\mathbb E[X^+]=\mathbb E[X^-]=\infty$, so the ordinary mean is undefined even
though the distribution is symmetric.  Since $X^2=(Y-1)^2$,

$$
\mathbb E[X^2]
=\mathbb E[Y^2]-2\mathbb E[Y]+1
=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-2}-2\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}+1
=\frac{2}{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)}
$$

for $\alpha>2$.  If $\alpha\le2$, the second moment of $Y$ is infinite, so the
variance of $X$ is infinite.

## Two-sided tail behavior and simulation check

The exact two-sided exceedance $\mathbb P(|X|>x)=(1+x)^{-\alpha}$ is a
regularly varying tail with the same exponent as the Pareto magnitude.  The
next plot compares this exact curve with an empirical exceedance curve from a
fixed-seed simulation.

```{code-cell} python
:label: double-pareto-tail-simulation
:tags: [hide-input]

alpha = 1.5
rng = np.random.default_rng(20260614)
sample = double_pareto.rvs(alpha, size=120_000, random_state=rng)
thresholds = np.geomspace(1, 80, 80)
empirical = np.array([np.mean(np.abs(sample) > threshold) for threshold in thresholds])
theoretical = (1 + thresholds) ** (-alpha)

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=FIGURE_SIZES["single"])
ax.loglog(thresholds, empirical, "o", markersize=3, label="simulation")
ax.loglog(thresholds, theoretical, label=r"exact $(1+x)^{-\alpha}$")
ax.set_xlabel("threshold x")
ax.set_ylabel(r"two-sided exceedance $\mathbb{P}(|X|>x)$")
ax.set_title("Double Pareto two-sided tail")
ax.legend()
style_axes(ax, grid_axis="both")
plt.show()
```

**What to notice.** The simulated exceedance curve follows the exact power-law
curve over the plotted range, with visible sampling noise in the farthest tail
where few observations remain.

The implementation is consolidated in `incerto.distributions` and vectorizes
the formulas above.

```{code-cell} python
:tags: [hide-input]
:label: double-pareto-python-check

import numpy as np
from incerto.distributions import double_pareto

alpha = 3.0
x = np.array([-4.0, 0.0, 4.0])
pdf = double_pareto.pdf(x, alpha)
cdf = double_pareto.cdf(x, alpha)
mean, variance = double_pareto.stats(alpha, moments="mv")

print(f"x grid: {x}")
print(f"PDF values: {np.round(pdf, 6)}")
print(f"CDF values: {np.round(cdf, 6)}")
print(f"Mean and variance: {(float(mean), float(variance))}")
```


## Caveats

- We describe the symmetric shifted construction in `incerto`, also
  readable as a symmetric Lomax law for $|X|$, not every distribution called
  "double Pareto" in the literature.
- For $\alpha\le1$, even the absolute first moment is infinite.  Symmetry can
  make a formal location or principal-value calculation look harmless while the
  ordinary expectation is undefined and absolute exposure remains uncontrolled.
- The distribution is a pedagogical model.  Empirical returns usually need
  skew, truncation, volatility clustering, dependence, or threshold modeling
  before a tail fit is credible.
