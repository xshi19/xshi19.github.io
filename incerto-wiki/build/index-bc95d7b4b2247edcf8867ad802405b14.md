# Incerto Wiki

Incerto Wiki is a concept-first reference for fat tails, extreme value theory,
and the mathematical ideas behind the Incerto framework.  The pages are meant
to work like cleaned-up lecture notes: definitions come with assumptions,
claims are supported by proofs, computations, or citations, and source texts
are organized through reading guides rather than copied into the wiki.

## Choose a route

The introductory route assumes basic calculus and introductory probability,
with no previous extreme value theory. Each route uses the same concept pages;
you can switch to the reference catalog whenever you need a definition.

### Understand fat tails

<!-- Use site-root slugs: MyST does not resolve Markdown filenames with queries. -->

1. Read [survival notation](notation/index.md) to distinguish the chance of
   exceeding a threshold from the density at that threshold.
2. Start with [Pareto](/pareto?entry=understand): see how changing the
   exponent changes the frequency of large observations.
3. Study [moment existence](/pareto-moment-existence?entry=understand) to
   learn when means and variances are finite.
4. Explore [running means](/lln-preasymptotic?entry=understand) to see why
   a finite mean can still be difficult to estimate.
5. Compare [max-to-sum ratios](/max-to-sum-ratio?entry=understand) to measure
   how much one observation contributes to a sample total.

Then [regular variation](/regular-variation?entry=understand) explains which
Pareto conclusions extend to tails that are only asymptotically power laws.

### Analyze a dataset

1. Start with the [NYC dog-name study](/nyc-dog-name-tail?entry=analyze),
   whose committed snapshot makes it available on the public site without a
   local cache. Define the variable, units, sample, and which side of the
   distribution is being studied.
2. Follow [body, shoulder, and tail diagnostics](/body-shoulder-tail?entry=analyze)
   to plot empirical survival and distinguish the body from possible tail behavior.
3. Read [threshold selection](/tail-threshold-selection?entry=analyze)
   before choosing which observations enter a tail fit.
4. Compare [Hill estimates](/hill-estimator?entry=analyze) with the
   [mean-excess diagnostic](/mean-excess-function?entry=analyze), checking
   their different assumptions and sensitivity to the threshold.
5. Return to the [NYC study's caveats](/nyc-dog-name-tail?entry=analyze#caveats)
   to assess dependence, pooled years, and discrete counts; report the threshold
   and limitations alongside any fitted exponent.

For an optional financial time-series example, use the
[S&P study](/sp500-tail?entry=analyze). Its data-dependent figures require
a local cache. Review its
[caveats](/sp500-tail?entry=analyze#interpretation-and-caveats) for dependence
and nonstationarity.

### Look up a mathematical result

Use site search or the [theorem index](concepts/theorem-concepts.md),
[distribution index](concepts/distribution-concepts.md), and
[method index](concepts/method-concepts.md). Check the assumptions beside the
statement, follow its proof or cited reference, then use the onward links for
special cases and applications. The [notation reference](notation/index.md)
provides the shared symbols.
