Chapter 4 - How Fat Tails Emerge From Random Scale

What This Chapter Is Doing¶

Chapter 4 of Taleb’s Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails moves from the taxonomy of Chapter 3 to mechanisms. The main point is that fat-tailed-looking behavior does not require an exact Pareto law from the start. Random scale, stochastic variance, and mixtures can make Gaussian habits fail in finite samples.

This guide routes the chapter into concept pages. It does not reproduce the chapter’s figures or notebook plots.

4.1 - Random Variance as a Fat-Tail Mechanism¶

The chapter starts with the simplest stochastic-volatility idea: observations can be conditionally Gaussian while their variance changes across states. A two-component normal mixture already shows the effect: the variance can be held fixed while fourth moments and shoulder/tail mass change.

  • Variance Gamma Distribution

4.3 - Body, Shoulders, and Tails¶

This section gives a geometric diagnostic for what scale randomization does to a density. In a symmetric location-scale family, scale mixing adds mass near the peak and far tails while removing mass from the shoulders. The boundary is not a Pareto threshold; it is a sign change in scale curvature.

Existing concept pages:

  • Body, Shoulders, and Tails

  • Normal Variance Mixture

Planned concept atoms:

  • Planned Tail Threshold Selection for empirical threshold selection, which is a separate problem from the scale-curvature diagnostic.

4.4 - Mean Deviation, Standard Deviation, and Rising Norms¶

The chapter then compares scale summaries. Standard deviation, mean absolute deviation, and median absolute deviation agree under a Gaussian calibration, but they respond differently to extremes. The disagreement is itself useful: it shows that the chosen norm can determine what the data appears to say.

  • LLN Failure Under Infinite Mean

  • Planned Rising Norms for the high-dimensional norm comparison.

  • Planned S&P 500 Dispersion Ratio for the rolling S&P 500 standard-deviation to mean-absolute-deviation ratio.
