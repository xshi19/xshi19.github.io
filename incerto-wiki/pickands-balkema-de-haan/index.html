Pickands-Balkema-de Haan Theorem

Statement¶

Let FFF be a distribution function with finite or infinite right endpoint xFx_FxF​. For a high threshold u<xFu<x_Fu<xF​, define the conditional excess distribution

Fu(y)=P(X−u≤y∣X>u)=F(u+y)−F(u)1−F(u),0≤y<xF−u.F_u(y)=\mathbb P(X-u\le y\mid X>u) =\frac{F(u+y)-F(u)}{1-F(u)}, \qquad 0\le y<x_F-u.Fu​(y)=P(X−u≤y∣X>u)=1−F(u)F(u+y)−F(u)​,0≤y<xF​−u.
(1)

If FFF is in the maximum domain of attraction of an extreme-value law with extreme-value index ξ\xiξ, then there is a positive scale function β(u)\beta(u)β(u) such that

sup⁡0≤y<xF−u∣Fu(y)−Gξ,β(u)(y)∣→0,u↑xF,\sup_{0\le y<x_F-u} \left|F_u(y)-G_{\xi,\beta(u)}(y)\right| \to 0, \qquad u\uparrow x_F,0≤y<xF​−usup​∣∣​Fu​(y)−Gξ,β(u)​(y)∣∣​→0,u↑xF​,
(2)

where Gξ,βG_{\xi,\beta}Gξ,β​ is the generalized Pareto distribution

Gξ,β(y)=1−(1+ξyβ)−1/ξ,ξ≠0,G_{\xi,\beta}(y) = 1-\left(1+\frac{\xi y}{\beta}\right)^{-1/\xi}, \qquad \xi\ne0,Gξ,β​(y)=1−(1+βξy​)−1/ξ,ξ=0,
(3)

on the support where 1+ξy/β>01+\xi y/\beta>01+ξy/β>0, and

G0,β(y)=1−e−y/βG_{0,\beta}(y)=1-e^{-y/\beta}G0,β​(y)=1−e−y/β
(4)

for ξ=0\xi=0ξ=0. The theorem is the asymptotic justification for the peaks-over-threshold model: sufficiently high threshold exceedances are modeled by a generalized Pareto distribution.

Intuition¶

Block-maxima theory says that properly normalized maxima have only a small number of possible limiting shapes. The Pickands-Balkema-de Haan theorem says the matching threshold view has the same discipline: once we condition on being far enough into the tail, the remaining excess has an approximately generalized Pareto shape.

For fat-tail work, the case ξ>0\xi>0ξ>0 is the main bridge. It corresponds to a regularly varying right tail with exponent α=1/ξ\alpha=1/\xiα=1/ξ. The theorem does not say every high observation is generated by a clean Pareto law. It says the excess distribution over moving high thresholds has a universal limit shape under the same domain-of-attraction assumptions used in extreme-value theory.

Proof¶

The full theorem is a classical result of Balkema and de Haan and, independently, Pickands. Its proof uses the equivalence between convergence of normalized maxima and convergence of normalized threshold excesses. This page records the statement and uses exact Pareto algebra as a checkable special case.

If XXX is Pareto Type I with lower cutoff xmx_mxm​ and tail exponent α\alphaα, then for u≥xmu\ge x_mu≥xm​,

P(X−u>y∣X>u)=P(X>u+y)P(X>u)=(1+yu)−α.\mathbb P(X-u>y\mid X>u) = \frac{\mathbb P(X>u+y)}{\mathbb P(X>u)} = \left(1+\frac{y}{u}\right)^{-\alpha}.P(X−u>y∣X>u)=P(X>u)P(X>u+y)​=(1+uy​)−α.
(5)

This is exactly a generalized Pareto survival function with

ξ=1α,β(u)=uα.\xi=\frac1\alpha, \qquad \beta(u)=\frac{u}{\alpha}.ξ=α1​,β(u)=αu​.
(6)

Thus exact Pareto tails do not merely approach the generalized Pareto form; they have it at every threshold above xmx_mxm​.

Python¶

The simulation below samples an exact Pareto tail and fits a generalized Pareto distribution to exceedances over several empirical thresholds. The fitted shape should fluctuate around ξ=1/α\xi=1/\alphaξ=1/α.

import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import genpareto

from incerto.distributions import pareto_type1

alpha = 1.7
xi_true = 1 / alpha
rng = np.random.default_rng(20260524)
sample = pareto_type1.rvs(alpha, size=120_000, random_state=rng)

for q in [0.90, 0.95, 0.98, 0.99]:
    u = np.quantile(sample, q)
    excesses = sample[sample > u] - u
    xi_hat, loc_hat, beta_hat = genpareto.fit(excesses, floc=0)
    print(
        f"threshold quantile={q:.2f}, exceedances={excesses.size:5d}, "
        f"xi_hat={xi_hat:.3f}, beta_hat/u={beta_hat / u:.3f}"
    )

print(f"true xi={xi_true:.3f}; exact Pareto beta(u)/u={1 / alpha:.3f}")
threshold quantile=0.90, exceedances=12000, xi_hat=0.586, beta_hat/u=0.585
threshold quantile=0.95, exceedances= 6000, xi_hat=0.592, beta_hat/u=0.584
threshold quantile=0.98, exceedances= 2400, xi_hat=0.592, beta_hat/u=0.581
threshold quantile=0.99, exceedances= 1200, xi_hat=0.600, beta_hat/u=0.566
true xi=0.588; exact Pareto beta(u)/u=0.588

The fitted scale ratio β^/u\widehat\beta/uβ​/u should also be near 1/α1/\alpha1/α for this exact Pareto example. Empirical data rarely behaves this cleanly.

Caveats¶

  • The theorem is asymptotic in the threshold. It does not choose the threshold for a finite dataset.

  • Dependence, seasonality, volatility clustering, rounding, truncation, and mixtures can all distort threshold exceedances.

  • A generalized Pareto fit is a tail model, not a proof that the full distribution is Pareto.

  • When ξ≥1\xi\ge1ξ≥1, the fitted generalized Pareto model has no finite mean. When ξ≥1/2\xi\ge1/2ξ≥1/2, it has no finite variance. These moment boundaries are often the practical reason the shape estimate matters.

  • A high threshold reduces bias but increases variance because fewer exceedances remain.
