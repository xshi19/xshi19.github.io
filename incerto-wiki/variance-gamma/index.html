Variance Gamma Distribution

Statement¶

In one convenient parameterization, let

α>∣β∣,λ>0,θ=2α2−β2.\alpha>|\beta|,\qquad \lambda>0,\qquad \theta=\frac{2}{\alpha^2-\beta^2}.α>∣β∣,λ>0,θ=α2−β22​.
(1)

Let G∼Gamma⁡(λ,θ)G\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(\lambda,\theta)G∼Gamma(λ,θ) use shape λ\lambdaλ and scale θ\thetaθ, and let Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim N(0,1)Z∼N(0,1) be independent. The univariate variance-gamma variable is

X=βG+G Z.X=\beta G+\sqrt{G}\,Z.X=βG+G​Z.
(2)

This is a normal variance-mean mixture: conditional on G=gG=gG=g, X∣G=g∼N(βg,g)X\mid G=g\sim N(\beta g,g)X∣G=g∼N(βg,g). When β=0\beta=0β=0, it reduces to a pure normal variance mixture.

For x≠0x\ne0x=0, its density is

f(x)=(α2−β2)λ∣x∣λ−1/2Kλ−1/2(α∣x∣)π Γ(λ)(2α)λ−1/2eβx,f(x) = \frac{(\alpha^2-\beta^2)^\lambda |x|^{\lambda-1/2} K_{\lambda-1/2}(\alpha |x|)} {\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\lambda)(2\alpha)^{\lambda-1/2}} e^{\beta x},f(x)=π​Γ(λ)(2α)λ−1/2(α2−β2)λ∣x∣λ−1/2Kλ−1/2​(α∣x∣)​eβx,
(3)

where KνK_\nuKν​ is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. Its mean and variance are

E[X]=βλθ,Var⁡(X)=λθ(1+β2θ).\mathbb E[X]=\beta\lambda\theta,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X)=\lambda\theta(1+\beta^2\theta).E[X]=βλθ,Var(X)=λθ(1+β2θ).
(4)

Its moment generating function is

MX(t)=(1−θβt−θt22)−λ,−α−β<t<α−β.M_X(t) = \left(1-\theta\beta t-\frac{\theta t^2}{2}\right)^{-\lambda}, \qquad -\alpha-\beta<t<\alpha-\beta.MX​(t)=(1−θβt−2θt2​)−λ,−α−β<t<α−β.
(5)

When β=0\beta=0β=0, the distribution is symmetric. Its tails decay exponentially, not as a power law.

Shape intuition¶

Variance-gamma is a continuous normal variance-mean mixture. The observation is normal after conditioning on a random gamma clock, but the random clock changes both conditional variance and, when β≠0\beta\ne0β=0, conditional mean. The parameter λ\lambdaλ controls the gamma clock’s shape, α\alphaα controls tail decay, and β\betaβ tilts the distribution to the right or left.

Relative to its own moment-matched Gaussian, a typical variance-gamma parameter choice has a sharper center and heavier far tails. It is still not the same object as a Pareto tail. The model is “semi-heavy”: heavier than Gaussian in many finite-sample diagnostics, yet all ordinary moments remain finite under this parameterization.

Gamma-normal mixture derivation¶

We derive the moment generating function, moment formulas, and exponential tail rates from the gamma-normal mixture representation. The Bessel density uses the displayed standard integral identity, and the origin behavior is cited as a standard consequence of Bessel-function asymptotics.

Condition on GGG. Since X∣G=gX\mid G=gX∣G=g is normal with mean βg\beta gβg and variance ggg,

E[etX∣G]=exp⁡((βt+t2/2)G).\mathbb E[e^{tX}\mid G] = \exp\left((\beta t+t^2/2)G\right).E[etX∣G]=exp((βt+t2/2)G).
(6)

The moment generating function of a gamma variable with shape λ\lambdaλ and scale θ\thetaθ is (1−θs)−λ(1-\theta s)^{-\lambda}(1−θs)−λ where it is finite. Therefore

E[etX]=(1−θβt−θt22)−λ.\mathbb E[e^{tX}] = \left(1-\theta\beta t-\frac{\theta t^2}{2}\right)^{-\lambda}.E[etX]=(1−θβt−2θt2​)−λ.
(7)

Because θ=2/(α2−β2)\theta=2/(\alpha^2-\beta^2)θ=2/(α2−β2), this MGF is finite exactly on

−α−β<t<α−β.-\alpha-\beta<t<\alpha-\beta.−α−β<t<α−β.
(8)

Differentiating at t=0t=0t=0 gives

E[X]=βλθ.\mathbb E[X]=\beta\lambda\theta.E[X]=βλθ.
(9)

The variance can also be obtained from conditional variance:

Var⁡(X)=E[Var⁡(X∣G)]+Var⁡(E[X∣G])=E[G]+Var⁡(βG)=λθ+β2λθ2.\operatorname{Var}(X) = \mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(X\mid G)] + \operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[X\mid G]) = \mathbb E[G]+\operatorname{Var}(\beta G) = \lambda\theta+\beta^2\lambda\theta^2.Var(X)=E[Var(X∣G)]+Var(E[X∣G])=E[G]+Var(βG)=λθ+β2λθ2.
(10)

The Bessel density follows by integrating the conditional normal density over the gamma mixing density and using

∫0∞gν−1e−a/g−bg dg=2(ab)ν/2Kν(2ab),\int_0^\infty g^{\nu-1}e^{-a/g-bg}\,dg = 2\left(\frac ab\right)^{\nu/2} K_\nu(2\sqrt{ab}),∫0∞​gν−1e−a/g−bgdg=2(ba​)ν/2Kν​(2ab​),
(11)

with

ν=λ−12,a=x22,b=α22.\nu=\lambda-\frac12,\qquad a=\frac{x^2}{2},\qquad b=\frac{\alpha^2}{2}.ν=λ−21​,a=2x2​,b=2α2​.
(12)

The special case β=0,λ=1\beta=0,\lambda=1β=0,λ=1 gives the Laplace density (α/2)e−α∣x∣(\alpha/2)e^{-\alpha|x|}(α/2)e−α∣x∣.

For tail behavior, use

Kν(z)∼π2ze−zK_\nu(z)\sim \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}e^{-z}Kν​(z)∼2zπ​​e−z
(13)

as z→∞z\to\inftyz→∞. Hence

f(x)∼C+xλ−1e−(α−β)x,x→∞,f(x)\sim C_+x^{\lambda-1}e^{-(\alpha-\beta)x}, \qquad x\to\infty,f(x)∼C+​xλ−1e−(α−β)x,x→∞,
(14)

and

f(−x)∼C−xλ−1e−(α+β)x,x→∞,f(-x)\sim C_-x^{\lambda-1}e^{-(\alpha+\beta)x}, \qquad x\to\infty,f(−x)∼C−​xλ−1e−(α+β)x,x→∞,
(15)

for positive constants C+C_+C+​ and C−C_-C−​. The right exponential rate is α−β\alpha-\betaα−β, and the left exponential rate is α+β\alpha+\betaα+β. This proves the distribution is not regularly varying.

At the origin, the finite-density case is

λ>12:f(0)=(α2−β2)λΓ(λ−12)2π Γ(λ)α2λ−1.\lambda>\frac12: \quad f(0)= \frac{(\alpha^2-\beta^2)^\lambda \Gamma(\lambda-\tfrac12)} {2\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(\lambda)\alpha^{2\lambda-1}}.λ>21​:f(0)=2π​Γ(λ)α2λ−1(α2−β2)λΓ(λ−21​)​.
(16)

When λ=1/2\lambda=1/2λ=1/2, the density has a logarithmic singularity at zero. When 0<λ<1/20<\lambda<1/20<λ<1/2, it has an integrable power singularity.

Tail comparison and simulation check¶

The implementation lives in incerto.distributions.univariate_variance_gamma.

Source
import numpy as np

from incerto.distributions import univariate_variance_gamma

alpha = 1.2
beta = -0.5
lam = 2.0

mean, variance = univariate_variance_gamma.stats(
    alpha, beta, lam, moments="mv"
)

rng = np.random.default_rng(20260523)
sample_size = 200_000
sample = univariate_variance_gamma.rvs(
    alpha, beta, lam, size=sample_size, random_state=rng
)
density_points = np.array([-3.0, 0.0, 3.0])
density_values = univariate_variance_gamma.pdf(
    density_points, alpha, beta, lam
)
sample_mean = float(np.mean(sample))
sample_variance = float(np.var(sample, ddof=1))
mean_se = float(np.std(sample, ddof=1) / np.sqrt(sample_size))
variance_mc_variance = (
    np.mean((sample - sample_mean) ** 4)
    - ((sample_size - 1) / sample_size * sample_variance) ** 2
)
variance_se = float(np.sqrt(max(variance_mc_variance, 0.0)) / np.sqrt(sample_size))

tail_x = np.linspace(8.0, 18.0, 80)
right_log_density = np.log(
    univariate_variance_gamma.pdf(tail_x, alpha, beta, lam)
)
left_log_density = np.log(
    univariate_variance_gamma.pdf(-tail_x, alpha, beta, lam)
)
right_slope = np.polyfit(tail_x[-30:], right_log_density[-30:], 1)[0]
left_slope = np.polyfit(tail_x[-30:], left_log_density[-30:], 1)[0]

print(
    f"Theoretical mean and variance: {float(mean):.4f}, "
    f"{float(variance):.4f}"
)
print(
    f"Simulated mean: {sample_mean:.4f} +/- {1.96 * mean_se:.4f} "
    "(approx. 95% MC)"
)
print(
    f"Simulated variance: {sample_variance:.4f} +/- {1.96 * variance_se:.4f} "
    "(approx. 95% MC)"
)
print(f"PDF at x={density_points.tolist()}: {np.round(density_values, 6)}")
print(
    "Log-density tail slopes, right/left: "
    f"{right_slope:.3f}, {left_slope:.3f}; "
    f"targets {- (alpha - beta):.3f}, {- (alpha + beta):.3f}"
)

Simulated mean: -1.6898 +/- 0.0096 (approx. 95% MC)
Simulated variance: 4.7798 +/- 0.0427 (approx. 95% MC)

The simulated mean and variance should be close to the theoretical values within Monte Carlo error. The asymmetric density values show the effect of β<0\beta<0β<0, and the log-density slopes approach the right and left exponential tail rates.

Caveats¶

  • Parameterizations vary across books and libraries. We use the parameterization implemented in incerto.distributions.

  • The Bessel density needs a limiting value at x=0x=0x=0 when λ>1/2\lambda>1/2λ>1/2; at and below λ=1/2\lambda=1/2λ=1/2, the density is singular but still integrable under the stated parameter restrictions.

  • Variance-gamma can have a sharper center and heavier far tails than its moment-matched Gaussian, but it is not a Pareto-type model. Hill estimates and moment-existence claims designed for regularly varying tails do not apply directly.

  • In financial modeling, variance-gamma is often used as a process with independent increments. We state only the one-step distribution.

  • Dependence, volatility clustering, and time aggregation are separate modeling assumptions.
