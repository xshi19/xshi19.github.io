Generalized Pareto Distribution

Statement¶

The generalized Pareto distribution is the standard limit family for threshold excesses. With location zero, an excess variable Y≥0Y\ge0Y≥0 has shape ξ\xiξ and scale β>0\beta>0β>0 when

Gξ,β(y)=1−(1+ξyβ)−1/ξ,ξ≠0,G_{\xi,\beta}(y) = 1-\left(1+\frac{\xi y}{\beta}\right)^{-1/\xi}, \qquad \xi\ne0,Gξ,β​(y)=1−(1+βξy​)−1/ξ,ξ=0,
(1)

for y≥0y\ge0y≥0 with 1+ξy/β>01+\xi y/\beta>01+ξy/β>0. For y<0y<0y<0 the CDF is zero; for ξ<0\xi<0ξ<0 it is one at and above −β/ξ-\beta/\xi−β/ξ, by continuity. The boundary case is

G0,β(y)=1−e−y/β.G_{0,\beta}(y)=1-e^{-y/\beta}.G0,β​(y)=1−e−y/β.
(2)

For ξ≥0\xi\ge0ξ≥0, the support is y≥0y\ge0y≥0. For ξ<0\xi<0ξ<0, the support is 0≤y≤−β/ξ0\le y\le -\beta/\xi0≤y≤−β/ξ, so the tail has a finite endpoint. The survival function for ξ≠0\xi\ne0ξ=0 is

Gˉξ,β(y)=(1+ξyβ)−1/ξ.\bar G_{\xi,\beta}(y) = \left(1+\frac{\xi y}{\beta}\right)^{-1/\xi}.Gˉξ,β​(y)=(1+βξy​)−1/ξ.
(3)

The mean exists only for ξ<1\xi<1ξ<1 and equals β/(1−ξ)\beta/(1-\xi)β/(1−ξ). The variance exists only for ξ<1/2\xi<1/2ξ<1/2 and equals

β2(1−ξ)2(1−2ξ).\frac{\beta^2}{(1-\xi)^2(1-2\xi)}.(1−ξ)2(1−2ξ)β2​.
(4)

Shape and endpoint intuition¶

The generalized Pareto distribution is the peaks-over-threshold counterpart of the generalized extreme-value distribution for block maxima. Once a threshold is high enough, the distribution of the excess over that threshold is modeled with one shape parameter ξ\xiξ and one scale parameter β\betaβ.

The sign and size of ξ\xiξ carry the tail story. Positive ξ\xiξ gives a Pareto-type heavy tail. Zero gives the exponential boundary. Negative ξ\xiξ gives a finite endpoint. In the fat-tail examples, the most important boundary values are ξ=1/2\xi=1/2ξ=1/2 for variance and ξ=1\xi=1ξ=1 for the mean.

For β=1\beta=1β=1, the three examples from the survival formula are:

What to notice. Positive ξ\xiξ gives the slowest-decaying curve, ξ=0\xi=0ξ=0 is the exponential boundary, and negative ξ\xiξ ends at the finite endpoint −β/ξ-\beta/\xi−β/ξ.

Threshold-excess stability and Pareto special case¶

A GPD is stable under raising the threshold. If YYY has parameters ξ,β\xi,\betaξ,β and u≥0u\ge0u≥0 satisfies β+ξu>0\beta+\xi u>0β+ξu>0, then, for admissible y≥0y\ge0y≥0 and ξ≠0\xi\ne0ξ=0,

Gˉξ,β(u+y)Gˉξ,β(u)=(1+ξyβ+ξu)−1/ξ.\frac{\bar G_{\xi,\beta}(u+y)}{\bar G_{\xi,\beta}(u)} =\left(1+\frac{\xi y}{\beta+\xi u}\right)^{-1/\xi}.Gˉξ,β​(u)Gˉξ,β​(u+y)​=(1+β+ξuξy​)−1/ξ.
(5)

For ξ=0\xi=0ξ=0, the ratio is e−y/βe^{-y/\beta}e−y/β. Thus excesses have the same shape and updated scale β+ξu\beta+\xi uβ+ξu. Combining this with the GPD mean gives the mean-excess formula.

The Pareto Type I distribution is an exact generalized Pareto excess model. If XXX has Pareto tail exponent α\alphaα and threshold u≥xmu\ge x_mu≥xm​, then

P(X−u>y∣X>u)=(1+yu)−α.\mathbb P(X-u>y\mid X>u) = \left(1+\frac{y}{u}\right)^{-\alpha}.P(X−u>y∣X>u)=(1+uy​)−α.
(6)

This is the generalized Pareto survival function with

ξ=1α,β(u)=uα.\xi=\frac1\alpha, \qquad \beta(u)=\frac{u}{\alpha}.ξ=α1​,β(u)=αu​.
(7)

The Pickands-Balkema-de Haan theorem (a dedicated page is planned) explains why the same family appears asymptotically for a much larger class of threshold exceedances.

Formula check¶

A concrete choice of shape and scale illustrates both the survival and moments.

(8)

Thus Gˉ(0)=1\bar G(0)=1Gˉ(0)=1, Gˉ(1)=1.2−2.5\bar G(1)=1.2^{-2.5}Gˉ(1)=1.2−2.5, Gˉ(5)=2−2.5\bar G(5)=2^{-2.5}Gˉ(5)=2−2.5, and Gˉ(10)=3−2.5\bar G(10)=3^{-2.5}Gˉ(10)=3−2.5. These are formula evaluations, with no numerical fitting or simulation.

Caveats¶

  • A GPD fit is a tail model for exceedances, not a model for the full distribution body.

  • The threshold uuu is not chosen by the theorem. Shape and scale estimates should be inspected across a range of thresholds.

  • When ξ≥1\xi\ge1ξ≥1, the fitted excess distribution has no finite mean. When ξ≥1/2\xi\ge1/2ξ≥1/2, it has no finite variance.

  • Dependence, truncation, censoring, seasonality, and mixture effects can distort threshold-excess fits.
