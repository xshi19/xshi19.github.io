Karamata's Theorem

Statement¶

Let LLL be eventually positive, measurable, locally bounded, and slowly varying at infinity. If ρ>−1\rho>-1ρ>−1, then

∫axtρL(t) dt∼xρ+1L(x)ρ+1,x→∞.\int_a^x t^\rho L(t)\,dt \sim \frac{x^{\rho+1}L(x)}{\rho+1}, \qquad x\to\infty.∫ax​tρL(t)dt∼ρ+1xρ+1L(x)​,x→∞.
(1)

If ρ<−1\rho<-1ρ<−1, then

∫x∞tρL(t) dt∼xρ+1L(x)−ρ−1,x→∞.\int_x^\infty t^\rho L(t)\,dt \sim \frac{x^{\rho+1}L(x)}{-\rho-1}, \qquad x\to\infty.∫x∞​tρL(t)dt∼−ρ−1xρ+1L(x)​,x→∞.
(2)

Here a>0a>0a>0 is fixed, and A(x)∼B(x)A(x)\sim B(x)A(x)∼B(x) means A(x)/B(x)→1A(x)/B(x)\to1A(x)/B(x)→1.

Thus a Pareto-type tail has finite pppth moment for p<αp<\alphap<α and infinite pppth moment for p>αp>\alphap>α. The boundary case p=αp=\alphap=α is not decided by this theorem alone; it depends on the slowly varying factor LLL.

Here Fˉ(x)=P(X>x)\bar F(x)=\mathbb P(X>x)Fˉ(x)=P(X>x), α\alphaα is the positive tail exponent, and ppp is the moment order. The integration parameters aaa and ρ\rhoρ belong to the integral theorem.

Intuition¶

Karamata’s theorem says that, for regularly varying functions, integrals are asymptotically governed by the endpoint where the mass accumulates. When ρ>−1\rho>-1ρ>−1, the integral up to xxx is controlled by the upper endpoint. When ρ<−1\rho<-1ρ<−1, the remaining tail integral beyond xxx is controlled by the lower endpoint.

This is the bridge between tail shape and moment existence. Once the survival tail behaves like x−αL(x)x^{-\alpha}L(x)x−αL(x), multiplying by xpx^pxp tests whether the pppth moment is still dominated by ordinary observations or by the far tail.

Examples¶

  • For an exact Pareto tail, LLL is constant, so Karamata reduces the moment test to integrating a pure power.

  • For L(x)=log⁡xL(x)=\log xL(x)=logx, the same power boundary applies, but the endpoint asymptotic gains the slowly varying logarithmic factor.

  • The boundary case p=αp=\alphap=α needs separate analysis. Exact Pareto tails diverge logarithmically, as shown in Pareto Moment Existence. More generally,

    (5)

    when Fˉ(t)=t−αL(t)\bar F(t)=t^{-\alpha}L(t)Fˉ(t)=t−αL(t) in the tail. Thus L(t)=1L(t)=1L(t)=1 gives logarithmic divergence, L(t)=1/log⁡tL(t)=1/\log tL(t)=1/logt gives log⁡log⁡t\log\log tloglogt divergence, and L(t)=1/(log⁡t)2L(t)=1/(\log t)^2L(t)=1/(logt)2 gives a finite boundary moment.

Deriving the moment consequences¶

We use Karamata’s integral asymptotics as a cited theorem, then derive the two moment consequences by integration by parts. The boundary case p=αp=\alphap=α is not decided by Karamata alone; it depends on the slowly varying factor and is handled through examples and caveats.

The integral asymptotics are the classical form of Karamata’s theorem for regularly varying functions. The full proof uses uniform convergence of slowly varying functions on compact multiplier intervals and a split of the integral into a near-endpoint part and a negligible remainder; this page cites the standard theorem rather than reproducing that argument here.

The moment consequences follow from integration by parts. For 0<p<α0<p<\alpha0<p<α,

(6)

∫x∞tp−1Fˉ(t) dt∼xp−αL(x)α−p=xpFˉ(x)α−p.\int_x^\infty t^{p-1}\bar F(t)\,dt \sim \frac{x^{p-\alpha}L(x)}{\alpha-p} = \frac{x^p\bar F(x)}{\alpha-p}.∫x∞​tp−1Fˉ(t)dt∼α−pxp−αL(x)​=α−pxpFˉ(x)​.

Moment-test calculation¶

For a slowly varying example take L(t)=log⁡tL(t)=\log tL(t)=logt on t>1t>1t>1 and ρ>−1\rho>-1ρ>−1. Integration by parts gives the exact identity

∫1xtρlog⁡t dt=xρ+1log⁡xρ+1−xρ+1−1(ρ+1)2.\int_1^x t^\rho\log t\,dt =\frac{x^{\rho+1}\log x}{\rho+1} -\frac{x^{\rho+1}-1}{(\rho+1)^2}.∫1x​tρlogtdt=ρ+1xρ+1logx​−(ρ+1)2xρ+1−1​.

Caveats¶

  • The assumptions on LLL matter. Slow variation is an asymptotic regularity condition, not a finite-sample diagnostic.

  • Karamata does not settle the boundary moment p=αp=\alphap=α. Exact Pareto tails diverge logarithmically there, while other slowly varying factors can change the boundary behavior.

  • Moment existence is a tail statement. Estimating a moment from data also depends on sample size, dependence, threshold choice, and whether the fitted tail model is credible.

  • We treat nonnegative right tails. Two-sided models need the same reasoning applied to ∣X∣|X|∣X∣ or to each side separately.
