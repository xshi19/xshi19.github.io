Max-to-Sum Ratio

Statement¶

For realized nonnegative observations x1,…,xnx_1,\dots,x_nx1​,…,xn​ with sn>0s_n>0sn​>0, define

sn=∑i=1nxi,mn=max⁡1≤i≤nxi,rn=mnsn.s_n=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i,\qquad m_n=\max_{1\le i\le n}x_i,\qquad r_n=\frac{m_n}{s_n}.sn​=i=1∑n​xi​,mn​=1≤i≤nmax​xi​,rn​=sn​mn​​.
(1)

Its random-sample counterpart is Rn=Mn/SnR_n=M_n/S_nRn​=Mn​/Sn​, where Sn=∑i=1nXiS_n=\sum_{i=1}^n X_iSn​=∑i=1n​Xi​ and Mn=max⁡1≤i≤nXiM_n=\max_{1\le i\le n}X_iMn​=max1≤i≤n​Xi​. When X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\dotsX1​,X2​,… are iid nonnegative variables with finite positive mean μ=E[X]\mu=\mathbb E[X]μ=E[X], then

Rn→0almost surely.R_n\to0\qquad\text{almost surely}.Rn​→0almost surely.

Thus a persistent large realized value rnr_nrn​ is a warning sign that the sample sum is being governed by extremes rather than by aggregation. For Pareto Type I samples with α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1, the ordinary mean is infinite, so the finite-mean argument below does not apply.

Lowercase symbols describe the observed list; uppercase symbols describe random samples. For a nonnegative list with positive total, 1/n≤rn≤11/n\le r_n\le11/n≤rn​≤1. If the total is zero, the ratio is undefined.

Extreme-dominance intuition¶

In a thin-tailed or comfortable finite-mean sample, the largest observation may be memorable, but it should eventually become a negligible fraction of the whole. The total grows like nμn\munμ, while the largest draw grows more slowly than nnn on the scale needed to dominate the sum.

Fat-tailed samples can look different. One observation can represent a large fraction of all observed mass, especially near or below the Pareto mean boundary α=1\alpha=1α=1. This is the same mechanism behind LLN Failure Under Infinite Mean (planned), but RnR_nRn​ makes the dominance visible without plotting the entire running average. It complements the path view in Pre-Asymptotic LLN Behavior (planned).

Finite-mean proof¶

We prove the finite-mean theorem: if the iid nonnegative observations have a positive finite mean, then the maximum becomes a negligible share of the sum. The proof uses the strong law and Borel--Cantelli. The infinite-mean Pareto regimes are cited or described heuristically, because their exact limits need heavier regular-variation machinery.

For a Pareto Type I tail, Pareto Moment Existence shows that the mean exists exactly when α>1\alpha>1α>1. The regimes are qualitatively different:

  • α>1\alpha>1α>1: Mn/Sn→0M_n/S_n\to0Mn​/Sn​→0 almost surely by the finite-mean theorem.

  • Exact Pareto α=1\alpha=1α=1: Mn/Sn→0M_n/S_n\to0Mn​/Sn​→0 in probability, but slowly; Mn/(xmn)M_n/(x_m n)Mn​/(xm​n) is bounded in probability, while Sn/(xmnlog⁡n)→1S_n/(x_m n\log n)\to1Sn​/(xm​nlogn)→1 in probability (a cited limit).

  • 0<α<10<\alpha<10<α<1: under standard regular-variation assumptions, Mn/SnM_n/S_nMn​/Sn​ has a nondegenerate limiting distribution. Infinite mean does not imply that one observation asymptotically equals the whole sum; several of the largest observations may keep material shares.

For an exact Pareto law with α>1\alpha>1α>1, the Frechet maximum scale is xmn1/αx_m n^{1/\alpha}xm​n1/α, whereas the sum is asymptotic to nμn\munμ. Thus a typical ratio has scale n1/α−1n^{1/\alpha-1}n1/α−1: its exponent is −3/13-3/13−3/13 at α=1.3\alpha=1.3α=1.3 and −2/3-2/3−2/3 at α=3\alpha=3α=3. This scale comparison supplies no universal finite-nnn quantile or confidence bound. The infinite-mean limits cited above require separate asymptotic results; no Monte Carlo quantiles are reported here.

Caveats¶

  • RnR_nRn​ is a dominance diagnostic, not an estimator of the tail exponent.

  • A small RnR_nRn​ does not prove thin tails. A sample may simply not have seen a record-sized observation yet.

  • A large RnR_nRn​ can also come from data errors, censoring, mixtures, dependence, or nonstationarity. Inspect the observation before interpreting the ratio as a tail fact.

  • RnR_nRn​ has no universal “large” threshold. Compare an observed value with a fitted-model or bootstrap reference distribution.

  • For signed returns, Mn/SnM_n/S_nMn​/Sn​ may be unstable or meaningless because of cancellation. Apply it to nonnegative losses, severities, exposures, or possibly ∣Xi∣|X_i|∣Xi​∣, explicitly changing the interpretation.

  • A running max-to-sum curve is order-dependent, while the final RnR_nRn​ is not.

  • For α<1\alpha<1α<1, exact limiting behavior of Mn/SnM_n/S_nMn​/Sn​ belongs to stable and Poisson point process asymptotics. Here those limits are cited from Resnick (2007); only the finite-mean statement is proved.
