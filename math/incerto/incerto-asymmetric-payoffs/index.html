Asymmetric payoffs and exposure

A forecast of an event and the payoff attached to that event answer different questions. Let XXX be a real random variable and let g(X)g(X)g(X) be its payoff. Probability notation follows the shared notation. An ordinary finite expected payoff requires E∣g(X)∣<∞\mathbb E|g(X)|<\inftyE∣g(X)∣<∞.

The frequency of gains does not determine their mean¶

The two-outcome calculation separates gain frequency from expected payoff.

Proposition 1 (Mean of a two-outcome payoff)

Let a,b>0a,b>0a,b>0 and q∈[0,1]q\in[0,1]q∈[0,1]. If a payoff equals aaa with probability 1−q1-q1−q and −b-b−b with probability qqq, its gain probability is 1−q1-q1−q and its mean is (1−q)a−qb(1-q)a-qb(1−q)a−qb.

Proof 1

Only the outcome aaa is positive. Summing each payoff times its probability gives (1−q)a+q(−b)=(1−q)a−qb(1-q)a+q(-b)=(1-q)a-qb(1−q)a+q(−b)=(1−q)a−qb.

This is a one-period expectation calculation. A repeated strategy with a finite resource constraint needs a model of ruin as well.

Curvature and Jensen’s inequality¶

We prove the differentiable, integrable case of Jensen’s inequality.

Suppose XXX takes values in an open interval III, its mean μ=EX\mu=\mathbb E Xμ=EX belongs to III, and E∣X∣<∞\mathbb E|X|<\inftyE∣X∣<∞. If g:I→Rg:I\to\mathbb Rg:I→R is differentiable and convex, and E∣g(X)∣<∞\mathbb E|g(X)|<\inftyE∣g(X)∣<∞, then

Eg(X)≥g(EX).\mathbb E g(X)\ge g(\mathbb E X).Eg(X)≥g(EX).
(1)

Let XXX assign probability 1/21/21/2 to each of ±1\pm1±1, and let YYY assign probability 3/43/43/4 to zero and 1/81/81/8 to each of ±2\pm2±2. Both have mean zero and variance one, but

A transformation changes the tail and its moment requirements¶

We obtain the power-payoff law by inverting its exceedance event.

Convexity also does not supply a lower bound on the payoff.

Proposition 6 (A convex payoff with unbounded downside)

The function g:R→Rg:\mathbb R\to\mathbb Rg:R→R given by g(x)=ex+xg(x)=e^x+xg(x)=ex+x is strictly convex and satisfies lim⁡x→−∞g(x)=−∞\lim_{x\to-\infty}g(x)=-\inftylimx→−∞​g(x)=−∞. Consequently, convexity alone does not imply a finite lower payoff bound.

print(f"B: {finite_payoff_mean(probabilities, [-1, 80]):.4g}")
print(f"Convex gap: {finite_payoff_mean([0.5, 0.5], [2**2, 6**2]) - 4**2:.4g}")
