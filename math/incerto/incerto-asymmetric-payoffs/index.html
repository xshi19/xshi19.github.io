Asymmetric payoffs and exposure

A forecast of an event and the payoff attached to that event answer different questions. Let XXX be a real random variable with CDF FFF and let g(X)g(X)g(X) be its payoff. We use ggg for the transformation, reserving FFF for the CDF as in the shared notation. An ordinary finite expected payoff requires E∣g(X)∣<∞\mathbb E|g(X)|<\inftyE∣g(X)∣<∞.

Worked calculation and sources¶

Use finite_payoff_mean.

from xmath.incerto.lecture_risk import finite_payoff_mean

probabilities = [0.98, 0.02]
print(f"A: {finite_payoff_mean(probabilities, [1, -80]):.4g}")
print(f"B: {finite_payoff_mean(probabilities, [-1, 80]):.4g}")
print(f"Convex gap: {finite_payoff_mean([0.5, 0.5], [2**2, 6**2]) - 4**2:.4g}")

Output

A: -0.62
B: 0.62
Convex gap: 4

A’s higher gain frequency does not imply a higher expected payoff.

The source context is Taleb (2025), ch. 3, §3.4.1, pp. 41–43, and §3.10, pp. 60–63. The chapter motivates distinguishing state from exposure. The finite-table calculation, differentiable Jensen argument, power-tail transformation and capped-mean calculation are proved here.
