Cauchy running statistics

A location without a mean¶

Let X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\ldotsX1​,X2​,… be iid Cauchy variables with location μ\muμ and scale σ>0\sigma>0σ>0. Their density and characteristic function are

f(x)=σπ((x−μ)2+σ2),φ(t)=exp⁡(iμt−σ∣t∣).\begin{gathered} f(x)=\frac{\sigma}{\pi((x-\mu)^2+\sigma^2)},\\ \varphi(t)=\exp(i\mu t-\sigma|t|). \end{gathered}f(x)=π((x−μ)2+σ2)σ​,φ(t)=exp(iμt−σ∣t∣).​
(1)

The characteristic function is a cited identity (Durrett (2019), §3.3.1, Example 3.3.16, p. 131). The positive and negative parts both have infinite expectation: integrating ∣x∣f(x)|x|f(x)∣x∣f(x) on either tail gives logarithmic divergence. Thus μ\muμ is a location and median, not an expectation. See the stable-law convention for the shared scale parameterization.

The average has exactly the same law¶

We prove this finite-sample identity from the stated characteristic function. Independence gives, for every integer n≥1n\ge1n≥1,

EeitXˉn=φ(t/n)n=exp⁡(iμt−σ∣t∣).\begin{aligned} \mathbb E e^{it\bar X_n}&=\varphi(t/n)^n\\ &=\exp(i\mu t-\sigma|t|). \end{aligned}EeitXˉn​​=φ(t/n)n=exp(iμt−σ∣t∣).​
(2)

Uniqueness of characteristic functions therefore gives Xˉn=dX1\bar X_n\overset d=X_1Xˉn​=dX1​. In particular,

P(∣Xˉn−μ∣>ε)=1−2πarctan⁡(ε/σ)>0(ε>0).\begin{gathered} P(|\bar X_n-\mu|>\varepsilon)\\ =1-\frac2\pi\arctan(\varepsilon/\sigma)>0\\ (\varepsilon>0). \end{gathered}P(∣Xˉn​−μ∣>ε)=1−π2​arctan(ε/σ)>0(ε>0).​
(3)

The average does not converge in probability to μ\muμ, or to any finite constant. This argument alone does not classify every possible pathwise limit. It also does not say that successive running averages are independent: they share observations.

The population quantile is Q(p)=μ+σtan⁡(π(p−1/2))Q(p)=\mu+\sigma\tan(\pi(p-1/2))Q(p)=μ+σtan(π(p−1/2)), 0<p<10<p<10<p<1. Consequently the interquartile range of every Xˉn\bar X_nXˉn​ is 2σ2\sigma2σ. By contrast, sample medians consistently estimate μ\muμ. For fixed ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0, the strong law applied to the indicators 1{Xi≤μ−ε}\mathbf1_{\{X_i\le\mu-\varepsilon\}}1{Xi​≤μ−ε}​ and 1{Xi≤μ+ε}\mathbf1_{\{X_i\le\mu+\varepsilon\}}1{Xi​≤μ+ε}​ eventually puts fewer than half the observations at or below the lower endpoint and more than half at or below the upper endpoint. Every sample median is then inside that interval. Intersect over rational positive ε\varepsilonε to obtain almost-sure convergence. This is a proof about the median, not a restoration of the missing mean.

Paths and repeated samples¶

Use running_mean for one path and repeated_centered_sums for independent repetitions. Here centering at zero names the Cauchy location; it does not assert a population mean.

import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import cauchy
from xmath.incerto.limits import repeated_centered_sums

n = np.array([10, 100, 1000])
sums = repeated_centered_sums(cauchy(), n, repetitions=400, random_state=71)
for size, row in zip(n, sums / n[:, None]):
    q = np.quantile(row, [.1, .5, .9])
    print(f"n={size}: " + ", ".join(f"{v:.4g}" for v in q))

Output

n=10: -2.694, 0.01121, 2.404
n=100: -3.183, -0.05087, 3.134
n=1000: -3.107, 0.04288, 2.812

Higher moments and ergodicity¶

For p>0p>0p>0, direct tail integration gives E∣X∣p<∞\mathbb E|X|^p<\inftyE∣X∣p<∞ exactly when p<1p<1p<1. At p≥1p\ge1p≥1, the nonnegative infinite-mean theorem applies to ∣Xi∣p|X_i|^p∣Xi​∣p: their sample average tends to +∞+\infty+∞ almost surely. It does not apply to the signed Cauchy observations themselves. A principal-value cancellation is not a Lebesgue expectation.

An iid Cauchy sequence is ergodic under the shift. The integrability hypothesis of the ergodic averaging theorem fails for the identity observable. Calling the process nonergodic because its sample mean fails would confuse two different conditions.
