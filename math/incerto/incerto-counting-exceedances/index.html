Counting exceedances

A finite-sample quantity¶

For finite real observations x1,…,xnx_1,\ldots,x_nx1​,…,xn​ with n≥1n\geq 1n≥1, define the fraction strictly above a finite threshold uuu by

S^n(u)=1n∑i=1n1{xi>u}.\widehat S_n(u)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf{1}\{x_i>u\}.Sn​(u)=n1​i=1∑n​1{xi​>u}.
(1)

Here uuu is the threshold in the shared notation. S^n\widehat S_nSn​ describes the observations; Fˉ(u)=P(X>u)\bar F(u)=P(X>u)Fˉ(u)=P(X>u) describes a population law. The symbol SnS_nSn​ without a hat denotes a sample sum elsewhere.

The strict inequality matters when an observation equals the threshold. For (1,2,2,4)(1,2,2,4)(1,2,2,4) at u=2u=2u=2, only the last observation contributes, so the fraction is 1/41/41/4. Counting observations greater than or equal to 2 would give 3/43/43/4 and answer a different question.

What the count establishes¶

The indicator definition gives two finite-sample properties.

Proposition 1 (Bounds and monotonicity of empirical survival)

For finite real observations x1,…,xnx_1,\ldots,x_nx1​,…,xn​, n≥1n\ge1n≥1, define S^n(u)=n−1∑i=1n1{xi>u}\widehat S_n(u)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{\{x_i>u\}}Sn​(u)=n−1∑i=1n​1{xi​>u}​. For every u∈Ru\in\mathbb Ru∈R, 0≤S^n(u)≤10\le\widehat S_n(u)\le10≤Sn​(u)≤1; for every s<us<us<u, S^n(u)≤S^n(s)\widehat S_n(u)\le\widehat S_n(s)Sn​(u)≤Sn​(s).

Proof 1

Each indicator is either zero or one, so 0≤S^n(u)≤10\leq\widehat S_n(u)\leq10≤Sn​(u)≤1. If s<us<us<u, every observation exceeding uuu also exceeds sss. Therefore S^n(u)≤S^n(s)\widehat S_n(u)\leq\widehat S_n(s)Sn​(u)≤Sn​(s).

Use exceedance_fraction, empirical_survival_function.

from xmath import exceedance_fraction
from xmath.incerto.stats import empirical_survival_function

sample = [1, 2, 2, 4]
fractions = [exceedance_fraction(sample, u) for u in (0, 2, 4)]
print("Fractions: [" + ", ".join(f"{v:.4g}" for v in fractions) + "]")
support, survival = empirical_survival_function(sample)
print("Support: [" + ", ".join(f"{v:.4g}" for v in support) + "]")
print("Strict survival: [" + ", ".join(f"{v:.4g}" for v in survival) + "]")

Output

Fractions: [1, 0.25, 0]
Support: [1, 2, 4]
Strict survival: [0.75, 0.25, 0]

Ties form one jump.

The same fixed-sample calculation is available as uv run python demos/incerto/exceedances.py.

A zero count above 4 does not establish that a population cannot exceed 4. Inference about an unseen population requires sampling and modeling assumptions that this example deliberately leaves unspecified. The Normix sample instead starts with an explicitly defined random-variable model.

The calculation uses a fixed synthetic sample; it needs no external data.

CDF, survival, and ties¶

Use the right-continuous convention F(u)=P(X≤u)F(u)=P(X\le u)F(u)=P(X≤u). Its complement is Fˉ(u)=P(X>u)\bar F(u)=P(X>u)Fˉ(u)=P(X>u), even when XXX has atoms. For the empirical law that assigns mass 1/n1/n1/n to each observation, the same partition gives the following identities.

Proposition 2 (Empirical CDF complement and mass at a tie)

For finite real observations x1,…,xnx_1,\ldots,x_nx1​,…,xn​, n≥1n\ge1n≥1, and a finite threshold uuu, let F^n(u)=n−1∑i=1n1{xi≤u}\widehat F_n(u)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{\{x_i\le u\}}Fn​(u)=n−1∑i=1n​1{xi​≤u}​ and S^n(u)=n−1∑i=1n1{xi>u}\widehat S_n(u)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{\{x_i>u\}}Sn​(u)=n−1∑i=1n​1{xi​>u}​. Then

S^n(u)=1−F^n(u),1n∑i=1n1{xi≥u}=S^n(u)+1n∑i=1n1{xi=u}.\begin{gathered} \widehat S_n(u)=1-\widehat F_n(u),\\ \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{\{x_i\ge u\}} =\widehat S_n(u)+\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{\{x_i=u\}}. \end{gathered}Sn​(u)=1−Fn​(u),n1​i=1∑n​1{xi​≥u}​=Sn​(u)+n1​i=1∑n​1{xi​=u}​.​
(2)
Proof 2

Partition the observations into xi≤ux_i\le uxi​≤u and xi>ux_i>uxi​>u to obtain the first identity. The event xi≥ux_i\ge uxi​≥u is the disjoint union of xi>ux_i>uxi​>u and xi=ux_i=uxi​=u; summing its indicators gives the second.

What iid sampling adds¶

Summing iid indicators also gives a pointwise consistency result.

Proposition 3 (Mean, variance, and consistency at a fixed threshold)

Let X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\ldotsX1​,X2​,… be iid real random variables with CDF FFF. Fix a nonrandom u∈Ru\in\mathbb Ru∈R and set Fˉ(u)=P(X1>u)\bar F(u)=P(X_1>u)Fˉ(u)=P(X1​>u) and S^n(u)=n−1∑i=1n1{Xi>u}\widehat S_n(u)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{\{X_i>u\}}Sn​(u)=n−1∑i=1n​1{Xi​>u}​. For every n≥1n\ge1n≥1,

ES^n(u)=Fˉ(u),Var⁡(S^n(u))=Fˉ(u)(1−Fˉ(u))n.\begin{gathered} \mathbb E\widehat S_n(u)=\bar F(u),\\ \operatorname{Var}(\widehat S_n(u))=\frac{\bar F(u)(1-\bar F(u))}n. \end{gathered}ESn​(u)=Fˉ(u),Var(Sn​(u))=nFˉ(u)(1−Fˉ(u))​.​
(3)

For every ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0,

P(∣S^n(u)−Fˉ(u)∣>ε)≤Fˉ(u)(1−Fˉ(u))nε2,P(|\widehat S_n(u)-\bar F(u)|>\varepsilon) \le\frac{\bar F(u)(1-\bar F(u))}{n\varepsilon^2},P(∣Sn​(u)−Fˉ(u)∣>ε)≤nε2Fˉ(u)(1−Fˉ(u))​,
(4)

so S^n(u)→Fˉ(u)\widehat S_n(u)\to\bar F(u)Sn​(u)→Fˉ(u) in probability as n→∞n\to\inftyn→∞. No moment assumption on X1X_1X1​ is required.

Proof 3

Each indicator has mean Fˉ(u)\bar F(u)Fˉ(u) and variance Fˉ(u)(1−Fˉ(u))\bar F(u)(1-\bar F(u))Fˉ(u)(1−Fˉ(u)), since its square equals itself. Independence makes the cross-covariances zero. Summing gives the displayed mean and variance. Chebyshev’s inequality gives the probability bound, whose right-hand side tends to zero.

Remark. No moment assumption on XXX is needed: the indicators are bounded. This argument is pointwise in a fixed uuu; it proves neither a uniform-in-threshold theorem nor consistency of an unbounded payoff average.
