Symmetric Shifted Double Pareto Distribution

Statement¶

We use the symmetric shifted convention defined below; the name “double Pareto” is overloaded in the literature and can also refer to positive-valued size distributions with different left and right exponents. Let YYY have a Pareto Type I distribution with lower cutoff 1 and tail exponent α>0\alpha>0α>0. Let BBB be independent of YYY with P(B=1)=P(B=−1)=1/2\mathbb P(B=1)=\mathbb P(B=-1)=1/2P(B=1)=P(B=−1)=1/2. The shifted symmetric double Pareto variable is

X=B(Y−1).X = B(Y-1).X=B(Y−1).
(1)

Its density on the real line is

f(x;α)=α2(1+∣x∣)−(α+1),x∈R.f(x;\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{2}(1+|x|)^{-(\alpha+1)},\qquad x\in\mathbb R.f(x;α)=2α​(1+∣x∣)−(α+1),x∈R.
(2)

Shape and two-sided tail intuition¶

The ordinary Pareto distribution is one-sided: the rare extreme is always on the right. The double Pareto keeps the same power-law magnitude but gives the shock a sign. This makes it useful for toy return models where both large gains and large losses are possible, while preserving a transparent tail exponent.

The density is symmetric, with peak f(0)=α/2f(0)=\alpha/2f(0)=α/2 and

f(x)f(0)=(1+∣x∣)−(α+1).\frac{f(x)}{f(0)}=(1+|x|)^{-(\alpha+1)}.f(0)f(x)​=(1+∣x∣)−(α+1).
(9)

Smaller α\alphaα lowers the central density and puts more probability far from zero on both sides. The chance of a large positive value and the chance of an equally large negative value decay at the same power rate.

We derive the symmetric shifted construction used here from the signed Pareto magnitude X=B(Y−1)X=B(Y-1)X=B(Y−1). The tail, density, and CDF formulas follow directly from symmetry. The moment thresholds use the same Pareto moment logic as Pareto Moment Existence.

Caveats¶

  • We describe a symmetric signed construction whose magnitude ∣X∣|X|∣X∣ has a Lomax law, not every distribution called “double Pareto” in the literature.

  • For α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1, even the absolute first moment is infinite. Symmetry can make a formal location or principal-value calculation look harmless while the ordinary expectation is undefined and absolute exposure remains uncontrolled.

  • The distribution is a pedagogical model. Empirical returns usually need skew, truncation, volatility clustering, dependence, or threshold modeling before a tail fit is credible.
