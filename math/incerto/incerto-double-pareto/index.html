Symmetric Shifted Double Pareto Distribution

Statement¶

We use the symmetric shifted convention defined below; the name “double Pareto” is overloaded in the literature and can also refer to positive-valued size distributions with different left and right exponents. Let YYY have a Pareto Type I distribution with lower cutoff 1 and tail exponent α>0\alpha>0α>0. Let BBB be independent of YYY with P(B=1)=P(B=−1)=1/2\mathbb P(B=1)=\mathbb P(B=-1)=1/2P(B=1)=P(B=−1)=1/2. The shifted symmetric double Pareto variable is

X=B(Y−1).X = B(Y-1).X=B(Y−1).
(1)

Its density on the real line is

f(x;α)=α2(1+∣x∣)−(α+1),x∈R.f(x;\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{2}(1+|x|)^{-(\alpha+1)},\qquad x\in\mathbb R.f(x;α)=2α​(1+∣x∣)−(α+1),x∈R.
(2)

The CDF is

F(x)={12(1−x)−α,x<0,1−12(1+x)−α,x≥0.F(x)= \begin{cases} \frac12(1-x)^{-\alpha}, & x<0,\\ 1-\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha}, & x\ge 0. \end{cases}F(x)={21​(1−x)−α,1−21​(1+x)−α,​x<0,x≥0.​
(3)

For x≥0x\ge0x≥0, each one-sided tail has probability

P(X>x)=12(1+x)−α,P(X<−x)=12(1+x)−α.\mathbb P(X>x)=\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha}, \qquad \mathbb P(X<-x)=\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha}.P(X>x)=21​(1+x)−α,P(X<−x)=21​(1+x)−α.
(4)

Consequently,

P(∣X∣>x)=(1+x)−α.\mathbb P(|X|>x)=(1+x)^{-\alpha}.P(∣X∣>x)=(1+x)−α.
(5)

More generally, the location-scale version μ+sX\mu+sXμ+sX, with μ∈R\mu\in\mathbb Rμ∈R and s>0s>0s>0, has density

fμ+sX(x)=α2s(1+∣x−μ∣s)−(α+1).f_{\mu+sX}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{2s} \left(1+\frac{|x-\mu|}{s}\right)^{-(\alpha+1)}.fμ+sX​(x)=2sα​(1+s∣x−μ∣​)−(α+1).
(6)

The ordinary mean exists and equals 0 exactly when α>1\alpha>1α>1. For α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1, the positive and negative parts are both infinite, so symmetry gives a center but not an ordinary expectation. The variance is finite exactly when α>2\alpha>2α>2, in which case

Var⁡(X)=2(α−1)(α−2).\operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac{2}{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)}.Var(X)=(α−1)(α−2)2​.
(7)

The absolute moments unify these thresholds:

E∣X∣p=Γ(p+1)Γ(α−p)Γ(α),0<p<α.\mathbb E|X|^p =\frac{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(\alpha-p)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}, \qquad 0<p<\alpha.E∣X∣p=Γ(α)Γ(p+1)Γ(α−p)​,0<p<α.
(8)

Here FFF and fff denote the CDF and density; BBB is an independent sign. Γ\GammaΓ denotes the gamma function. Absolute moments diverge for p≥αp\ge\alphap≥α; the displayed gamma expression only applies for 0<p<α0<p<\alpha0<p<α.

Shape and two-sided tail intuition¶

The ordinary Pareto distribution is one-sided: the rare extreme is always on the right. The double Pareto keeps the same power-law magnitude but gives the shock a sign. This makes it useful for toy return models where both large gains and large losses are possible, while preserving a transparent tail exponent.

The density is symmetric, with peak f(0)=α/2f(0)=\alpha/2f(0)=α/2 and

f(x)f(0)=(1+∣x∣)−(α+1).\frac{f(x)}{f(0)}=(1+|x|)^{-(\alpha+1)}.f(0)f(x)​=(1+∣x∣)−(α+1).
(9)

Smaller α\alphaα lowers the central density and puts more probability far from zero on both sides. The chance of a large positive value and the chance of an equally large negative value decay at the same power rate.

Derivation from signed Pareto magnitude¶

We derive the symmetric shifted construction used here from the signed Pareto magnitude X=B(Y−1)X=B(Y-1)X=B(Y−1). The tail, density, and CDF formulas follow directly from symmetry. The moment thresholds use the same Pareto moment logic as Pareto Moment Existence.

For x≥0x\ge0x≥0,

P(X>x)=P(B=1,Y−1>x)=12P(Y>1+x)=12(1+x)−α.\mathbb P(X>x) =\mathbb P(B=1, Y-1>x) =\frac12\mathbb P(Y>1+x) =\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha}.P(X>x)=P(B=1,Y−1>x)=21​P(Y>1+x)=21​(1+x)−α.
(10)

The negative tail is identical by symmetry. Differentiating the CDF on either side gives the density. The expectation is zero only when the first absolute moment exists. When α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1, E[X+]=E[X−]=∞\mathbb E[X^+]=\mathbb E[X^-]=\inftyE[X+]=E[X−]=∞, so the ordinary mean is undefined even though the distribution is symmetric. Since X2=(Y−1)2X^2=(Y-1)^2X2=(Y−1)2,

E[X2]=E[Y2]−2E[Y]+1=αα−2−2αα−1+1=2(α−1)(α−2)\mathbb E[X^2] =\mathbb E[Y^2]-2\mathbb E[Y]+1 =\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-2}-2\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}+1 =\frac{2}{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)}E[X2]=E[Y2]−2E[Y]+1=α−2α​−2α−1α​+1=(α−1)(α−2)2​
(11)

for α>2\alpha>2α>2. For 1<α≤21<\alpha\le21<α≤2, the mean exists but the variance is infinite. For α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1, the ordinary mean, and hence variance about that mean, is undefined; the second raw moment is still infinite.

Matching a Student tail or a variance¶

SCoFT v4 §5.3, p. 101 Taleb, 2025, compares a symmetric shifted power law with a Student law. We keep the normalized density α/2\alpha/2α/2 above on each side and derive both matching scales. Equal exponents do not imply equal moments or equal leading tail constants.

Caveats¶

  • We describe a symmetric signed construction whose magnitude ∣X∣|X|∣X∣ has a Lomax law, not every distribution called “double Pareto” in the literature.

  • For α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1, even the absolute first moment is infinite. Symmetry can make a formal location or principal-value calculation look harmless while the ordinary expectation is undefined and absolute exposure remains uncontrolled.

  • The distribution is a pedagogical model. Empirical returns usually need skew, truncation, volatility clustering, dependence, or threshold modeling before a tail fit is credible.
