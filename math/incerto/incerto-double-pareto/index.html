Symmetric Shifted Double Pareto Distribution

Statement¶

We use the symmetric shifted convention defined below; the name “double Pareto” is overloaded in the literature and can also refer to positive-valued size distributions with different left and right exponents. The signed-magnitude construction gives the following formulas.

Proposition 1 (Symmetric shifted double-Pareto law and moments)

Let YYY have a Pareto Type I distribution with lower cutoff 1 and tail exponent α>0\alpha>0α>0. Let BBB be independent of YYY with P(B=1)=P(B=−1)=1/2\mathbb P(B=1)=\mathbb P(B=-1)=1/2P(B=1)=P(B=−1)=1/2. The shifted symmetric double Pareto variable is

X=B(Y−1).X = B(Y-1).X=B(Y−1).
(1)

Its density on the real line is

f(x;α)=α2(1+∣x∣)−(α+1),x∈R.f(x;\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{2}(1+|x|)^{-(\alpha+1)},\qquad x\in\mathbb R.f(x;α)=2α​(1+∣x∣)−(α+1),x∈R.
(2)

The CDF is

F(x)={12(1−x)−α,x<0,1−12(1+x)−α,x≥0.F(x)= \begin{cases} \frac12(1-x)^{-\alpha}, & x<0,\\ 1-\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha}, & x\ge 0. \end{cases}F(x)={21​(1−x)−α,1−21​(1+x)−α,​x<0,x≥0.​
(3)

For x≥0x\ge0x≥0, each one-sided tail has probability

P(X>x)=12(1+x)−α,P(X<−x)=12(1+x)−α.\mathbb P(X>x)=\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha}, \qquad \mathbb P(X<-x)=\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha}.P(X>x)=21​(1+x)−α,P(X<−x)=21​(1+x)−α.
(4)

Consequently,

P(∣X∣>x)=(1+x)−α.\mathbb P(|X|>x)=(1+x)^{-\alpha}.P(∣X∣>x)=(1+x)−α.
(5)

More generally, the location-scale version μ+sX\mu+sXμ+sX, with μ∈R\mu\in\mathbb Rμ∈R and s>0s>0s>0, has density

fμ+sX(x)=α2s(1+∣x−μ∣s)−(α+1).f_{\mu+sX}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{2s} \left(1+\frac{|x-\mu|}{s}\right)^{-(\alpha+1)}.fμ+sX​(x)=2sα​(1+s∣x−μ∣​)−(α+1).
(6)

The ordinary mean exists and equals 0 exactly when α>1\alpha>1α>1. For α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1, the expectations of the positive and negative parts are both infinite, so symmetry gives a center but not an ordinary expectation. The variance is finite exactly when α>2\alpha>2α>2, in which case

Var⁡(X)=2(α−1)(α−2).\operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac{2}{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)}.Var(X)=(α−1)(α−2)2​.
(7)

The absolute moments unify these thresholds:

E∣X∣p=Γ(p+1)Γ(α−p)Γ(α),0<p<α.\mathbb E|X|^p =\frac{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(\alpha-p)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}, \qquad 0<p<\alpha.E∣X∣p=Γ(α)Γ(p+1)Γ(α−p)​,0<p<α.
(8)

Here FFF and fff denote the CDF and density; BBB is an independent sign. Γ\GammaΓ denotes the gamma function. Absolute moments diverge for p≥αp\ge\alphap≥α; the displayed gamma expression only applies for 0<p<α0<p<\alpha0<p<α. For 1<α≤21<\alpha\le21<α≤2, the variance is infinite. For 0<α≤10<\alpha\le10<α≤1, variance about the undefined mean is undefined; the second raw moment is infinite for every 0<α≤20<\alpha\le20<α≤2.

Derivation from signed Pareto magnitude¶

We prove Proposition 1 from X=B(Y−1)X=B(Y-1)X=B(Y−1). The moment thresholds use the same Pareto moment logic as Pareto Moment Existence.

Proof 1

For x≥0x\ge0x≥0,

P(X>x)=P(B=1,Y−1>x)=12P(Y>1+x)=12(1+x)−α.\mathbb P(X>x) =\mathbb P(B=1, Y-1>x) =\frac12\mathbb P(Y>1+x) =\frac12(1+x)^{-\alpha}.P(X>x)=P(B=1,Y−1>x)=21​P(Y>1+x)=21​(1+x)−α.
(9)

The negative tail is identical by symmetry; adding the two tails gives the absolute survival. Complementing the appropriate tail gives the CDF, with F(0)=1/2F(0)=1/2F(0)=1/2 because there is no atom at zero. Differentiating on either side gives the density, with the continuous density value at zero as stated. The change of variable x↦(x−μ)/sx\mapsto(x-\mu)/sx↦(x−μ)/s gives the location-scale density. The expectation is zero only when the first absolute moment exists. When α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1, E[X+]=E[X−]=∞\mathbb E[X^+]=\mathbb E[X^-]=\inftyE[X+]=E[X−]=∞, so the ordinary mean is undefined even though the distribution is symmetric. Since X2=(Y−1)2X^2=(Y-1)^2X2=(Y−1)2,

E[X2]=E[Y2]−2E[Y]+1=αα−2−2αα−1+1=2(α−1)(α−2)\mathbb E[X^2] =\mathbb E[Y^2]-2\mathbb E[Y]+1 =\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-2}-2\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}+1 =\frac{2}{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)}E[X2]=E[Y2]−2E[Y]+1=α−2α​−2α−1α​+1=(α−1)(α−2)2​
(10)

for α>2\alpha>2α>2. For 1<α≤21<\alpha\le21<α≤2, the mean exists but the variance is infinite. For α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1, the ordinary mean, and hence variance about that mean, is undefined; the second raw moment is still infinite.

More generally, integration of the absolute-value density gives the beta integral

E∣X∣p=α∫0∞tp(1+t)−α−1 dt=Γ(p+1)Γ(α−p)Γ(α),0<p<α.\mathbb E|X|^p=\alpha\int_0^\infty t^p(1+t)^{-\alpha-1}\,dt =\frac{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(\alpha-p)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}, \qquad 0<p<\alpha.E∣X∣p=α∫0∞​tp(1+t)−α−1dt=Γ(α)Γ(p+1)Γ(α−p)​,0<p<α.
(11)

The integrand is integrable near zero for p>0p>0p>0 and behaves as tp−α−1t^{p-\alpha-1}tp−α−1 at infinity, so it diverges for p≥αp\ge\alphap≥α. By symmetry, each first-moment half is infinite when α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1.

Shape and two-sided tail intuition¶

The ordinary Pareto distribution is one-sided: the rare extreme is always on the right. The double Pareto keeps the same power-law magnitude but gives the shock a sign. This makes it useful for toy return models where both large gains and large losses are possible, while preserving a transparent tail exponent.

The density is symmetric, with peak f(0)=α/2f(0)=\alpha/2f(0)=α/2 and

f(x)f(0)=(1+∣x∣)−(α+1).\frac{f(x)}{f(0)}=(1+|x|)^{-(\alpha+1)}.f(0)f(x)​=(1+∣x∣)−(α+1).
(12)

Smaller α\alphaα lowers the central density and puts more probability far from zero on both sides. The chance of a large positive value and the chance of an equally large negative value decay at the same power rate.

Two-sided tail calculations¶

The exact two-sided survival (1+x)−α(1+x)^{-\alpha}(1+x)−α is regularly varying with index −α-\alpha−α, as the ratio calculation shows.

Corollary 1 (Regular variation of the shifted magnitude)

Let XXX have density f(x)=α2(1+∣x∣)−α−1f(x)=\frac\alpha2(1+|x|)^{-\alpha-1}f(x)=2α​(1+∣x∣)−α−1 with α>0\alpha>0α>0. For every fixed t>0t>0t>0,

lim⁡x→∞P(∣X∣>tx)P(∣X∣>x)=t−α.\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{P(|X|>tx)}{P(|X|>x)}=t^{-\alpha}.x→∞lim​P(∣X∣>x)P(∣X∣>tx)​=t−α.
(13)

Thus its absolute survival, and each one-sided tail, are regularly varying with index −α-\alpha−α.

Proof 2

By Proposition 1, for x≥0x\ge0x≥0,

P(∣X∣>tx)P(∣X∣>x)=(1+tx1+x)−α→t−α.\frac{\mathbb P(|X|>tx)}{\mathbb P(|X|>x)} =\left(\frac{1+tx}{1+x}\right)^{-\alpha}\to t^{-\alpha}.P(∣X∣>x)P(∣X∣>tx)​=(1+x1+tx​)−α→t−α.
(14)

Each one-sided tail is half the absolute survival, so the factor cancels in its ratio.

Matching a Student tail or a variance¶

Caveats¶

  • We describe a symmetric signed construction whose magnitude ∣X∣|X|∣X∣ has a Lomax law, not every distribution called “double Pareto” in the literature.

  • For α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1, even the absolute first moment is infinite. Symmetry can make a formal location or principal-value calculation look harmless while the ordinary expectation is undefined and absolute exposure remains uncontrolled.

  • The distribution is a pedagogical model. Empirical returns usually need skew, truncation, volatility clustering, dependence, or threshold modeling before a tail fit is credible.
