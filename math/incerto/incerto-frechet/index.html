Frechet Distribution and Frechet-Type Limits

Statement¶

The Frechet distribution is the positive-shape extreme-value law for normalized maxima with heavy right tails. In the standard one-parameter form with shape α>0\alpha>0α>0, its CDF is

Φα(y)={0,y≤0,exp⁡(−y−α),y>0.\Phi_\alpha(y) = \begin{cases} 0, & y\le0,\\ \exp(-y^{-\alpha}), & y>0. \end{cases}Φα​(y)={0,exp(−y−α),​y≤0,y>0.​
(1)

For y>0y>0y>0, the density is

ϕα(y)=αy−α−1exp⁡(−y−α).\phi_\alpha(y) = \alpha y^{-\alpha-1}\exp(-y^{-\alpha}).ϕα​(y)=αy−α−1exp(−y−α).
(2)

It is the ξ>0\xi>0ξ>0 member of the generalized extreme-value distribution. With ξ=1/α\xi=1/\alphaξ=1/α, location μ=1\mu=1μ=1, and scale σ=ξ\sigma=\xiσ=ξ,

Hξ,1,ξ(y)=exp⁡[−y−1/ξ]=Φα(y),y>0.\begin{gathered} H_{\xi,1,\xi}(y)=\exp[-y^{-1/\xi}]\\ =\Phi_\alpha(y),\qquad y>0. \end{gathered}Hξ,1,ξ​(y)=exp[−y−1/ξ]=Φα​(y),y>0.​
(3)

The phrase Frechet-type refers to distributions whose normalized maxima converge to this law. In the usual right-tail setting, this is the regularly varying maximum-domain-of-attraction case: a Pareto-type survival tail with exponent α\alphaα has extreme-value index ξ=1/α>0\xi=1/\alpha>0ξ=1/α>0. Here FFF is the parent CDF, Fˉ=1−F\bar F=1-FFˉ=1−F its survival function, XiX_iXi​ its iid draws, and xm>0x_m>0xm​>0 the lower cutoff in the exact Pareto example. Φα\Phi_\alphaΦα​ and ϕα\phi_\alphaϕα​ denote the Frechet CDF and density.

Maxima interpretation¶

Frechet-type behavior is a statement about extremes, not necessarily about the full parent distribution. If X1,…,XnX_1,\dots,X_nX1​,…,Xn​ are iid and the right tail is regularly varying with exponent α>0\alpha>0α>0, then an appropriate positive normalization ana_nan​ puts the maximum

Mn=max⁡1≤i≤nXiM_n=\max_{1\le i\le n} X_iMn​=1≤i≤nmax​Xi​
(4)

on the Frechet scale. The full regular-variation domain-of-attraction theorem is cited in the references; the exact Pareto calculation below shows the core mechanism.

Let X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\ldotsX1​,X2​,… be iid Pareto Type I with lower cutoff xm>0x_m>0xm​>0 and exponent α>0\alpha>0α>0. Set Mn=max⁡1≤i≤nXiM_n=\max_{1\le i\le n}X_iMn​=max1≤i≤n​Xi​ and an=xmn1/αa_n=x_m n^{1/\alpha}an​=xm​n1/α. For every fixed y>0y>0y>0 and all nnn such that any≥xma_ny\ge x_man​y≥xm​,

Tail behavior¶

Although the Frechet law is a limit law for maxima, it is itself heavy-tailed. The survival asymptotic follows from the elementary exponential limit.

Proposition 2 (Tail exponent of the Frechet law)

For α>0\alpha>0α>0, the standard Frechet CDF Φα(y)=exp⁡(−y−α)\Phi_\alpha(y)=\exp(-y^{-\alpha})Φα​(y)=exp(−y−α) on y>0y>0y>0 satisfies

Φˉα(y)=1−exp⁡(−y−α)∼y−α\bar\Phi_\alpha(y) = 1-\exp(-y^{-\alpha}) \sim y^{-\alpha}Φˉα​(y)=1−exp(−y−α)∼y−α
(7)

as y→∞y\to\inftyy→∞, and its survival is regularly varying with index −α-\alpha−α.

Proof 2

Set v=y−α→0v=y^{-\alpha}\to0v=y−α→0. Then (1−e−v)/v→1(1-e^{-v})/v\to1(1−e−v)/v→1, proving the equivalence. Taking the ratio at tytyty and yyy gives t−αt^{-\alpha}t−α for every fixed t>0t>0t>0.

Thus the Frechet survival tail is regularly varying with index −α-\alpha−α. This is why the ξ>0\xi>0ξ>0 GEV regime is also the Pareto-type regime: the block-maximum limit and the parent survival tail share the same reciprocal coordinate ξ=1/α\xi=1/\alphaξ=1/α.

A power tail need not have a constant multiplier¶

SCoFT v4 §9.1.1, pp. 173–174, motivates the power-tail/Fréchet link Taleb, 2025. We prove its sufficient direction without replacing a slowly varying factor by a constant.

Proposition 3 (Regular variation implies a Frechet maximum limit)

Let X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\ldotsX1​,X2​,… be iid with CDF FFF, survival Fˉ(x)=x−αL(x)\bar F(x)=x^{-\alpha}L(x)Fˉ(x)=x−αL(x) eventually, where α>0\alpha>0α>0 and LLL is positive, measurable, and slowly varying. Let an>0a_n>0an​>0 tend to infinity with nFˉ(an)→1n\bar F(a_n)\to1nFˉ(an​)→1, and set Mn=max⁡1≤i≤nXiM_n=\max_{1\le i\le n}X_iMn​=max1≤i≤n​Xi​. Then, for every fixed y>0y>0y>0, P(Mn/an≤y)→exp⁡(−y−α)P(M_n/a_n\le y)\to\exp(-y^{-\alpha})P(Mn​/an​≤y)→exp(−y−α).

Proof 4

For fixed y>0y>0y>0, regular variation gives

nFˉ(any)=nFˉ(an)Fˉ(any)Fˉ(an)⟶y−α.\begin{aligned} &n\bar F(a_ny)\\ &\quad=n\bar F(a_n)\frac{\bar F(a_ny)}{\bar F(a_n)}\\ &\longrightarrow y^{-\alpha}. \end{aligned}​nFˉ(an​y)=nFˉ(an​)Fˉ(an​)Fˉ(an​y)​⟶y−α.​
(9)

Independence gives P(Mn/an≤y)=(1−Fˉ(any))nP(M_n/a_n\le y)=(1-\bar F(a_ny))^nP(Mn​/an​≤y)=(1−Fˉ(an​y))n. Taking logarithms and using log⁡(1−v)∼−v\log(1-v)\sim-vlog(1−v)∼−v proves the limit.

For an eventually continuous, strictly decreasing survival, the tail quantile solving Fˉ(an)=1/n\bar F(a_n)=1/nFˉ(an​)=1/n is an admissible choice. The general converse remains a cited domain-of-attraction result Embrechts et al., 1997Resnick, 2007. The exact Pareto scale xmn1/αx_mn^{1/\alpha}xm​n1/α does not apply to every LLL.

A separate fixed-block fact has a different limit.

Proposition 4 (A fixed-block maximum preserves the tail exponent)

Let X1,…,XnX_1,\ldots,X_nX1​,…,Xn​ be iid with survival Fˉ∈RV−α\bar F\in RV_{-\alpha}Fˉ∈RV−α​, where α>0\alpha>0α>0 and the integer n≥1n\ge1n≥1 is fixed. For Mn=max⁡1≤i≤nXiM_n=\max_{1\le i\le n}X_iMn​=max1≤i≤n​Xi​, as x→∞x\to\inftyx→∞,

P(Mn>x)=1−(1−Fˉ(x))n∼nFˉ(x).\begin{aligned} P(M_n>x)&=1-(1-\bar F(x))^n\\ &\sim n\bar F(x). \end{aligned}P(Mn​>x)​=1−(1−Fˉ(x))n∼nFˉ(x).​
(10)

In particular, the survival of MnM_nMn​ is regularly varying with index −α-\alpha−α.

Proof 5

Independence gives the equality. Since Fˉ(x)→0\bar F(x)\to0Fˉ(x)→0, the binomial expansion gives 1−(1−Fˉ(x))n=nFˉ(x)+O(Fˉ(x)2)1-(1-\bar F(x))^n=n\bar F(x)+O(\bar F(x)^2)1−(1−Fˉ(x))n=nFˉ(x)+O(Fˉ(x)2). The equivalence preserves the fixed-multiplier survival ratios.

This limit sends the threshold to infinity with nnn fixed; the Fréchet limit sends nnn to infinity after rescaling. Neither implies an exact finite-block Fréchet law. The hidden-tail calculation uses the exact Pareto maximum and therefore keeps finite-sample corrections.

Caveats¶

  • Frechet-type is a domain-of-attraction label. It does not say the original observations themselves follow a Frechet distribution.

  • The ξ>0\xi>0ξ>0 conclusion is asymptotic. Finite samples can look Frechet-like over one range and deviate elsewhere because of body contamination, dependence, truncation, censoring, or second-order tail behavior.

  • GEV block-maxima fitting uses a location-scale family. The unit Frechet law above is a convenient standard representative of the positive-shape class.
