Generalized Extreme-Value Distribution

Statement¶

The generalized extreme-value distribution is the standard limit family for normalized block maxima. With location μ\muμ, scale σ>0\sigma>0σ>0, shape ξ\xiξ, and standardized variable z=(x−μ)/σz=(x-\mu)/\sigmaz=(x−μ)/σ, its CDF is

Hξ,μ,σ(x)=exp⁡[−(1+ξz)−1/ξ],ξ≠0,\begin{gathered} H_{\xi,\mu,\sigma}(x)\\ =\exp\left[-\left(1+\xi z\right)^{-1/\xi}\right],\\ \xi\ne0, \end{gathered}Hξ,μ,σ​(x)=exp[−(1+ξz)−1/ξ],ξ=0,​
(1)

where 1+ξz>01+\xi z>01+ξz>0. For ξ>0\xi>0ξ>0, extend the CDF by zero at and below μ−σ/ξ\mu-\sigma/\xiμ−σ/ξ; for ξ<0\xi<0ξ<0, extend it by one at and above μ−σ/ξ\mu-\sigma/\xiμ−σ/ξ. The boundary case is the Gumbel law,

H0,μ,σ(x)=exp⁡[−e−z].H_{0,\mu,\sigma}(x) = \exp\left[-e^{-z}\right].H0,μ,σ​(x)=exp[−e−z].
(2)

The shape parameter ξ\xiξ is the same extreme-value index used in threshold excesses: ξ>0\xi>0ξ>0 is Frechet-type heavy-tail behavior, ξ=0\xi=0ξ=0 is Gumbel-type behavior, and ξ<0\xi<0ξ<0 is Weibull-type finite endpoint. Here XiX_iXi​ are observations and Mn=max⁡1≤i≤nXiM_n=\max_{1\le i\le n}X_iMn​=max1≤i≤n​Xi​. The Pareto example below uses lower cutoff xm>0x_m>0xm​>0 and exponent α>0\alpha>0α>0. Gumbel attraction is broader than exponential parent tails: normal and lognormal parents are examples, despite their different tail decay.

Maxima intuition¶

The distribution is to block maxima what the Generalized Pareto Distribution is to threshold exceedances. For iid observations, if there are constants an>0a_n>0an​>0 and bnb_nbn​ such that (Mn−bn)/an(M_n-b_n)/a_n(Mn​−bn​)/an​ converges to a nondegenerate law, that law is a GEV distribution up to location and scale. Existence of such a limit is an assumption; it does not hold for every parent law.

For fat-tail work, ξ>0\xi>0ξ>0 is the main case. A Pareto tail with exponent α\alphaα has ξ=1/α\xi=1/\alphaξ=1/α, so the fitted block-maximum shape should point to the same tail coordinate as a Hill or GPD threshold analysis when the modeling assumptions are reasonable.

Pareto block maxima example¶

If X1,…,XnX_1,\dots,X_nX1​,…,Xn​ are iid Pareto Type I with lower cutoff xmx_mxm​ and exponent α\alphaα, then for x≥xmx\ge x_mx≥xm​,

P(Mn≤x)=(1−(xmx)α)n.\begin{aligned} &\mathbb P(M_n\le x)\\ &\quad=\left(1-\left(\frac{x_m}{x}\right)^\alpha\right)^n. \end{aligned}​P(Mn​≤x)=(1−(xxm​​)α)n.​
(3)

With an=xmn1/αa_n=x_m n^{1/\alpha}an​=xm​n1/α, for fixed y>0y>0y>0 and sufficiently large nnn,

P(Mn/an≤y)=(1−1nyα)n→exp⁡(−y−α),y>0.\begin{gathered} \begin{aligned} &\mathbb P(M_n/a_n\le y)\\ &\quad=\left(1-\frac{1}{ny^\alpha}\right)^n\\ &\quad\to\exp(-y^{-\alpha}), \end{aligned}\\ y>0. \end{gathered}​P(Mn​/an​≤y)=(1−nyα1​)n→exp(−y−α),​y>0.​
(4)

This is the Frechet member of the GEV family with ξ=1/α\xi=1/\alphaξ=1/α after a standard location-scale reparameterization.

Block-size calibration¶

For an exact Pareto parent with α=1.8=9/5\alpha=1.8=9/5α=1.8=9/5, the limiting shape is ξ=5/9\xi=5/9ξ=5/9. At block size n=250n=250n=250, the normalizing scale is an=xm2505/9a_n=x_m250^{5/9}an​=xm​2505/9. At the normalized threshold y=1y=1y=1,

P(M250/a250≤1)=(249/250)250≈0.367142,Φ1.8(1)=e−1≈0.367879.\begin{gathered} \begin{aligned} &\mathbb P(M_{250}/a_{250}\le1)\\ &\quad=(249/250)^{250}\approx0.367142, \end{aligned}\\ \Phi_{1.8}(1)=e^{-1}\approx0.367879. \end{gathered}​P(M250​/a250​≤1)=(249/250)250≈0.367142,​Φ1.8​(1)=e−1≈0.367879.​
(5)

This compares an exact finite-block CDF with its limit; it is not a GEV fit or an assessment of sampling uncertainty. The standard Frechet limit has GEV parameters (ξ,μ,σ)=(5/9,1,5/9)(\xi,\mu,\sigma)=(5/9,1,5/9)(ξ,μ,σ)=(5/9,1,5/9) on the normalized scale.

Caveats¶

  • Block maxima discard within-block information. Threshold methods may use extremes more efficiently, but they require a threshold choice.

  • Blocks should be chosen with dependence and seasonality in mind. Overlapping or strongly dependent blocks can make fitted uncertainty too optimistic.

  • A GEV fit describes maxima, not the entire parent distribution.

  • Software may use a different shape sign convention. Translate fitted parameters into the displayed CDF before comparing maxima and excesses.
