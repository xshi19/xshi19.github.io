Generalized Pareto Distribution

Statement¶

The generalized Pareto distribution is the standard limit family for threshold excesses. With location zero, an excess variable Y≥0Y\ge0Y≥0 has shape ξ\xiξ and scale β>0\beta>0β>0 when

Gξ,β(y)=1−(1+ξyβ)−1/ξ,ξ≠0,\begin{gathered} G_{\xi,\beta}(y) =1-\left(1+\frac{\xi y}{\beta}\right)^{-1/\xi},\\ \xi\ne0, \end{gathered}Gξ,β​(y)=1−(1+βξy​)−1/ξ,ξ=0,​
(1)

for y≥0y\ge0y≥0 with 1+ξy/β>01+\xi y/\beta>01+ξy/β>0. For y<0y<0y<0 the CDF is zero; for ξ<0\xi<0ξ<0 it is one at and above −β/ξ-\beta/\xi−β/ξ, by continuity. The boundary case is

G0,β(y)=1−e−y/β.G_{0,\beta}(y)=1-e^{-y/\beta}.G0,β​(y)=1−e−y/β.
(2)

For ξ≥0\xi\ge0ξ≥0, the support is y≥0y\ge0y≥0. For ξ<0\xi<0ξ<0, the support is 0≤y≤−β/ξ0\le y\le -\beta/\xi0≤y≤−β/ξ, so the tail has a finite endpoint. The survival function for ξ≠0\xi\ne0ξ=0 is

Gˉξ,β(y)=(1+ξyβ)−1/ξ.\bar G_{\xi,\beta}(y) = \left(1+\frac{\xi y}{\beta}\right)^{-1/\xi}.Gˉξ,β​(y)=(1+βξy​)−1/ξ.
(3)

The mean exists only for ξ<1\xi<1ξ<1 and equals β/(1−ξ)\beta/(1-\xi)β/(1−ξ). The variance exists only for ξ<1/2\xi<1/2ξ<1/2 and equals

β2(1−ξ)2(1−2ξ).\frac{\beta^2}{(1-\xi)^2(1-2\xi)}.(1−ξ)2(1−2ξ)β2​.
(4)

The moment formulas and their boundaries are proved in Proposition 2.

Shape and endpoint intuition¶

The generalized Pareto distribution is the peaks-over-threshold counterpart of the generalized extreme-value distribution for block maxima. Once a threshold is high enough, the distribution of the excess over that threshold is modeled with one shape parameter ξ\xiξ and one scale parameter β\betaβ.

The sign and size of ξ\xiξ carry the tail story. Positive ξ\xiξ gives a Pareto-type heavy tail. Zero gives the exponential boundary. Negative ξ\xiξ gives a finite endpoint. In the fat-tail examples, the most important boundary values are ξ=1/2\xi=1/2ξ=1/2 for variance and ξ=1\xi=1ξ=1 for the mean.

For β=1\beta=1β=1, the three examples from the survival formula are:

Shape ξ\xiξSurvival Gˉ(y)\bar G(y)Gˉ(y)Upper endpoint
-0.4(1−0.4y)2.5(1-0.4y)^{2.5}(1−0.4y)2.5 for 0≤y≤2.50\le y\le2.50≤y≤2.52.5
0e−ye^{-y}e−y for y≥0y\ge0y≥0Infinite
0.4(1+0.4y)−2.5(1+0.4y)^{-2.5}(1+0.4y)−2.5 for y≥0y\ge0y≥0Infinite

What to notice. Positive ξ\xiξ gives the slowest-decaying curve, ξ=0\xi=0ξ=0 is the exponential boundary, and negative ξ\xiξ ends at the finite endpoint −β/ξ-\beta/\xi−β/ξ.

Threshold-excess stability and Pareto special case¶

A survival ratio establishes threshold stability and the exact Pareto case.

Proposition 1 (Threshold stability and exact Pareto excesses)

If YYY has a GPD with location zero, shape ξ∈R\xi\in\mathbb Rξ∈R, and scale β>0\beta>0β>0, and u≥0u\ge0u≥0 satisfies β+ξu>0\beta+\xi u>0β+ξu>0, then Y−u∣Y>uY-u\mid Y>uY−u∣Y>u is GPD with shape ξ\xiξ and scale β+ξu\beta+\xi uβ+ξu. If XXX is Pareto Type I with cutoff xm>0x_m>0xm​>0 and exponent α>0\alpha>0α>0, then for every u≥xmu\ge x_mu≥xm​, X−u∣X>uX-u\mid X>uX−u∣X>u is GPD with ξ=1/α\xi=1/\alphaξ=1/α and β(u)=u/α\beta(u)=u/\alphaβ(u)=u/α.

Proof 1

For admissible y≥0y\ge0y≥0 and ξ≠0\xi\ne0ξ=0, the conditional survival is

Gˉξ,β(u+y)Gˉξ,β(u)=(1+ξyβ+ξu)−1/ξ.\begin{gathered} \frac{\bar G_{\xi,\beta}(u+y)}{\bar G_{\xi,\beta}(u)}\\ =\left(1+\frac{\xi y}{\beta+\xi u}\right)^{-1/\xi}. \end{gathered}Gˉξ,β​(u)Gˉξ,β​(u+y)​=(1+β+ξuξy​)−1/ξ.​
(5)

For ξ=0\xi=0ξ=0, the ratio is e−y/βe^{-y/\beta}e−y/β. Thus excesses have the same shape and updated scale β+ξu\beta+\xi uβ+ξu.

The Pareto Type I distribution is an exact generalized Pareto excess model. If XXX has Pareto tail exponent α>0\alpha>0α>0 and threshold u≥xm>0u\ge x_m>0u≥xm​>0, then for y≥0y\ge0y≥0,

P(X−u>y∣X>u)=(1+yu)−α.\begin{aligned} &\mathbb P(X-u>y\mid X>u)\\ &\quad=\left(1+\frac{y}{u}\right)^{-\alpha}. \end{aligned}​P(X−u>y∣X>u)=(1+uy​)−α.​
(6)

This is the generalized Pareto survival function with

ξ=1α,β(u)=uα.\xi=\frac1\alpha, \qquad \beta(u)=\frac{u}{\alpha}.ξ=α1​,β(u)=αu​.
(7)

Combining threshold stability with the GPD mean gives the mean-excess formula.

The Pickands-Balkema-de Haan theorem, cited rather than proved here, explains why the same family appears asymptotically for a much larger class of threshold exceedances.

(8)

Thus Gˉ(0)=1\bar G(0)=1Gˉ(0)=1, Gˉ(1)=1.2−2.5\bar G(1)=1.2^{-2.5}Gˉ(1)=1.2−2.5, Gˉ(5)=2−2.5\bar G(5)=2^{-2.5}Gˉ(5)=2−2.5, and Gˉ(10)=3−2.5\bar G(10)=3^{-2.5}Gˉ(10)=3−2.5. These are formula evaluations, with no numerical fitting or simulation.

Deriving the moment boundaries¶

We prove the moment formulas used above by integrating survival.

Proposition 2 (GPD moments and their finiteness boundaries)

Let YYY have a GPD with location zero, shape ξ∈R\xi\in\mathbb Rξ∈R, and scale β>0\beta>0β>0. Its first two raw moments are

E[Y]={β/(1−ξ),ξ<1,+∞,ξ≥1,E[Y2]={2β2/[(1−ξ)(1−2ξ)],ξ<1/2,+∞,ξ≥1/2.\begin{gathered} \mathbb E[Y]= \begin{cases} \beta/(1-\xi),&\xi<1,\\ +\infty,&\xi\ge1, \end{cases}\\ \mathbb E[Y^2]= \begin{cases} 2\beta^2/[(1-\xi)(1-2\xi)],&\xi<1/2,\\ +\infty,&\xi\ge1/2. \end{cases} \end{gathered}E[Y]={β/(1−ξ),+∞,​ξ<1,ξ≥1,​E[Y2]={2β2/[(1−ξ)(1−2ξ)],+∞,​ξ<1/2,ξ≥1/2.​​
(9)

For ξ<1/2\xi<1/2ξ<1/2, Var⁡(Y)=β2/[(1−ξ)2(1−2ξ)]\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\beta^2/[(1-\xi)^2(1-2\xi)]Var(Y)=β2/[(1−ξ)2(1−2ξ)]. For 1/2≤ξ<11/2\le\xi<11/2≤ξ<1, the variance is infinite; for ξ≥1\xi\ge1ξ≥1, the variance about a finite mean is undefined. These formulas include the exponential case ξ=0\xi=0ξ=0 and the bounded-support cases ξ<0\xi<0ξ<0.

Proof 2

Let bbb be the upper support endpoint, possibly infinite. For nonnegative YYY, Tonelli’s theorem gives

E[Y]=∫0bGˉ(y) dy,E[Y2]=2∫0byGˉ(y) dy.\begin{gathered} \mathbb E[Y]=\int_0^b\bar G(y)\,dy,\\ \mathbb E[Y^2]=2\int_0^b y\bar G(y)\,dy. \end{gathered}E[Y]=∫0b​Gˉ(y)dy,E[Y2]=2∫0b​yGˉ(y)dy.​
(10)

For ξ≠0\xi\ne0ξ=0, change variables y←1+ξy/βy\leftarrow1+\xi y/\betay←1+ξy/β in both integrals; in this paragraph, yyy denotes the transformed integration coordinate. The integration limits become 1 to 0 for ξ<0\xi<0ξ<0 and 1 to ∞\infty∞ for ξ>0\xi>0ξ>0, with differential factor β/ξ\beta/\xiβ/ξ. For ξ≠1\xi\ne1ξ=1, the transformed first integral’s antiderivative is βy1−1/ξ/(ξ−1)\beta y^{1-1/\xi}/(\xi-1)βy1−1/ξ/(ξ−1). At the appropriate endpoint this gives β/(1−ξ)\beta/(1-\xi)β/(1−ξ) when ξ<1\xi<1ξ<1. At ξ=1\xi=1ξ=1 the integral diverges logarithmically, and it also diverges for ξ>1\xi>1ξ>1. The excess factor in the second integral becomes β(y−1)/ξ\beta(y-1)/\xiβ(y−1)/ξ, which similarly gives

E[Y2]=2β2(1−ξ)(1−2ξ),ξ<1/2.\begin{gathered} \mathbb E[Y^2]=\frac{2\beta^2}{(1-\xi)(1-2\xi)},\\ \xi<1/2. \end{gathered}E[Y2]=(1−ξ)(1−2ξ)2β2​,ξ<1/2.​
(11)

Subtracting the squared mean gives the variance stated above. For ξ=0\xi=0ξ=0, exponential integration gives the same formulas directly. For ξ>0\xi>0ξ>0, the second-moment integrand in the original excess coordinate is asymptotic to a positive constant times y1−1/ξy^{1-1/\xi}y1−1/ξ, so that integral diverges precisely when ξ≥1/2\xi\ge1/2ξ≥1/2. Positive shape 1/2≤ξ<11/2\le\xi<11/2≤ξ<1 has infinite variance; at ξ≥1\xi\ge1ξ≥1 the ordinary variance about a finite mean is undefined. Negative shape has bounded support, so all these integrals are finite.

The chapter 9 guide connects these exact-model boundaries to the PBDH theorem. A fitted GPD mean does not automatically estimate the true tail mean accurately.

Caveats¶

  • A GPD fit is a tail model for exceedances, not a model for the full distribution body.

  • The threshold uuu is not chosen by the theorem. Shape and scale estimates should be inspected across a range of thresholds.

  • When ξ≥1\xi\ge1ξ≥1, the fitted excess distribution has no finite mean. When ξ≥1/2\xi\ge1/2ξ≥1/2, it has no finite variance.

  • Dependence, truncation, censoring, seasonality, and mixture effects can distort threshold-excess fits.
