Hill Estimator

Overview¶

How heavy is the upper tail, and how sensitive is the answer to where we start fitting it? Hill estimates the exponent of a positive Pareto-type tail from the largest observations. Choosing how many observations to retain is part of the estimate.

Read the Pareto and regular variation pages first. The formula uses logarithms and sorted observations; the extreme-value index ξ\xiξ is the reciprocal of the Pareto exponent α\alphaα. For data analysis, inspect several thresholds and check dependence and measurement limits before interpreting a stable-looking curve. A dedicated threshold-selection page is planned.

Estimator¶

Let x1,…,xnx_1,\dots,x_nx1​,…,xn​ be observed right-tail data with xi>0x_i>0xi​>0 for every iii, and write the ascending realized order statistics as

x1:n≤⋯≤xn:n.x_{1:n}\le \cdots \le x_{n:n}.x1:n​≤⋯≤xn:n​.
(1)

For 1≤k<n1\le k<n1≤k<n, the Hill estimate of the extreme-value index is

ξ^k,n=1k∑j=1klog⁡(xn−j+1:nxn−k:n).\widehat\xi_{k,n} = \frac1k\sum_{j=1}^{k} \log\left(\frac{x_{n-j+1:n}}{x_{n-k:n}}\right).ξ​k,n​=k1​j=1∑k​log(xn−k:n​xn−j+1:n​​).
(2)

For a Pareto-type right tail with exponent α\alphaα, the corresponding tail exponent estimate is

α^k,n=1ξ^k,n.\widehat\alpha_{k,n}=\frac{1}{\widehat\xi_{k,n}}.αk,n​=ξ​k,n​1​.
(3)

The tuning parameter kkk is the number of upper order statistics used. A Hill stability plot graphs ξ^k,n\widehat\xi_{k,n}ξ​k,n​ or α^k,n\widehat\alpha_{k,n}αk,n​ over a range of kkk values. On this page, we name the plotted coordinate explicitly: ξ\xiξ is the extreme-value index, while α=1/ξ\alpha=1/\xiα=1/ξ is the Pareto tail exponent.

Here xi:nx_{i:n}xi:n​ is the iiith ascending observation, and kkk selects the top kkk values above the order-statistic threshold. If all selected values equal that threshold, ξ^=0\widehat\xi=0ξ​=0 and there is no finite reciprocal estimate. Ties can also make the strict exceedance count smaller than kkk.

When to use it¶

Hill is a right-tail estimator for strictly positive observations in a Pareto-type regime, meaning the survival tail is expected to be regularly varying. It is appropriate only after deciding what data transformation makes the tail positive and one-sided.

Small kkk means the threshold is very high, so the estimate uses only the most extreme observations and has high variance. Large kkk lowers the threshold, which adds data but can mix non-tail observations into the calculation. A stable region is a practical compromise between those two failures.

Report a range of kkk values, the corresponding thresholds, and the sensitivity of the estimated exponent. A plateau can guide further checks, but it does not establish regular variation or independence.

Exact Pareto calibration¶

For a Pareto Type I variable,

P(X>x)=(xmx)α,x≥xm.\mathbb P(X>x)=\left(\frac{x_m}{x}\right)^\alpha,\qquad x\ge x_m.P(X>x)=(xxm​​)α,x≥xm​.
(4)

For exact Pareto samples this explains why ξ^k,n\widehat\xi_{k,n}ξ​k,n​ targets 1/α1/\alpha1/α. For a positive iid sample whose right tail is regularly varying with extreme-value index ξ>0\xi>0ξ>0, the standard consistency result uses an intermediate sequence k=knk=k_nk=kn​ with

kn→∞,knn→0.k_n\to\infty,\qquad \frac{k_n}{n}\to0.kn​→∞,nkn​​→0.
(7)

Under these assumptions, ξ^kn,n\widehat\xi_{k_n,n}ξ​kn​,n​ converges in probability to ξ\xiξ. The proof of that full theorem belongs to extreme-value asymptotics; we use the exact Pareto calibration and cite the general result in Resnick 2007. Hill’s original estimator is described in his 1975 paper, pp. 1163–1174.

Stability diagnostics¶

A stability diagnostic repeats the same calculation over kkk and reports both the threshold and the chosen tail coordinate. The arithmetic is visible even in a fixed four-observation example.

Take the fixed ordered observations (1,2,4,8)(1,2,4,8)(1,2,4,8). The Hill calculation gives:

kkkThreshold xn−k:nx_{n-k:n}xn−k:n​ξ^k,n\widehat\xi_{k,n}ξ​k,n​α^k,n\widehat\alpha_{k,n}αk,n​
14log⁡2\log 2log21/log⁡21/\log 21/log2
223log⁡2/23\log 2/23log2/22/(3log⁡2)2/(3\log 2)2/(3log2)
312log⁡22\log 22log21/(2log⁡2)1/(2\log 2)1/(2log2)

This small deterministic example shows how the selected threshold changes the estimate. It is an illustration of the arithmetic, with no claim that the four values are a random Pareto sample.

In a distribution with a bounded body and a Pareto tail, increasing kkk far enough pulls body observations into the calculation. For empirical data, a plateau is evidence to inspect, not a certificate.

Failure modes¶

  • Hill is a right-tail estimator for strictly positive data: every realized input must satisfy xi>0x_i>0xi​>0. Transform or split two-sided data before using it.

  • The estimator is threshold-sensitive. A reported α^\widehat\alphaα should include the selected kkk, the threshold, and a stability plot.

  • A plateau is suggestive, not a proof of a Pareto tail. Dependence, mixtures, truncation, and measurement limits can all manufacture or destroy apparent stability.

  • Estimating α\alphaα and plugging it into moments is dangerous near moment boundaries. Small estimation error around α=1\alpha=1α=1 or α=2\alpha=2α=2 can change whether a mean or variance is treated as finite.

  • The reciprocal α^=1/ξ^\widehat\alpha=1/\widehat\xiα=1/ξ​ is unstable when ξ^\widehat\xiξ​ is close to zero. Thin-tail regimes need different tools.
