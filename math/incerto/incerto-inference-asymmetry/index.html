Finite-sample evidence and tail non-identification

A sample can give an accurate count of what happened while leaving the size of an unobserved loss almost unconstrained. We make this distinction precise with two laws whose samples usually coincide but whose means can be far apart. The exceedance note explains why estimating a fixed event probability does not require a moment of the underlying variable.

Use the shared notation: PPP and QQQ are probability laws, X1,…,XnX_1,\ldots,X_nX1​,…,Xn​ are observations, and α\alphaα is a positive Pareto exponent. All draws below are iid under the chosen law.

A finite-sample indistinguishability bound¶

We prove the sample-law bound and its consequence for bounded tests.

Theorem 1 (Finite-sample indistinguishability under contamination)

Let PPP and RRR be probability laws on the same measurable space and let Q=(1−ε)P+εRQ=(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon RQ=(1−ε)P+εR, with 0<ε<10<\varepsilon<10<ε<1. Define total variation by

dTV(P,Q)=sup⁡A∣P(A)−Q(A)∣,d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|,dTV​(P,Q)=Asup​∣P(A)−Q(A)∣,
(1)

where the supremum is over measurable events. For every integer n≥1n\ge1n≥1, set d=1−(1−ε)nd=1-(1-\varepsilon)^nd=1−(1−ε)n. Then

dTV(P⊗n,Q⊗n)≤1−(1−ε)n≤nε.d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P^{\otimes n},Q^{\otimes n}) \le 1-(1-\varepsilon)^n\le n\varepsilon.dTV​(P⊗n,Q⊗n)≤1−(1−ε)n≤nε.
(2)

Every measurable test φ\varphiφ on the nnn-fold sample space with values in [0,1][0,1][0,1] satisfies

∣EQ⊗nφ−EP⊗nφ∣≤d.\left|\mathbb E_{Q^{\otimes n}}\varphi- \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\varphi\right|\le d.∣EQ⊗n​φ−EP⊗n​φ∣≤d.
(3)

In particular, for δ∈[0,1]\delta\in[0,1]δ∈[0,1], rejection probability at most δ\deltaδ under P⊗nP^{\otimes n}P⊗n implies power at most δ+d\delta+dδ+d under Q⊗nQ^{\otimes n}Q⊗n.

Proof 1

To generate a QQQ sample, independently choose the RRR component with probability ε\varepsilonε at each draw. With probability (1−ε)n(1-\varepsilon)^n(1−ε)n, no such choice occurs and the conditional sample law is exactly P⊗nP^{\otimes n}P⊗n. Thus Q⊗n=(1−d)P⊗n+dHQ^{\otimes n}=(1-d)P^{\otimes n}+dHQ⊗n=(1−d)P⊗n+dH for some probability law HHH, where d=1−(1−ε)nd=1-(1-\varepsilon)^nd=1−(1−ε)n. For any event AAA, the probability difference is d∣H(A)−P⊗n(A)∣≤dd|H(A)-P^{\otimes n}(A)|\le dd∣H(A)−P⊗n(A)∣≤d. The union bound on the nnn component choices gives d≤nεd\le n\varepsilond≤nε. The same mixture identity proves the test bound, because both expectations under HHH and P⊗nP^{\otimes n}P⊗n lie in [0,1][0,1][0,1]. A test with rejection probability at most δ\deltaδ under P⊗nP^{\otimes n}P⊗n therefore has power at most δ+d\delta+dδ+d under Q⊗nQ^{\otimes n}Q⊗n.

Here δ\deltaδ is a test level, not the tail exponent.

A bounded law and a Pareto contamination¶

The bound is sharp for disjoint supports and permits arbitrarily large mean gaps in the following family.

What remains identifiable under additional bounds¶

The one-draw mixture also bounds the effect on a bounded payoff.

Corollary 2 (Bounded-payoff sensitivity to contamination)

Let P,RP,RP,R be probability laws on the same measurable space, Q=(1−ε)P+εRQ=(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon RQ=(1−ε)P+εR, and 0<ε<10<\varepsilon<10<ε<1. If a measurable payoff satisfies 0≤g≤B0\le g\le B0≤g≤B with 0≤B<∞0\le B<\infty0≤B<∞, then

∣EQg(X)−EPg(X)∣≤εB.|\mathbb E_Q g(X)-\mathbb E_P g(X)|\le\varepsilon B.∣EQ​g(X)−EP​g(X)∣≤εB.
(5)

If instead ∣g∣≤B|g|\le B∣g∣≤B, the bound is 2εB2\varepsilon B2εB.

Proof 3

The mixture identity used in Theorem 1 gives EQg−EPg=ε(ERg−EPg)\mathbb E_Qg-\mathbb E_Pg=\varepsilon(\mathbb E_Rg-\mathbb E_Pg)EQ​g−EP​g=ε(ER​g−EP​g). The expectations lie in [0,B][0,B][0,B] in the first case and [−B,B][-B,B][−B,B] in the second.

A common payoff bound therefore changes the problem; see asymmetric payoffs. A known uniform integrability condition or a restricted parametric family can also rule out the alternatives used here. Such information is an assumption to justify.

An observation outside [0,1][0,1][0,1] rejects this particular bounded PPP exactly. For a nondegenerate Gaussian, every finite upper-tail threshold has positive exceedance probability. A large value therefore supports a statistical comparison, not a logical contradiction. No finite observation alone rejects the class of all bounded laws with unknown bounds.

Worked calculation¶

Use repeated_event_probability, finite_payoff_mean.

from xmath.incerto.lecture_risk import repeated_event_probability, finite_payoff_mean

epsilon, alpha, cutoff = 1e-4, 1.5, 1e6
tail_mean = alpha * cutoff / (alpha - 1)
mean = finite_payoff_mean([1 - epsilon, epsilon], [0.5, tail_mean])
print(f"Mixture mean: {mean:.4g}")
for n in (10, 1000, 10000):
    print(f"{n:d} {repeated_event_probability(epsilon, n):.3e}")
