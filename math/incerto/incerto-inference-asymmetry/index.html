Finite-sample evidence and tail non-identification

A sample can give an accurate count of what happened while leaving the size of an unobserved loss almost unconstrained. We make this distinction precise with two laws whose samples usually coincide but whose means can be far apart. The exceedance note explains why estimating a fixed event probability does not require a moment of the underlying variable.

Use the shared notation: PPP and QQQ are probability laws, X1,…,XnX_1,\ldots,X_nX1​,…,Xn​ are observations, and α\alphaα is a positive Pareto exponent. All draws below are iid under the chosen law.

A finite-sample indistinguishability bound¶

Let PPP be any probability law and let Q=(1−ε)P+εRQ=(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon RQ=(1−ε)P+εR, where RRR is another law and 0<ε<10<\varepsilon<10<ε<1. Define total variation by

dTV(P,Q)=sup⁡A∣P(A)−Q(A)∣,d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|,dTV​(P,Q)=Asup​∣P(A)−Q(A)∣,
(1)

where the supremum is over measurable events. For every integer n≥1n\ge1n≥1,

dTV(P⊗n,Q⊗n)≤1−(1−ε)n≤nε.d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P^{\otimes n},Q^{\otimes n}) \le 1-(1-\varepsilon)^n\le n\varepsilon.dTV​(P⊗n,Q⊗n)≤1−(1−ε)n≤nε.
(2)

We prove this bound and its consequence for tests. To generate a QQQ sample, independently choose the RRR component with probability ε\varepsilonε at each draw. With probability (1−ε)n(1-\varepsilon)^n(1−ε)n, no such choice occurs and the conditional sample law is exactly P⊗nP^{\otimes n}P⊗n. Thus Q⊗n=(1−d)P⊗n+dHQ^{\otimes n}=(1-d)P^{\otimes n}+dHQ⊗n=(1−d)P⊗n+dH for some probability law HHH, where d=1−(1−ε)nd=1-(1-\varepsilon)^nd=1−(1−ε)n. For any event AAA, the probability difference is d∣H(A)−P⊗n(A)∣≤dd|H(A)-P^{\otimes n}(A)|\le dd∣H(A)−P⊗n(A)∣≤d. The union bound on the nnn component choices gives d≤nεd\le n\varepsilond≤nε.

More generally, a measurable test φ\varphiφ taking values in [0,1][0,1][0,1] obeys

∣EQ⊗nφ−EP⊗nφ∣≤d.\left|\mathbb E_{Q^{\otimes n}}\varphi- \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\varphi\right|\le d.∣EQ⊗n​φ−EP⊗n​φ∣≤d.
(3)

The same mixture identity proves this, because both expectations under HHH and P⊗nP^{\otimes n}P⊗n lie in [0,1][0,1][0,1]. A test with rejection probability at most δ\deltaδ under PPP therefore has power at most δ+d\delta+dδ+d against this QQQ. Here δ\deltaδ is a test level, not the tail exponent.

A bounded law and a Pareto contamination¶

Take PPP uniform on [0,1][0,1][0,1] and RRR an exact Pareto law with minimum xm=b>1x_m=b>1xm​=b>1 and exponent α>1\alpha>1α>1. Their supports are disjoint. These follow by mixing the two survivals and the two finite means. For 1<α≤21<\alpha\le21<α≤2, QQQ has infinite second moment by the Pareto moment calculation, although PPP is bounded. For any fixed nnn, any 0<η<10<\eta<10<η<1, and any desired positive mean gap, choose 0<ε≤η/n0<\varepsilon\le\eta/n0<ε≤η/n, then increase bbb until the gap is exceeded. Sample total variation stays at most η\etaη. This proves that a small sample-law discrepancy need not control an unbounded mean functional over this unrestricted family.

The order of the quantifiers matters. For each fixed ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0, the detection probability tends to one as n→∞n\to\inftyn→∞. The construction does not say that a fixed alternative can remain hidden indefinitely.

Conditional on that event, the entire observed vector has the baseline law. What remains identifiable under additional bounds¶

If a measurable payoff satisfies 0≤g(x)≤B0\le g(x)\le B0≤g(x)≤B with finite BBB, the one-draw mixture identity gives

∣EQg(X)−EPg(X)∣≤εB.|\mathbb E_Q g(X)-\mathbb E_P g(X)|\le\varepsilon B.∣EQ​g(X)−EP​g(X)∣≤εB.
(5)

For ∣g∣≤B|g|\le B∣g∣≤B, replace the right side by 2εB2\varepsilon B2εB. A common payoff bound therefore changes the problem; see asymmetric payoffs. A known uniform integrability condition or a restricted parametric family can also rule out the alternatives used here. Such information is an assumption to justify.

An observation outside [0,1][0,1][0,1] rejects this particular bounded PPP exactly. For a nondegenerate Gaussian, every finite upper-tail threshold has positive exceedance probability. A large value therefore supports a statistical comparison, not a logical contradiction. No finite observation alone rejects the class of all bounded laws with unknown bounds.

Worked calculation¶

Use repeated_event_probability, finite_payoff_mean.

from xmath.incerto.lecture_risk import repeated_event_probability, finite_payoff_mean

epsilon, alpha, cutoff = 1e-4, 1.5, 1e6
tail_mean = alpha * cutoff / (alpha - 1)
mean = finite_payoff_mean([1 - epsilon, epsilon], [0.5, tail_mean])
print(f"Mixture mean: {mean:.4g}")
for n in (10, 1000, 10000):
    print(f"{n:d} {repeated_event_probability(epsilon, n):.3e}")

Output

10 9.996e-04
1000 9.517e-02
10000 6.321e-01
