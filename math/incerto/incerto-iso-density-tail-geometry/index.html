Iso-Density Tail Geometry

Independent coordinates¶

Iso-density tail geometry studies the level sets of the iid joint density

fX1,X2(x1,x2)=f(x1)f(x2)f_{X_1,X_2}(x_1,x_2)=f(x_1)f(x_2)fX1​,X2​​(x1​,x2​)=f(x1​)f(x2​)
(1)

near a large-deviation constraint such as x1+x2=sx_1+x_2=sx1​+x2​=s. The diagnostic asks whether the most likely configurations along the constraint sit near the diagonal, where both coordinates are moderately large, or near the axes, where one coordinate is large and the other is ordinary.

Here X1,X2X_1,X_2X1​,X2​ are independent copies of a continuous random variable with density fff, and x1,x2x_1,x_2x1​,x2​ are its coordinate values. The sum level sss is fixed for each comparison. See Shared notation.

Normal versus Cauchy geometry¶

A large Gaussian sum is geometrically a many-moderate-deviation event.

Along a large-sum line, the Cauchy geometry increasingly favors one large coordinate rather than two equal coordinates. This is the density-contour version of the one-big-jump intuition in Subexponentiality and the Survival Tail Ratio.

The axial points are comparison points, not the exact Cauchy maximizers for finite positive sss. We locate the maximizers next.

This algebra describes density along a constraint of probability zero. It does not prove a tail probability asymptotic. The Cauchy example is two-sided; applying the nonnegative-summand subexponential theorem to it requires a separate right-tail argument.

Switching correlation can destroy ellipticality¶

The following mixture has normal marginals without elliptical joint geometry.

Proposition 4 (Opposite-correlation normal mixture is not elliptical)

Let (X1,X2)(X_1,X_2)(X1​,X2​) be an equal mixture of centered bivariate normal laws with unit marginal variances and correlations ρ\rhoρ and −ρ-\rho−ρ, where 0<ρ<10<\rho<10<ρ<1. Both marginals are standard normal and the covariance is I2I_2I2​. The characteristic function is

φ(t1,t2)=e−(t12+t22)/2cosh⁡(ρt1t2).\varphi(t_1,t_2)=e^{-(t_1^2+t_2^2)/2}\cosh(\rho t_1t_2).φ(t1​,t2​)=e−(t12​+t22​)/2cosh(ρt1​t2​).
(13)

This joint law is not elliptical.

Proof 4

Both component marginals are standard normal. Averaging their covariance matrices gives I2I_2I2​, since both means vanish. Averaging their characteristic functions gives the displayed formula. For a nondegenerate elliptical law with finite covariance, covariance is a positive scalar multiple of scatter: whitening gives a rotationally invariant law, whose covariance commutes with every orthogonal matrix and hence is scalar. Thus an elliptical law with covariance I2I_2I2​ must be spherical. But the displayed characteristic function at (v,0)(v,0)(v,0) is e−v2/2e^{-v^2/2}e−v2/2, whereas at (v/2,v/2)(v/\sqrt2,v/\sqrt2)(v/2​,v/2​) it is e−v2/2cosh⁡(ρv2/2)e^{-v^2/2}\cosh(\rho v^2/2)e−v2/2cosh(ρv2/2). For v≠0v\ne0v=0 these differ at the same radius. This disproves sphericality and hence ellipticality. Both marginal laws remain standard normal.

The iid Cauchy vector supplies a separate stable, nonelliptical example.

Proposition 5 (Independent Cauchy coordinates are stable but not elliptical)

Let X1,X2X_1,X_2X1​,X2​ be independent standard Cauchy variables. Their joint characteristic function is e−∣t1∣−∣t2∣e^{-|t_1|-|t_2|}e−∣t1​∣−∣t2​∣. For every a,b∈Ra,b\in\mathbb Ra,b∈R not both zero, aX1+bX2aX_1+bX_2aX1​+bX2​ is centered Cauchy with scale ∣a∣+∣b∣|a|+|b|∣a∣+∣b∣. The vector is stable but is not elliptical.

Proof 5

Independence multiplies the marginal characteristic functions. Substituting (t1,t2)=(at,bt)(t_1,t_2)=(at,bt)(t1​,t2​)=(at,bt) gives e−(∣a∣+∣b∣)∣t∣e^{-(|a|+|b|)|t|}e−(∣a∣+∣b∣)∣t∣, the claimed Cauchy law. For independent copies of the vector, the same multiplication shows that a linear combination with scalar coefficients a,ba,ba,b has the law of (∣a∣+∣b∣)(X1,X2)(|a|+|b|)(X_1,X_2)(∣a∣+∣b∣)(X1​,X2​), establishing stability. To disprove ellipticality without moments, its characteristic function on either axis would force the elliptical profile to be exponential in the square root of its argument. It is also unchanged when one coordinate changes sign, forcing off-diagonal scatter zero. The equal marginal scales would then force circular level sets; ∣t1∣+∣t2∣|t_1|+|t_2|∣t1​∣+∣t2​∣ instead has diamond level sets.

Thus closure under linear combinations does not require ellipticality.

Use bivariate_student.

import numpy as np
from xmath.incerto.multivariate import bivariate_student

rng = np.random.default_rng(20261020)
sample = bivariate_student(rng, nu=1, rho=0, size=10000)
radius = np.linalg.norm(sample, axis=1)
angle = np.arctan2(sample[:, 1], sample[:, 0])
sector = (radius > 3) & (angle >= 0) & (angle <= np.pi / 3)
print(f"Sector frequency: {sector.mean():.4g}")
print(f"Exact probability: {1 / (6 * np.sqrt(10)):.4g}")

Caveats¶

  • Iso-density geometry requires a density. Discrete, singular, censored, or heavily rounded data need a different diagnostic.

  • Density at a point is not probability mass. The contour picture explains local geometry; probability statements require integration over regions.

  • The iid product-density formula fails under dependence. Volatility clustering, common factors, and contagion can rotate or bend the contours.

  • The examples here are symmetric and centered. Skewed losses or one-sided positive variables should first be put into a problem-specific coordinate system.
