Maximum entropy and power tails

An entropy maximizer answers a constrained optimization question. Specifying only “maximum entropy” leaves that question incomplete: the support, measure, and constraints determine the answer. Here all densities are with respect to Lebesgue measure in the stated coordinate, with natural logarithms and fixed measurement units.

Appendix B of Taleb (2025), printed pp. 127–133, motivates the examples. We prove the Shannon optima below by a relative-entropy bound. We then derive the normalization and moments of a q-exponential family and distinguish them from a global optimization theorem under escort constraints.

A certificate for a Shannon maximum¶

For a density fff on a fixed support DDD, its differential entropy is h(f)=−∫Df(x)log⁡f(x) dxh(f)=-\int_D f(x)\log f(x)\,dxh(f)=−∫D​f(x)logf(x)dx, when defined. Consider a candidate

(1)

This proves a global maximum, once a feasible candidate exists. It does not assert existence for arbitrary moment constraints.

Uniform and Gaussian constraints¶

On [a,b][a,b][a,b] with finite a<ba<ba<b, normalization alone gives g=1/(b−a)g=1/(b-a)g=1/(b−a) and h(g)=log⁡(b−a)h(g)=\log(b-a)h(g)=log(b−a). Set xm=1x_m=1xm​=1, m=1/2m=1/2m=1/2. In the coordinate Z=log⁡XZ=\log XZ=logX, the maximizer is exponential with rate 2. A competitor with the same mean is gamma with shape 2 and rate 4, density 16ze−4z16z e^{-4z}16ze−4z on z>0z>0z>0. Transforming back gives a log-gamma density 16log⁡(x)x−516\log(x)x^{-5}16log(x)x−5 for x≥1x\ge1x≥1. Numerical integration of the log-coordinate entropy plus the Jacobian correction mmm gives entropies approximately 0.806853 and 0.690921. Both calculations integrate to infinity; plotting endpoints do not truncate either model.
