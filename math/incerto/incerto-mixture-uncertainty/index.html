Variance mixtures and uncertain scale

A Gaussian conditional model need not give a Gaussian marginal law. Let Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim N(0,1)Z∼N(0,1) and let Y>0Y>0Y>0 be independent of ZZZ. We study

X=YZ,X∣Y=y∼N(0,y).X=\sqrt Y Z,\qquad X\mid Y=y\sim N(0,y).X=Y​Z,X∣Y=y∼N(0,y).
(1)

Here YYY is variance, so Y\sqrt YY​ is conditional standard deviation. This is the centered scalar case of the Normix normal-mixture construction. We use its shared mixing symbol YYY and the notation canon. A one-observation mixture specifies no temporal dependence: volatility clustering requires a model for a sequence of mixing variables.

Two states, finite moments¶

Choose equal probabilities for Y=1−aY=1-aY=1−a and Y=1+aY=1+aY=1+a, with 0≤a<10\le a<10≤a<1. Conditioning gives variance 1 and fourth moment 3(1+a2)3(1+a^2)3(1+a2). At a=0.6a=0.6a=0.6 these are 1 and 4.08. The density is the average of two Gaussian densities. Its moment generating function (MGF) and characteristic function are

MX(t)=12e(1−a)t2/2+12e(1+a)t2/2,φX(t)=12e−(1−a)t2/2+12e−(1+a)t2/2.M_X(t)=\tfrac12e^{(1-a)t^2/2}+\tfrac12e^{(1+a)t^2/2},\qquad \varphi_X(t)=\tfrac12e^{-(1-a)t^2/2}+\tfrac12e^{-(1+a)t^2/2}.MX​(t)=21​e(1−a)t2/2+21​e(1+a)t2/2,φX​(t)=21​e−(1−a)t2/2+21​e−(1+a)t2/2.
(4)

They follow from the two conditional Gaussian transforms. They are different functions, even though the mean is zero. For a>0a>0a>0, division by the unit-normal density ϕ(x)\phi(x)ϕ(x) gives

fa(x)ϕ(x)∼121+aexp⁡(ax22(1+a)),∣x∣→∞.\frac{f_a(x)}{\phi(x)} \sim \frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+a}} \exp\left(\frac{ax^2}{2(1+a)}\right),\qquad |x|\to\infty.ϕ(x)fa​(x)​∼21+a​1​exp(2(1+a)ax2​),∣x∣→∞.
(5)

The narrower component’s ratio tends to zero; the wider component’s ratio increases without bound. Nevertheless MX(t)M_X(t)MX​(t) is finite for every real ttt. The mixture has more far-tail density than the unit normal and still satisfies the Cramér condition. It is not a power law. At a=1a=1a=1 the lower state is an atom at zero and the full law no longer has a Lebesgue density.

Unequal probabilities remove the equal-weight example’s kurtosis ceiling. For a different worked case, take

P(Y=0.5)=0.99,P(Y=50.5)=0.01.P(Y=0.5)=0.99,\qquad P(Y=50.5)=0.01.P(Y=0.5)=0.99,P(Y=50.5)=0.01.
(6)

Then EY=1\mathbb EY=1EY=1, EY2=25.75\mathbb EY^2=25.75EY2=25.75 and kurtosis is 77.25. All moments and the MGF remain finite. More generally, fixing 0<c<10<c<10<c<1 and putting probability 0<p<10<p<10<p<1 on b=[1−(1−p)c]/pb=[1-(1-p)c]/pb=[1−(1−p)c]/p and 1−p1-p1−p on ccc preserves EY=1\mathbb EY=1EY=1, while EY2\mathbb EY^2EY2 diverges as p↓0p\downarrow0p↓0. Each fixed p>0p>0p>0 still gives a finite Gaussian mixture. A rare state can make a finite moment hard to estimate without making it infinite.

Variance and precision give different laws¶

Three explicit constructions show why the distribution of the mixing variable matters. All parameters below are local to this section.

Gamma variance. Let YYY have gamma shape k>0k>0k>0 and scale 1/k1/k1/k, so EY=1\mathbb EY=1EY=1. Integrating its density gives

EYr=k−rΓ(k+r)Γ(k),r>−k,MX(t)=(1−t22k)−k,∣t∣<2k.\mathbb EY^r=k^{-r}\frac{\Gamma(k+r)}{\Gamma(k)},\quad r>-k, \qquad M_X(t)=\left(1-\frac{t^2}{2k}\right)^{-k},\quad |t|<\sqrt{2k}.EYr=k−rΓ(k)Γ(k+r)​,r>−k,MX​(t)=(1−2kt2​)−k,∣t∣<2k​.
(7)

For the MGF, condition on YYY and integrate et2Y/2e^{t^2Y/2}et2Y/2 against the gamma density. The integral diverges at and beyond the endpoints. All positive power moments exist, and kurtosis is 3(1+1/k)3(1+1/k)3(1+1/k). At k=1k=1k=1, the MGF is that of a centered Laplace law with scale 1/21/\sqrt21/2​; this law has exponential tails. The density calculation below gives the general gamma-mixture decay.

Lognormal variance. Let Y=exp⁡(τU−τ2/2)Y=\exp(\tau U-\tau^2/2)Y=exp(τU−τ2/2), where UUU is standard normal, independent of ZZZ, and τ>0\tau>0τ>0. Completing the square gives

EYr=e(r2−r)τ2/2,E∣X∣q=cqeq(q−2)τ2/8.\mathbb EY^r=e^{(r^2-r)\tau^2/2},\qquad \mathbb E|X|^q=c_q e^{q(q-2)\tau^2/8}.EYr=e(r2−r)τ2/2,E∣X∣q=cq​eq(q−2)τ2/8.
(8)

Every positive power moment exists, yet MX(t)=Eet2Y/2=∞M_X(t)=\mathbb E e^{t^2Y/2}=\inftyMX​(t)=Eet2Y/2=∞ for every t≠0t\ne0t=0. To see divergence, write the expectation as an integral over uuu; its exponent contains a positive multiple of eτue^{\tau u}eτu minus u2/2u^2/2u2/2, and is eventually positive on an infinite half-line. Finite moments of every order therefore do not imply a finite MGF. Nor does this construction have a finite power-tail exponent: a regularly varying survival with finite positive exponent would force sufficiently high moments to diverge.

Gamma precision. Let Q=1/YQ=1/YQ=1/Y have gamma shape ν/2\nu/2ν/2 and rate ν/2\nu/2ν/2, with ν>0\nu>0ν>0. Integrating the conditional density gives

fX(x)=Γ((ν+1)/2)νπΓ(ν/2)(1+x2ν)−(ν+1)/2.f_X(x)=\frac{\Gamma((\nu+1)/2)}{\sqrt{\nu\pi}\Gamma(\nu/2)} \left(1+\frac{x^2}{\nu}\right)^{-(\nu+1)/2}.fX​(x)=νπ​Γ(ν/2)Γ((ν+1)/2)​(1+νx2​)−(ν+1)/2.
(9)

Indeed the integrand is proportional to q(ν+1)/2−1e−(ν+x2)q/2q^{(\nu+1)/2-1}e^{-(\nu+x^2)q/2}q(ν+1)/2−1e−(ν+x2)q/2, a gamma integral. This is Student’s ttt law at unit scale. Its density is asymptotic to a positive constant times ∣x∣−ν−1|x|^{-\nu-1}∣x∣−ν−1; integrating that comparison gives a survival exponent α=ν\alpha=\nuα=ν. The moment identity above reduces to an integral of q(ν−q0)/2−1q^{(\nu-q_0)/2-1}q(ν−q0​)/2−1 near zero for moment order q0>0q_0>0q0​>0, so E∣X∣q0<∞\mathbb E|X|^{q_0}<\inftyE∣X∣q0​<∞ exactly when q0<νq_0<\nuq0​<ν. For ν>2\nu>2ν>2 its variance is ν/(ν−2)\nu/(\nu-2)ν/(ν−2), not 1.

These are proved model calculations. SCoFT v4 §4.2, pp. 74–75 supplies the variance-versus-precision question; it is not a claim that every stochastic volatility model produces a power law. Recursive uncertainty belongs to a later treatment and requires a specified limiting construction.

Reproduce the worked cases¶

Use normal_mixture_absolute_moment, normal_mixture_log_abs_survival.

import numpy as np
from xmath.incerto.finite_moments import (
    normal_mixture_absolute_moment, normal_mixture_log_abs_survival,
)

for variances, weights in (([1], [1]), ([0.5, 50.5], [0.99, 0.01])):
    m2 = normal_mixture_absolute_moment(2, variances, weights)
    m4 = normal_mixture_absolute_moment(4, variances, weights)
    tail = np.exp(normal_mixture_log_abs_survival(4, variances, weights))
    print(f"Variance / kurtosis / P(|X|>4): {m2:.4g} {m4 / m2**2:.4g} {tail:.4g}")

Output

Variance / kurtosis / P(|X|>4): 1 3 6.334e-05
Variance / kurtosis / P(|X|>4): 1 77.25 0.005735

All moments of these two finite Gaussian mixtures exist.

Variance–mean gamma calculation¶

The following extension permits a moving conditional mean.

(10)
