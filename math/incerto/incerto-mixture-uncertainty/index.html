Variance mixtures and uncertain scale

A Gaussian conditional model need not give a Gaussian marginal law. Let Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim N(0,1)Z∼N(0,1) and let Y>0Y>0Y>0 be independent of ZZZ. We study

X=YZ,X∣Y=y∼N(0,y).\begin{gathered} X=\sqrt Y Z,\\ X\mid Y=y\sim N(0,y). \end{gathered}X=Y​Z,X∣Y=y∼N(0,y).​
(1)

Here YYY is variance, so Y\sqrt YY​ is conditional standard deviation. This is the centered scalar case of the Normix normal-mixture construction. We use its shared mixing symbol YYY and the notation canon. A one-observation mixture specifies no temporal dependence: volatility clustering requires a model for a sequence of mixing variables.

Which moments does mixing preserve?¶

We first transfer moment questions to the mixing variable.

Proposition 1 (Absolute moments of a centered normal variance mixture)

Let Y>0Y>0Y>0 be finite almost surely, independent of Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim N(0,1)Z∼N(0,1), and put X=YZX=\sqrt YZX=Y​Z. For every real q>0q>0q>0, the following absolute-moment identity holds, allowing infinite expectations:

If 0<EY<∞0<\mathbb EY<\infty0<EY<∞, then E∣X∣<∞\mathbb E|X|<\inftyE∣X∣<∞, EX=0\mathbb EX=0EX=0 and Var⁡(X)=EY\operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb EYVar(X)=EY. If also EY2<∞\mathbb EY^2<\inftyEY2<∞, its Pearson kurtosis is

Proof 1

The factorization follows from independence and Tonelli’s theorem applied to the nonnegative product Yq/2∣Z∣qY^{q/2}|Z|^qYq/2∣Z∣q. In the normal integral for E∣Z∣q\mathbb E|Z|^qE∣Z∣q, substituting z2/2z^2/2z2/2 as the integration variable gives the displayed gamma integral. The Gaussian factor is positive and finite, so the moment exists exactly when the mixing moment exists. For EY<∞\mathbb EY<\inftyEY<∞, Cauchy–Schwarz makes XXX integrable, and symmetry gives EX=0\mathbb EX=0EX=0. The cases q=2,4q=2,4q=2,4 give the variance and fourth moment. Symmetry alone does not make an odd signed moment exist; absolute integrability is still required. The Gaussian reference is 3. For fixed mean variance, nonconstant YYY raises kurtosis. This comparison is specific to centered normal variance mixtures; it is not an ordering of arbitrary distributions by tail risk.

Two states, finite moments¶

Proposition 2 (Two-state Gaussian variance mixtures)

Let 0≤a<10\le a<10≤a<1, let YYY take values 1−a1-a1−a and 1+a1+a1+a with equal probabilities, and let Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim N(0,1)Z∼N(0,1) be independent. Proof 2

The cases q=2,4q=2,4q=2,4 of Proposition 1 give the moments. Conditioning on the two values of YYY gives the density and both transforms. The transform is finite for every real ttt, and each component has all positive power moments.

At a=0.6a=0.6a=0.6, the variance and fourth moment are 1 and 4.08. The MGF and characteristic function are different functions even though the mean is zero. This mixture has more far-tail density than the unit normal and still satisfies the Cramér condition. It is not a power law. At a=1a=1a=1 the lower state is an atom at zero, and the full law no longer has a Lebesgue density.

Unequal probabilities remove the equal-weight example’s kurtosis ceiling.

Proposition 3 (Unbounded kurtosis across finite mixtures)

Fix 0<c<10<c<10<c<1. For 0<p<10<p<10<p<1, let YYY equal ccc with probability 1−p1-p1−p and [1−(1−p)c]/p[1-(1-p)c]/p[1−(1−p)c]/p with probability ppp. With independent Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim N(0,1)Z∼N(0,1), put X=YZX=\sqrt YZX=Y​Z. For a different worked case, take

P(Y=0.5)=0.99,P(Y=50.5)=0.01.\begin{gathered} P(Y=0.5)=0.99,\\ P(Y=50.5)=0.01. \end{gathered}P(Y=0.5)=0.99,P(Y=50.5)=0.01.​

A rare state can make a finite moment hard to estimate without making it infinite.

Variance and precision give different laws¶

Three explicit constructions show why the distribution of the mixing variable matters. All parameters below are local to this section.

Proposition 4 (Gamma mixing of variance)

Let YYY have gamma shape k>0k>0k>0 and scale 1/k1/k1/k, and let Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim N(0,1)Z∼N(0,1) be independent. For X=YZX=\sqrt YZX=Y​Z,

EYr=k−rΓ(k+r)Γ(k),r>−k,MX(t)=(1−t22k)−k,∣t∣<2k.\begin{gathered} \mathbb EY^r=k^{-r}\frac{\Gamma(k+r)}{\Gamma(k)},\\ r>-k,\\ M_X(t)=\left(1-\frac{t^2}{2k}\right)^{-k},\\ |t|<\sqrt{2k}. \end{gathered}EYr=k−rΓ(k)Γ(k+r)​,r>−k,MX​(t)=(1−2kt2​)−k,∣t∣<2k​.​
(6)

The MGF is infinite for ∣t∣≥2k|t|\ge\sqrt{2k}∣t∣≥2k​. All positive power moments of XXX exist, its variance is 1 and its kurtosis is 3(1+1/k)3(1+1/k)3(1+1/k). At k=1k=1k=1, XXX has a centered Laplace law with scale 1/21/\sqrt21/2​.

Proof 4

Integrating yry^ryr against kkyk−1e−ky/Γ(k)k^ky^{k-1}e^{-ky}/\Gamma(k)kkyk−1e−ky/Γ(k) gives the gamma moment formula for r>−kr>-kr>−k. Conditioning on YYY gives MX(t)=Eet2Y/2M_X(t)=\mathbb E e^{t^2Y/2}MX​(t)=Eet2Y/2; the gamma integral converges exactly when k−t2/2>0k-t^2/2>0k−t2/2>0 and evaluates to the displayed MGF. Proposition 1 gives the moment conclusions. At k=1k=1k=1, the MGF (1−t2/2)−1(1-t^2/2)^{-1}(1−t2/2)−1 identifies the stated Laplace law.

The Laplace law has exponential tails. The density calculation below gives the general gamma-mixture decay.

Proposition 5 (Lognormal mixing of variance)

Let U,ZU,ZU,Z be independent standard normals, τ>0\tau>0τ>0, Y=eτU−τ2/2Y=e^{\tau U-\tau^2/2}Y=eτU−τ2/2 and X=YZX=\sqrt YZX=Y​Z. For r∈Rr\in\mathbb Rr∈R and q>0q>0q>0,

EYr=e(r2−r)τ2/2,E∣X∣q=E∣Z∣qeq(q−2)τ2/8.\mathbb EY^r=e^{(r^2-r)\tau^2/2},\qquad \mathbb E|X|^q=\mathbb E|Z|^q e^{q(q-2)\tau^2/8}.EYr=e(r2−r)τ2/2,E∣X∣q=E∣Z∣qeq(q−2)τ2/8.
(7)

Every positive power moment of XXX is finite, but MX(t)=∞M_X(t)=\inftyMX​(t)=∞ for every t≠0t\ne0t=0. Its right survival cannot be regularly varying with a finite positive power exponent.

Proof 5

Completing the square in the normal integral gives EYr\mathbb EY^rEYr. Apply Proposition 1 with r=q/2r=q/2r=q/2. For t≠0t\ne0t=0, MX(t)=Eet2Y/2M_X(t)=\mathbb E e^{t^2Y/2}MX​(t)=Eet2Y/2. In the integral over uuu, the exponent is a positive multiple of eτue^{\tau u}eτu minus u2/2u^2/2u2/2, eventually positive on an infinite half-line, so the integral diverges. A regularly varying right survival with finite positive exponent would force sufficiently high positive moments to diverge, by the regular-variation moment test.

Finite moments of every order therefore do not imply a finite MGF.

Proposition 6 (Gamma mixing of precision gives Student tails)

Let 1/Y1/Y1/Y have gamma shape and rate both ν/2\nu/2ν/2, with ν>0\nu>0ν>0, and let Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim N(0,1)Z∼N(0,1) be independent of YYY. Then X=YZX=\sqrt YZX=Y​Z has density

fX(x)=Γ((ν+1)/2)νπΓ(ν/2)×(1+x2ν)−(ν+1)/2.\begin{aligned} f_X(x)&=\frac{\Gamma((\nu+1)/2)}{\sqrt{\nu\pi}\Gamma(\nu/2)}\\ &\quad\times\left(1+\frac{x^2}{\nu}\right)^{-(\nu+1)/2}. \end{aligned}fX​(x)​=νπ​Γ(ν/2)Γ((ν+1)/2)​×(1+νx2​)−(ν+1)/2.​

Proof 6

Integrating the conditional normal density over precision g=1/yg=1/yg=1/y gives an integrand proportional to g(ν+1)/2−1e−(ν+x2)g/2g^{(\nu+1)/2-1}e^{-(\nu+x^2)g/2}g(ν+1)/2−1e−(ν+x2)g/2, a gamma integral, and hence the displayed density. It is asymptotic to a positive constant times ∣x∣−ν−1|x|^{-\nu-1}∣x∣−ν−1; integrating gives the survival exponent. By Proposition 1, the qqqth moment reduces to an integral of g(ν−q)/2−1g^{(\nu-q)/2-1}g(ν−q)/2−1 near zero, finite exactly when q<νq<\nuq<ν. At q=2<νq=2<\nuq=2<ν, the gamma recurrence gives EY=ν/(ν−2)\mathbb EY=\nu/(\nu-2)EY=ν/(ν−2), which is the variance of XXX.

Unit scale is not unit variance.

These are proved model calculations. SCoFT v4 §4.2, pp. 74–75 supplies the variance-versus-precision question; it is not a claim that every stochastic volatility model produces a power law. Recursive uncertainty belongs to a later treatment and requires a specified limiting construction.

Reproduce the worked cases¶

Use normal_mixture_absolute_moment, normal_mixture_log_abs_survival.

import numpy as np
from xmath.incerto.finite_moments import (
    normal_mixture_absolute_moment, normal_mixture_log_abs_survival,
)

for variances, weights in (([1], [1]), ([0.5, 50.5], [0.99, 0.01])):
    m2 = normal_mixture_absolute_moment(2, variances, weights)
    m4 = normal_mixture_absolute_moment(4, variances, weights)
    tail = np.exp(normal_mixture_log_abs_survival(4, variances, weights))
    print(f"Variance / kurtosis / P(|X|>4): {m2:.4g} {m4 / m2**2:.4g} {tail:.4g}")

Output

Variance–mean gamma calculation¶

The following extension permits a moving conditional mean.

Random rates before exponentiation¶

Appendix C of Taleb (2025), §C.1, pp. 183–185, concerns transforming a growth rate. The fixed-horizon calculation shows that X=xmeTRX=x_m e^{TR}X=xm​eTR with R∼Exp(λ)R\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)R∼Exp(λ) has Pareto exponent λ/T\lambda/Tλ/T. A distribution over rates must be specified before claims about the outcome tail follow.

$$

Proof 8

Conditioning on JJJ and applying the fixed-horizon transformation gives the survival. Factor its smallest power; all other terms vanish relative to it as x→∞x\to\inftyx→∞. For rates 1 and 3 with equal weights and T=1T=1T=1, the outcome mean is infinite, although the rate has finite mean 2/32/32/3. This is a finite mixture, not an unspecified infinite hierarchy of uncertainty. A continuous rate mixture near zero needs a separate analysis.

from xmath.incerto.distributions import pareto_type1
