Tail assumptions in statistical pipelines

A risk estimator needs a target, a sampling model, and an account of what happens outside the observed range. Parametric extrapolation supplies an assumption about that range. An empirical distribution puts no mass beyond its largest observation. Neither choice settles whether its risk estimate will be useful on the next sample.

SCoFT v4 Appendix F, §§F.1–F.2, printed pp. 208–212, discusses parametric and nonparametric risk estimation. Appendix G, pp. 213–216, discusses bounded and unbounded basis functions and calibration by angles Taleb, 2025. We develop limited mathematical statements and a specified evaluation procedure. They do not imply a blanket failure of econometrics or machine learning.

Which moment does the estimator need?¶

For iid XiX_iXi​ with E[X2]<∞\mathbb E[X^2]<\inftyE[X2]<∞, the raw second-moment estimator is consistent by the strong law. If also E[X4]<∞\mathbb E[X^4]<\inftyE[X4]<∞, then

An=1n∑i=1nXi2,Var⁡(An)=Var⁡(X2)n,Var⁡(X2)=E[X4]−(E[X2])2.\begin{gathered} A_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2,\\ \operatorname{Var}(A_n) =\frac{\operatorname{Var}(X^2)}n,\\ \operatorname{Var}(X^2) =\mathbb E[X^4]-(\mathbb E[X^2])^2. \end{gathered}An​=n1​i=1∑n​Xi2​,Var(An​)=nVar(X2)​,Var(X2)=E[X4]−(E[X2])2.​
(1)

The variance formula follows by independence and expansion of the square. If the second moment is finite but the fourth is infinite, AnA_nAn​ still converges almost surely, while its sampling variance is infinite: its nonnegative square dominates X14/n2X_1^4/n^2X14​/n2. Thus consistency and finite sampling variance are different requirements. This concerns a raw second moment; estimating an unknown mean and then a central variance introduces another step. The moment-estimation note treats the broader distinction.

Moment requirements depend on the estimator and loss. A sample fraction for a fixed event averages bounded indicators. A sample mean of an unbounded loss has a different integrability requirement. Likewise a finite population covariance can coexist with difficult finite-sample covariance estimation. High sample kurtosis alone cannot decide that all methods using covariance are invalid.

What a nonparametric fit leaves unspecified¶

Let PPP be a loss distribution supported on [0,b][0,b][0,b], and let δB\delta_BδB​ put all mass at B>bB>bB>b. For 0<ε<10<\varepsilon<10<ε<1, consider

QB=(1−ε)P+εδB,sup⁡x∣FQB(x)−FP(x)∣≤ε,EQB[X]−EP[X]=ε(B−EP[X]).\begin{gathered} Q_B=(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon\delta_B,\\ \sup_x|F_{Q_B}(x)-F_P(x)|\le\varepsilon,\\ \mathbb E_{Q_B}[X]-\mathbb E_P[X]\\ =\varepsilon(B-\mathbb E_P[X]). \end{gathered}QB​=(1−ε)P+εδB​,xsup​∣FQB​​(x)−FP​(x)∣≤ε,EQB​​[X]−EP​[X]=ε(B−EP​[X]).​
(2)

The CDF bound follows by writing the mixture CDF and using that both CDFs lie in [0,1][0,1][0,1]. Linearity of expectation proves the last identity. For any fixed ε\varepsilonε, the mean difference grows without bound as BBB grows, although the CDF distance stays small. In nnn iid mixture draws the probability of no contamination is (1−ε)n(1-\varepsilon)^n(1−ε)n.

This proves a limitation without a bound on the unseen loss, not an impossibility theorem for every nonparametric estimand. Bounded-payoff error has different controls. An ordinary empirical bootstrap can resample only observed values; additional tail modeling supplies additional assumptions. See hidden tails and plug-in tail estimation.

Freeze the pipeline before the test period¶

The local S&P procedure uses the first half of chronological log returns for fitting and the second half for evaluation. Loss is the negative of the log return. We compare a Gaussian 95% cutoff, fitted with the training mean and standard deviation, with the training empirical 95th percentile. Both are frozen before the first test observation.

Report strict exceedance counts, the test sample size, and the mean loss among those exceedances. They measure frequency and severity separately. For nnn sorted training values and probability qqq, the empirical quantile selects zero-based index ⌈(n−1)q⌉\lceil(n-1)q\rceil⌈(n−1)q⌉ (higher interpolation). Ties may put more mass at the cutoff. Neither cutoff is promised to yield exactly 5% test exceedances. No binomial error bars are attached to dependent returns.

Use chronological_tail_check.

Code: a fixed training set and two test periods
import numpy as np
from xmath.incerto.diagnostics import chronological_tail_check

train = np.array([-0.03, -0.01, 0, 0.01, 0.03])
for label, test in (("quiet", [0, -0.01, 0.01]), ("stress", [-0.08, 0, -0.12])):
    result = chronological_tail_check(np.r_[train, test], train_size=5)
    for method in ("gaussian", "empirical"):
        row = result[method]
        print(f"{label} / {method}: cutoff={row['threshold']:.4f}, hits={row['exceedances']}, severity={row['mean_exceedance_loss']:.4f}")

Output

quiet / gaussian: cutoff=0.0329, hits=0, severity=nan
quiet / empirical: cutoff=0.0300, hits=0, severity=nan
stress / gaussian: cutoff=0.0329, hits=2, severity=0.1000
stress / empirical: cutoff=0.0300, hits=2, severity=0.1000

These are invented returns. The missing-severity value is exposed rather than replaced by zero. The unchanged cutoffs demonstrate the frozen fit; the stress period changes the realized hits and losses.

The S&P diagnostics pipeline applies exactly this procedure to a local authorized cache. It reports dates and uses all returns, with no training/test overlap in return observations. Its overlapping aggregation comparison is descriptive and does not enter the fit. No empirical S&P holdout result is asserted without that cache. Choosing the probability level after inspecting test results, or repeatedly trying windows and publishing only a favorable one, would invalidate the interpretation as a single prespecified comparison. Rolling retraining, dependence-aware inference, and competing conditional-volatility models would require separately specified experiments.

What finite basis expansions can extrapolate¶

A finite sum of bounded sigmoid functions with finite coefficients is bounded. Indeed, if ∣bj(x)∣≤1|b_j(x)|\le1∣bj​(x)∣≤1, then

∣∑j=1mwjbj(x)∣≤∑j=1m∣wj∣.\left|\sum_{j=1}^m w_j b_j(x)\right| \le\sum_{j=1}^m|w_j|.∣∣​j=1∑m​wj​bj​(x)∣∣​≤j=1∑m​∣wj​∣.
(3)

It cannot equal an unbounded target on the whole real line. This says nothing against approximation on a compact interval, where the target may itself be bounded. Adding an unbounded linear term also changes the class of functions.

A hinge (x−K)+=max⁡(x−K,0)(x-K)_+=\max(x-K,0)(x−K)+​=max(x−K,0) has a linear right tail. We can represent every continuous piecewise-linear function with finitely many knots exactly by an affine term and a finite sum of hinges. With no knots the function is affine. Otherwise, let K1<⋯<KmK_1<\cdots<K_mK1​<⋯<Km​, m≥1m\ge1m≥1, be the knots and s0,…,sms_0,\ldots,s_ms0​,…,sm​ the successive slopes. Define slope changes wj=sj−sj−1w_j=s_j-s_{j-1}wj​=sj​−sj−1​. Then

(4)

To prove the representation, differentiate away from the knots. On segment jjj the slope is s0+∑i=1jwi=sjs_0+\sum_{i=1}^j w_i=s_js0​+∑i=1j​wi​=sj​ by telescoping. The formula is continuous at every knot and equals f(K1)f(K_1)f(K1​) at K1K_1K1​. Those slopes and one anchor value determine the specified function.

Finite hinge sums are piecewise linear, not exact representations of arbitrary nonlinear functions. Even for this class, a chosen slope outside the observed range remains an extrapolation assumption. Linearity outside the knots can help when the target has linear tails, and fail when it does not. A function’s extrapolation and the input distribution’s tail are separate objects.

(5)

It is not the arctangent of an angle.
