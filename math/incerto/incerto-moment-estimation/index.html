Moment estimation and its limits

A finite sample produces a finite empirical moment in exact arithmetic. It does not establish that the corresponding population moment exists. Even when that moment exists, the estimator can have infinite variance or be dominated by events that most samples miss. We separate these questions using an exact Pareto model and a finite Gaussian mixture.

The sample notation follows the shared canon. For iid Xi≥0X_i\ge0Xi​≥0 and order q>0q>0q>0, write

mq=EXq,m^q,n=1n∑i=1nXiq.m_q=\mathbb E X^q,\qquad \widehat m_{q,n}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nX_i^q.mq​=EXq,mq,n​=n1​i=1∑n​Xiq​.
(1)

These are raw moments. For a signed variable, replace XqX^qXq by ∣X∣q|X|^q∣X∣q; a signed odd moment requires separate integrability conditions.

The estimator needs a higher moment¶

For Pareto with α,xm>0\alpha,x_m>0α,xm​>0,

mq=αxmqα−q(q<α),mq=∞(q≥α).m_q=\frac{\alpha x_m^q}{\alpha-q}\quad(q<\alpha),\qquad m_q=\infty\quad(q\ge\alpha).mq​=α−qαxmq​​(q<α),mq​=∞(q≥α).
(2)

Assume q<αq<\alphaq<α. Then the sample moment is unbiased for every n≥1n\ge1n≥1, but it has finite variance only if 2q<α2q<\alpha2q<α. In that regime,

Var⁡(m^q,n)=1n[αxm2qα−2q−(αxmqα−q)2].\operatorname{Var}(\widehat m_{q,n}) =\frac1n\left[ \frac{\alpha x_m^{2q}}{\alpha-2q} -\left(\frac{\alpha x_m^q}{\alpha-q}\right)^2\right].Var(mq,n​)=n1​[α−2qαxm2q​​−(α−qαxmq​​)2].
(3)

We prove both assertions. Linearity gives the expectation whenever mqm_qmq​ is finite. For 2q<α2q<\alpha2q<α, independence makes the cross-covariances zero. For q<α≤2qq<\alpha\le2qq<α≤2q, nonnegativity gives m^q,n2≥X12q/n2\widehat m_{q,n}^2\ge X_1^{2q}/n^2mq,n2​≥X12q​/n2, whose expectation is infinite. Since the estimator has finite mean, its variance is infinite too. Equality at 2q=α2q=\alpha2q=α is excluded by the logarithmic divergence of the Pareto moment integral.

For example, α=3,xm=1\alpha=3,x_m=1α=3,xm​=1 gives m2=3m_2=3m2​=3, but m^2,n\widehat m_{2,n}m2,n​ has infinite variance at every finite nnn. Consistency does not require estimator variance¶

We prove convergence in probability when mq<∞m_q<\inftymq​<∞, without a finite 2q2q2qth moment. Set Ti=XiqT_i=X_i^qTi​=Xiq​ and Ti(B)=min⁡(Ti,B)T_i^{(B)}=\min(T_i,B)Ti(B)​=min(Ti​,B). The triangle inequality proves m^q,n→mq\widehat m_{q,n}\to m_qmq,n​→mq​ in probability. This argument supplies no uniform rate over all integrable laws.

When mq=∞m_q=\inftymq​=∞, the stronger almost-sure divergence follows from the nonnegative infinite-mean theorem, applied to XiqX_i^qXiq​. That linked note proves the result by truncation. Finite observed values and floating-point overflow are separate matters; neither determines which population regime applies.

A square root changes the moment question¶

Let Rn=m^2,nR_n=\sqrt{\widehat m_{2,n}}Rn​=m2,n​​. If m2<∞m_2<\inftym2​<∞, then

Thus infinite variance of the empirical second moment does not imply infinite variance of the empirical root mean square. The square root can introduce downward bias through concavity. The sample raw moment remains unbiased. Bias, estimator variance, and the frequency of an underestimate are different properties.

Finite moments that most samples miss¶

Use the two-state variance mixture with P(Y=0.5)=0.99P(Y=0.5)=0.99P(Y=0.5)=0.99, P(Y=50.5)=0.01P(Y=50.5)=0.01P(Y=50.5)=0.01, and X=YZX=\sqrt YZX=Y​Z, with independent standard-normal ZZZ. Var⁡(1n∑Xi2)=76.25n.\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac1n\sum X_i^2\right)=\frac{76.25}{n}.Var(n1​∑Xi2​)=n76.25​.

Rare samples compensate in the unconditional mean; the raw second moment estimator is still unbiased.

E[xm1−ξ^n  |  ξ^n<1]=∞.\mathbb E\left[\frac{x_m}{1-\widehat\xi_n} \;\middle|\;\widehat\xi_n<1\right]=\infty.E[1−ξ​n​xm​​∣∣​ξ​n​<1]=∞.

Simulations must count non-finite fits; removing them does not remove the singularity of the remaining distribution. No universal ranking of the sample mean and this plug-in follows.

Rising power means and different targets¶

For fixed observations and positive weights wiw_iwi​ summing to 1, define Ap=(∑iwi∣xi∣p)1/pA_p=(\sum_iw_i|x_i|^p)^{1/p}Ap​=(∑i​wi​∣xi​∣p)1/p. For 0<p<q0<p<q0<p<q,

For geometric unit balls, use the unnormalized norm ∥x∥p=(∑∣xi∣p)1/p\|x\|_p=(\sum|x_i|^p)^{1/p}∥x∥p​=(∑∣xi​∣p)1/p. Its value decreases with ppp at fixed xxx. Mean absolute deviation is a different estimand¶

If μ=EX\mu=\mathbb EXμ=EX and E∣X∣<∞\mathbb E|X|<\inftyE∣X∣<∞, then E∣X−μ∣≤E∣X∣+∣μ∣<∞\mathbb E|X-\mu|\le\mathbb E|X|+|\mu|<\inftyE∣X−μ∣≤E∣X∣+∣μ∣<∞. If the variance is finite, Cauchy–Schwarz gives E∣X−μ∣≤Var⁡(X)\mathbb E|X-\mu|\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}E∣X−μ∣≤Var(X)​. Equality requires ∣X−μ∣|X-\mu|∣X−μ∣ to be constant almost surely. These are unscaled deviations; Gaussian calibration multiplies the mean absolute deviation by π/2\sqrt{\pi/2}π/2​, so that universal inequality does not apply to the calibrated value.

For example, the symmetric law on {−1,1}\{-1,1\}{−1,1} has standard deviation and unscaled mean absolute deviation both equal to 1. Its Gaussian-calibrated Std/MeanAD ratio is 2/π<1\sqrt{2/\pi}<12/π​<1. The Gaussian benchmark 1 in the dispersion diagnostic is a model reference, not a universal lower bound. Mean and median absolute deviations also answer different questions. Neither universally replaces a second-moment estimand or dominates every estimator under every loss.

Reproduce and interpret¶

Use pareto_type1, pareto_moment_estimator_summary.

Code
import numpy as np
from xmath.incerto.distributions import pareto_type1
from xmath.incerto.finite_moments import pareto_moment_estimator_summary

rng = np.random.default_rng(20261020)
for alpha in (3, 6):
    sample = pareto_type1.rvs(alpha, size=(100, 200), random_state=rng)
    estimates = np.mean(sample**2, axis=1)
    print(f"alpha={alpha:.4g}")
    for name, value in pareto_moment_estimator_summary(alpha, q=2, n=200).items():
        print(f"  {name}: {value:.4g}")
    quantiles = np.quantile(estimates, [0.1, 0.5, 0.9])
    print("Sample quantiles: [" + ", ".join(f"{v:.4g}" for v in quantiles) + "]")
