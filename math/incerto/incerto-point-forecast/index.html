Point forecasts under heavy-tailed losses

A point forecast needs a target and a loss function. The mean, median, and an event probability summarize different aspects of a distribution. Binary calibration evaluates the last of these after an event has been specified. This note develops the amount-score comparisons needed for SCoFT v4 chapter 11, §11.4, pp. 231–235 Taleb, 2025. It makes no empirical claim about forecasting contests or pandemics. Broader forecasting questions belong to the chapter 12 treatment.

Write d∈Rd\in\mathbb Rd∈R for a fixed point forecast and XXX for the future amount. We use ℓ(d,X)\ell(d,X)ℓ(d,X) for a loss, leaving LLL for the slowly varying function in the shared notation. The expected loss Eℓ(d,X)\mathbb E\ell(d,X)Eℓ(d,X) evaluates the action ddd under a specified probability law. Estimating that law from observations is a further problem.

Which point minimizes expected loss?¶

We prove the squared- and absolute-loss claims at their finite-risk scope. If EX2<∞\mathbb E X^2<\inftyEX2<∞, expansion around μ=EX\mu=\mathbb E Xμ=EX gives

E[(X−d)2]=Var⁡(X)+(d−μ)2.\mathbb E[(X-d)^2] =\operatorname{Var}(X)+(d-\mu)^2.E[(X−d)2]=Var(X)+(d−μ)2.
(1)

The cross term vanishes because E(X−μ)=0\mathbb E(X-\mu)=0E(X−μ)=0. The mean is the unique minimizing forecast. Finite variance of the sample mean is not needed to define a population median, and existence of a population mean alone does not make the squared-loss objective finite.

For absolute loss, assume E∣X∣<∞\mathbb E|X|<\inftyE∣X∣<∞. For d2>d1d_2>d_1d2​>d1​, integrate the pointwise slopes of ∣x−d∣|x-d|∣x−d∣ in ddd. Their magnitude is at most one, so interchanging expectation and this finite integral is valid. With FFF the CDF, the result is

E∣X−d2∣−E∣X−d1∣=∫d1d2{2F(t)−1} dt.\begin{aligned} &\mathbb E|X-d_2|-\mathbb E|X-d_1|\\ &\qquad=\int_{d_1}^{d_2}\{2F(t)-1\}\,dt. \end{aligned}​E∣X−d2​∣−E∣X−d1​∣=∫d1​d2​​{2F(t)−1}dt.​
(2)

The value of a slope exactly at an atom does not affect the integral. Monotonicity of FFF shows that the minimizing points are precisely the medians: F(d−)≤1/2≤F(d)F(d-)\le1/2\le F(d)F(d−)≤1/2≤F(d). This covers nonunique medians and atoms. A median is a property of any real probability distribution; the preceding finite-expected-loss optimization requires the additional integrability assumption.

A finite point does not remove an infinite expected loss¶

For exact Pareto XXX with xm>0x_m>0xm​>0, the moment theorem gives a sharp boundary. For each fixed finite ddd,

E∣X−d∣<∞⟺α>1,E[(X−d)2]<∞⟺α>2.\begin{gathered} \mathbb E|X-d|<\infty \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\alpha>1,\\ \mathbb E[(X-d)^2]<\infty \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\alpha>2. \end{gathered}E∣X−d∣<∞⟺α>1,E[(X−d)2]<∞⟺α>2.​
(3)

To prove necessity, for X>2∣d∣X>2|d|X>2∣d∣ the absolute error is at least X/2X/2X/2. The tail integral of the corresponding power diverges at and below the moment boundary. For sufficiency use ∣X−d∣≤X+∣d∣|X-d|\le X+|d|∣X−d∣≤X+∣d∣ and (X−d)2≤2X2+2d2(X-d)^2\le2X^2+2d^2(X−d)2≤2X2+2d2. Thus every finite point has infinite squared risk when 1<α≤21<\alpha\le21<α≤2, even though a finite mean exists. One can compare suitable differences of losses under weaker conditions, but that is a different optimization formulation; it is not a finite minimum of the ordinary squared-risk objective.

For α>1\alpha>1α>1 the exact Pareto mean and median are

μ=αxmα−1,d1/2=xm21/α.\mu=\frac{\alpha x_m}{\alpha-1}, \qquad d_{1/2}=x_m2^{1/\alpha}.μ=α−1αxm​​,d1/2​=xm​21/α.
(4)

The median formula follows from F(d1/2)=1/2F(d_{1/2})=1/2F(d1/2​)=1/2. For d≥xmd\ge x_md≥xm​, use ∣X−d∣=d−X+2(X−d)+|X-d|=d-X+2(X-d)_+∣X−d∣=d−X+2(X−d)+​ and the excess-mean formula to obtain

E∣X−d∣=d−μ+2xmαd1−αα−1.\mathbb E|X-d| =d-\mu+\frac{2x_m^\alpha d^{1-\alpha}}{\alpha-1}.E∣X−d∣=d−μ+α−12xmα​d1−α​.
(5)

This finite expression permits an exact comparison; a finite Monte Carlo average cannot prove that an expected loss exists.

Use pareto_tail_bet for the excess mean and point_forecast_loss for observed errors. The simulation uses exact Pareto draws at α=1.5,xm=1\alpha=1.5,x_m=1α=1.5,xm​=1, PCG64 seed 1110, and 20,000 observations. It has a finite absolute-risk target and an infinite squared-risk target.

Code: exact absolute risk and finite simulated losses
import numpy as np
from xmath.incerto.calibration import pareto_tail_bet, point_forecast_loss

alpha, xm = 1.5, 1.0
mean = alpha * xm / (alpha - 1)
median = xm * 2**(1 / alpha)
rng = np.random.default_rng(1110)
x = xm * (1 + rng.pareto(alpha, 20000))
for label, d in (("median", median), ("mean", mean)):
    excess = pareto_tail_bet(alpha, d, xm=xm)["excess_mean"]
    risk = d - mean + 2 * excess
    print(f"{label}: point={d:.3f}, exact absolute risk={risk:.3f}")
    print(f"  sample absolute={point_forecast_loss(x, d).mean():.3f}")
    squared = point_forecast_loss(x, d, metric="squared").mean()
    print(f"  sample squared={squared:.3f}; population squared=inf")

Output

  sample absolute=1.702
  sample squared=80.337; population squared=inf
mean: point=3.000, exact absolute risk=2.309
  sample absolute=2.252
  sample squared=78.520; population squared=inf

The moment-estimation note distinguishes existence of an estimand from finite variance of its estimator. That distinction also applies to losses: iid averages of integrable absolute errors can be consistent even when their sampling variance is infinite.

Normalization changes the score’s tail¶

Absolute error, squared error, MASE, and sMAPE are different functions of the same forecast and outcome. For a fixed finite ddd and sufficiently large ttt, exact Pareto XXX satisfies

P(∣X−d∣>t)=Fˉ(t+d),P((X−d)2>t)=Fˉ(t+d).\begin{gathered} P(|X-d|>t)=\bar F(t+d),\\ P((X-d)^2>t)=\bar F(\sqrt t+d). \end{gathered}P(∣X−d∣>t)=Fˉ(t+d),P((X−d)2>t)=Fˉ(t​+d).​
(6)

For the first identity, t>∣d∣t>|d|t>∣d∣ eliminates the lower branch X<d−tX<d-tX<d−t; for the second use t>∣d∣\sqrt t>|d|t​>∣d∣. Once the remaining thresholds exceed xmx_mxm​, the Pareto formula applies. Taking ratios as t→∞t\to\inftyt→∞ shows tail exponents α\alphaα and α/2\alpha/2α/2, respectively. Squaring the error changes its moment requirements.

MASE divides absolute error by a positive training-sample scale s0s_0s0​ Hyndman & Koehler, 2006, author manuscript §3, pp. 12–13. For a fixed realized s0>0s_0>0s0​>0, it preserves the absolute error’s tail exponent. If that scale is random, a conditional statement given training data does not automatically extend to the unconditional ratio: values near zero can change its moments. A constant training series can give s0=0s_0=0s0​=0, requiring an explicit policy before scoring.

For a fixed training vector y1,…,ymy_1,\ldots,y_my1​,…,ym​ and seasonal lag h<mh<mh<m, a conventional scale is the mean of ∣yj−yj−h∣|y_j-y_{j-h}|∣yj​−yj−h​∣ over j=h+1,…,mj=h+1,\ldots,mj=h+1,…,m. It is a naive forecast’s training error, not the dispersion of future observations around their sample mean. The helper accepts the scale explicitly, so the chosen lag and training window remain visible.

Several denominator conventions have been called sMAPE Hyndman, 2014. The convention used here is

ℓs(d,x)=2∣x−d∣∣x∣+∣d∣,∣x∣+∣d∣>0,ℓs(0,0)=0.\begin{gathered} \ell_{\rm s}(d,x)=\frac{2|x-d|}{|x|+|d|},\\ |x|+|d|>0,\\ \ell_{\rm s}(0,0)=0. \end{gathered}ℓs​(d,x)=∣x∣+∣d∣2∣x−d∣​,∣x∣+∣d∣>0,ℓs​(0,0)=0.​
(7)

The triangle inequality proves 0≤ℓs≤20\le\ell_{\rm s}\le20≤ℓs​≤2. A percentage presentation multiplies this by 100. Thus this normalized score is bounded even for unbounded XXX. A statement that every M4-style score has the same tail class as the underlying amount would be false for this sMAPE. The source’s §11.4 formula also needs absolute values in its numerator to describe either of these absolute-error metrics: signed errors can cancel. The chosen zero convention is stated explicitly, rather than evaluating 0/00/00/0.

Use point_forecast_loss. The outputs show how bounded normalization can hide changes in magnitude.

Forecast accuracy and the decision¶

Changing the score can change the preferred point and the sampling uncertainty. It does not by itself specify a useful action. A probability forecast may guide a threshold decision; an amount forecast may guide capacity; an explicit nonlinear payoff may require several parts of the predictive distribution. The hinge representation and payoff identity show how to evaluate a stated exposure when the required expectations exist. None of these arguments says that every point forecast of a heavy-tailed variable is meaningless.

For an event score, reporting the conditional probability minimizes expected Brier loss. For absolute amount error, a median minimizes finite expected loss. For squared amount error, the mean does so when the second moment is finite. These are separate statements with separate domains. Quantile losses, predictive ranges, and the broader chapter 12 argument will extend this page at their own scope.
