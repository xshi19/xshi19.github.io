Tail probabilities, payoffs, and error propagation

A forecast can accurately describe whether a threshold will be crossed while leaving the cost of crossing it largely unknown. The calibration note defines the event and its payoffs; the point-forecast note explains how the choice of loss changes the prediction target. Here we prove two nonidentification results and quantify error propagation in an exact tail model. They concern information supplied by forecasts, not the abilities of particular forecasters.

The source context is SCoFT v4, chapter 13, §§13.2–13.3, pp. 253–258, and §13.6, pp. 259–260 Taleb, 2025. The chapter guide records the section map and qualifications. All examples below are synthetic.

The forecast-martingale note addresses the separate question of how probabilities for the same event evolve through time under one law and an increasing information set.

A binary score does not identify a payoff mean¶

Let X≥0X\ge0X≥0 be a loss, u>0u>0u>0 a fixed threshold, and D=1{X>u}D=\mathbf1_{\{X>u\}}D=1{X>u}​. Write p=P(D=1)p=P(D=1)p=P(D=1) and let q∈[0,1]q\in[0,1]q∈[0,1] be a constant probability report. In the shared notation, p=Fˉ(u)p=\bar F(u)p=Fˉ(u). The Brier loss is (q−D)2(q-D)^2(q−D)2. Its excess expected loss above the true report is

E[(q−D)2]=p(1−p)+(q−p)2,RB(q)−RB(p)=(q−p)2.\begin{gathered} \mathbb E[(q-D)^2]=p(1-p)+(q-p)^2,\\ R_B(q)-R_B(p)=(q-p)^2. \end{gathered}E[(q−D)2]=p(1−p)+(q−p)2,RB​(q)−RB​(p)=(q−p)2.​
(1)

Expanding the square and using D2=DD^2=DD2=D proves both identities. They measure an error in an event probability. They contain no conditional severity. In particular, a calibrated report q=pq=pq=p need not have a small raw Brier score: the term p(1−p)p(1-p)p(1−p) is the outcome’s irreducible variation.

We now prove that even the complete iid binary-score sampling law cannot bound expected excess-payoff error. Fix 0<p<10<p<10<p<1 and u>0u>0u>0. For each α>1\alpha>1α>1, take an exact Pareto loss with minimum xm=up1/αx_m=u p^{1/\alpha}xm​=up1/α. The existing tail-payoff derivation then gives

P(X>u)=p,Cα(u)=E[(X−u)+],Cα(u)=upα−1.\begin{gathered} P(X>u)=p,\\ C_\alpha(u)=\mathbb E[(X-u)_+],\\ C_\alpha(u)=\frac{up}{\alpha-1}. \end{gathered}P(X>u)=p,Cα​(u)=E[(X−u)+​],Cα​(u)=α−1up​.​
(2)

Every model yields the same Bernoulli law for DDD, hence the same score law for any fixed report and any iid sample size. Yet Cα(u)C_\alpha(u)Cα​(u) can be arbitrarily large as α↓1\alpha\downarrow1α↓1. An amount estimate based only on ppp therefore has unbounded possible error across these models, even with zero Brier regret. All means in this comparison are finite; an infinite mean is not needed for the counterexample. With the same fixed ppp and uuu, the threshold-scaled tail-level mean is

E[XD]up=1+1α−1.\frac{\mathbb E[X D]}{up} =1+\frac1{\alpha-1}.upE[XD]​=1+α−11​.
(3)

This ratio increases as the exponent decreases toward one. It states precisely what is held fixed when we say that thicker tails enlarge the binary/continuous payoff gap. Across arbitrary families with changing scales, means, or caps, there is no universal ordering by a tail label.

The example uses brier_score and pareto_tail_bet. The fixed outcome list illustrates the identical scores; the payoff means are exact model evaluations, not estimates from that list.

from xmath.incerto.calibration import brier_score, pareto_tail_bet

p, u = .1, 5
outcomes = [0] * 9 + [1]
for alpha in (3, 1.5, 1.05):
    payoff = pareto_tail_bet(alpha, u, xm=u * p**(1 / alpha))
    score = brier_score([p] * 10, outcomes)
    print(f"alpha={alpha:.2f}: Brier={score:.3f}, excess mean={payoff['excess_mean']:.2f}")

Even uniformly small probability errors need tail control¶

One threshold loses information. The entire survival function determines the mean of a nonnegative loss through Tonelli’s theorem:

EX=∫0∞Fˉ(v) dv.\mathbb E X=\int_0^\infty\bar F(v)\,dv.EX=∫0∞​Fˉ(v)dv.
(4)

Knowing every probability exactly is therefore sufficient. Approximating every probability to a small absolute error is a different claim. We prove the distinction with a finite-support sequence.

Let the reference loss be identically zero. For each integer n≥2n\ge2n≥2, let Xn=n2X_n=n^2Xn​=n2 with probability 1/n1/n1/n, and zero otherwise. If F0F_0F0​ and FnF_nFn​ are their CDFs, then

sup⁡v∣Fn(v)−F0(v)∣=1n,EXn=n.\begin{gathered} \sup_v|F_n(v)-F_0(v)|=\frac1n,\\ \mathbb E X_n=n. \end{gathered}vsup​∣Fn​(v)−F0​(v)∣=n1​,EXn​=n.​
(5)

Indeed, the CDF difference equals 1/n1/n1/n on [0,n2)[0,n^2)[0,n2) and zero elsewhere. Multiplying the only positive value by its probability gives the mean. For every nonnegative threshold below n2n^2n2, reporting the reference exceedance probability zero has Brier regret 1/n21/n^21/n2 under the XnX_nXn​ model. Thus even uniformly vanishing thresholdwise regret does not control a mean over this class. Each individual law is bounded; the class has no common cap or uniform tail-integrability bound.

The relevant extra assumption can be seen directly. For two nonnegative laws F,GF,GF,G with finite means and any H>0H>0H>0, split the mean integrals at HHH:

∣EFX−EGX∣≤H∥F−G∥∞+EF[(X−H)+]+EG[(X−H)+].\begin{aligned} |\mathbb E_F X-\mathbb E_G X| &\le H\|F-G\|_\infty\\ &\quad+\mathbb E_F[(X-H)_+]\\ &\quad+\mathbb E_G[(X-H)_+]. \end{aligned}∣EF​X−EG​X∣​≤H∥F−G∥∞​+EF​[(X−H)+​]+EG​[(X−H)+​].​
(6)

The first term bounds the integral on [0,H][0,H][0,H]; positivity bounds the remaining difference by the two tails. If the tail terms tend uniformly to zero in a model class, uniform CDF convergence does imply convergence of means. A common finite cap supplies this control immediately. See moment estimation for why a finite sample alone does not establish the needed population moments.

Small report variance alone is insufficient: for fixed aaa and growing bbb, the beta variance tends to zero while the moment boundary aαa\alphaaα is unchanged. The report mean also tends to zero in this comparison. This is not a fixed-mean, vanishing-noise theorem. Concentrating reports away from zero, or imposing Π≥ε>0\Pi\ge\varepsilon>0Π≥ε>0, changes the conclusion. Likewise, a fixed physical cap bounds the transformed loss quantile.

What an evaluation must establish¶

The mathematical issues raised in §§13.4–13.5, pp. 258–259, can be stated without a judgment about a person or a trading record:

  • Evaluation of one indicator does not identify an unbounded severity distribution. The model needs an exposure and conditional severity.

  • One finite path cannot prove a universal distributional identity or exclude every rare-loss model. A specified iid sample and loss function can still supply statistical evidence; the sample size is not necessarily one merely because a record belongs to one strategy.

  • A probability improvement has decision value only relative to actions, costs, and consequences. In a fixed-severity binary decision it can change the preferred action, as the calibration extension demonstrates. There is no general theorem that better probabilities are useless.

The risk-measures note treats a different uncertainty model: a random tail exponent at a fixed probability level. It separates expected model VaR/ES from the VaR/ES of a predictive mixture. Neither construction follows just from the beta-report calculation.
