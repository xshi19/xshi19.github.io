Rayleigh speeds

The speed of a uniformly sampled particle in a two-dimensional equilibrium gas has a Rayleigh distribution. Each signed velocity component is Gaussian; taking the length of the velocity vector changes the law. This answers Taleb’s distribution challenge Taleb, 2026: the density already written in the accompanying animation is Rayleigh Matharium, 2026. No extra physical parameter is needed.

The sampling rule matters. An event recorded when a particle crosses a small hole favors faster particles. We derive the chamber law first, then the effusion law and the finite-system correction. A seeded simulation compares seven fitted families; it illustrates these distinctions without testing whether the animation implements equilibrium dynamics.

From components to speed¶

Generalized Gamma, not GIG¶

For a local shape s>0s>0s>0, define

Squared speed and energy¶

Let Q=V2Q=V^2Q=V2 and let ε=mV2/2\varepsilon=mV^2/2ε=mV2/2 be kinetic energy. The change of variable from vvv to qqq has Jacobian 1/(2q)1/(2\sqrt q)1/(2q​), so under hsh_shs​,

Crossing a hole changes the sample¶

Assume the hole is small compared with the mean free path, the gas near it is isotropic and equilibrated, and detection efficiency does not depend on speed. In time dtdtdt, particles that can cross a small area come from a layer of thickness vcos⁡θ dtv\cos\theta\,dtvcosθdt, where θ\thetaθ is the angle to the outward normal and cos⁡θ>0\cos\theta>0cosθ>0. This is the flux factor in the usual effusion calculation Fitzpatrick, n.d..

Multiplying the equilibrium velocity measure by this factor and integrating over outward directions leaves one extra factor vvv. Normalizing proves

gd(v)=vfd(v)EfV=2c(d+1)/2Γ((d+1)/2)vde−cv2,v>0.g_d(v)=\frac{vf_d(v)}{\mathbb E_f V} =\frac{2c^{(d+1)/2}}{\Gamma((d+1)/2)}v^d e^{-cv^2},\qquad v>0.gd​(v)=Ef​Vvfd​(v)​=Γ((d+1)/2)2c(d+1)/2​vde−cv2,v>0.
(11)

The effusing speed divided by σ\sigmaσ therefore has law χd+1\chi_{d+1}χd+1​. In particular,

g2(v)=2πv2σ3e−v2/(2σ2)=f3(v).g_2(v)=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\frac{v^2}{\sigma^3} e^{-v^2/(2\sigma^2)}=f_3(v).g2​(v)=π2​​σ3v2​e−v2/(2σ2)=f3​(v).
(12)

Two-dimensional effusion has the same speed law as a three-dimensional chamber. The change comes from sampling, not an added spatial dimension. Its mode is σd\sigma\sqrt dσd​, and its squared speed and energy use s=(d+1)/2s=(d+1)/2s=(d+1)/2 in the preceding Gamma formulas. Selecting only an outward direction would not introduce the factor vvv; counting crossings does.

The animation shows particles leaving an opening and accumulating in a histogram Matharium, 2026. If those are physical crossing events, the effusion law is the appropriate equilibrium prediction. If the opening merely conveys uniformly selected particles to a histogram, Rayleigh is appropriate. The displayed formula specifies the chamber law; without the sampling algorithm, the drawing does not resolve this distinction.

A finite isolated chamber¶

Canonical velocities have the preceding Gaussian components even for finite NNN. An isolated finite system with fixed total kinetic energy has a different joint measure. Assume N>1N>1N>1 ideal particles, or elastic hard disks or spheres, in reflecting hard walls, with total kinetic energy E>0E>0E>0 and no imposed total-momentum constraint. The velocity measure is uniform on the sphere

∑i=1N∥ui∥2=2Emin RdN.\sum_{i=1}^N\|\mathbf u_i\|^2=\frac{2E}{m} \quad\text{in }\mathbb R^{dN}.i=1∑N​∥ui​∥2=m2E​in RdN.
(13)

We derive its one-particle marginal. Integrating the remaining d(N−1)d(N-1)d(N−1) coordinates against the energy-shell delta function supplies the factor (E−mv2/2)d(N−1)/2−1(E-mv^2/2)^{d(N-1)/2-1}(E−mv2/2)d(N−1)/2−1. The first particle’s radial element contributes vd−1 dvv^{d-1}\,dvvd−1dv. Put vmax⁡=2E/mv_{\max}=\sqrt{2E/m}vmax​=2E/m​ and B(x,y)=Γ(x)Γ(y)/Γ(x+y)B(x,y)=\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)/\Gamma(x+y)B(x,y)=Γ(x)Γ(y)/Γ(x+y). fN,d(v)=2vd−1vmax⁡dB(d/2,d(N−1)/2)(1−v2vmax⁡2)d(N−1)/2−1,0<v<vmax⁡.f_{N,d}(v)= \frac{2v^{d-1}}{v_{\max}^{d}B(d/2,d(N-1)/2)} \left(1-\frac{v^2}{v_{\max}^2}\right)^{d(N-1)/2-1}, \quad 0<v<v_{\max}.fN,d​(v)=vmaxd​B(d/2,d(N−1)/2)2vd−1​(1−vmax2​v2​)d(N−1)/2−1,0<v<vmax​.
(14)

Equivalently,

mV22E∼Beta⁡ ⁣(d2,d(N−1)2).\boxed{\frac{mV^2}{2E}\sim \operatorname{Beta}\!\left(\frac d2,\frac{d(N-1)}2\right).}2EmV2​∼Beta(2d​,2d(N−1)​).​
(15)

This is a scaled Beta energy law, with a finite upper endpoint Scalas et al., 2015. For the instantaneous effusion measure, multiplication by vvv changes the first Beta shape to (d+1)/2(d+1)/2(d+1)/2, leaving the second unchanged.

Define the kinetic temperature by E=dNkTkin/2E=dNkT_{\rm kin}/2E=dNkTkin​/2. At fixed TkinT_{\rm kin}Tkin​ and fixed speed, the finite-system factor tends to e−mv2/(2kTkin)e^{-mv^2/(2kT_{\rm kin})}e−mv2/(2kTkin​) as N→∞N\to\inftyN→∞, and the Gamma-ratio asymptotic Γ(z+a)/Γ(z)∼za\Gamma(z+a)/\Gamma(z)\sim z^aΓ(z+a)/Γ(z)∼za gives the canonical normalizer. This proves the chi limit; it is not a uniform relative approximation all the way to vmax⁡v_{\max}vmax​. Fixing total momentum as well would change the available degrees of freedom and the Beta law.

Hard-core exclusion affects the allowed positions but introduces no velocity-dependent potential, so it does not alter the canonical factorization. Elastic collisions conserve energy; that alone does not prove that an arbitrary initial condition has equilibrated. Noninteracting point particles reflecting in a fixed box retain their individual speeds.

Leaking cools the chamber¶

The moment formula gives mean energy dkT/2dkT/2dkT/2 inside and (d+1)kT/2(d+1)kT/2(d+1)kT/2 among effusing particles. A leak therefore removes more energy per particle than the chamber average. In a large, unheated chamber that re-equilibrates between losses, treat NNN as continuous and use dots for time derivatives. Energy balance gives

d2k(NT˙+TN˙)=d+12kTN˙,NT˙=TN˙d.\frac d2 k(N\dot T+T\dot N) =\frac{d+1}{2}kT\dot N, \qquad N\dot T=\frac{T\dot N}{d}.2d​k(NT˙+TN˙)=2d+1​kTN˙,NT˙=dTN˙​.
(16)

Since N˙<0\dot N<0N˙<0, temperature falls. Starting at N0,T0N_0,T_0N0​,T0​, integration gives T/T0=(N/N0)1/dT/T_0=(N/N_0)^{1/d}T/T0​=(N/N0​)1/d within this quasistatic approximation. If the outgoing event rate is J(t)J(t)J(t), the accumulated speed density over an observation interval is

hcollected(v)=∫J(t)gd(v;T(t)) dt∫J(t) dt.h_{\rm collected}(v)= \frac{\int J(t)g_d(v;T(t))\,dt}{\int J(t)\,dt}.hcollected​(v)=∫J(t)dt∫J(t)gd​(v;T(t))dt​.
(17)

It generally mixes temperatures. The variance-mixture note explains why averaging over scales can change a marginal law. A fixed-temperature effusion density does not describe an entire cooling history.

A reproducible comparison¶

We draw 50,000 independent pairs of Gaussian components with kT/m=1kT/m=1kT/m=1, seed 20261022, and take their Euclidean lengths. All first components are drawn before all second components. This samples the canonical chamber law directly; it is neither molecular dynamics nor a sample extracted from the animation.

We fit Gaussian, Gamma, inverse Gamma, inverse Gaussian, GIG, Rayleigh, and Weibull laws by maximum likelihood. Positive families have location fixed at zero. Gaussian estimates mean and standard deviation; Rayleigh estimates one scale; Weibull, Gamma, and the two inverse families estimate two parameters. With KKK estimated parameters and maximized log likelihood ℓ\ellℓ, the reported score is AIC=2K−2ℓ\mathrm{AIC}=2K-2\ellAIC=2K−2ℓ in these fixed speed units.

The table’s KS distance is D=sup⁡v∣Fn(v)−Fθ^(v)∣D=\sup_v|F_n(v)-F_{\hat\theta}(v)|D=supv​∣Fn​(v)−Fθ^​(v)∣, where FnF_nFn​ is the empirical CDF. Its p-values are the ordinary two-sided KS calculation without correction for parameter fitting. They are descriptive diagnostics, not calibrated tests of the fitted families. Calibration would require, for example, a parametric bootstrap with refitting in each replicate SciPy developers, 2026. Zeros below are numerical underflow, not exact zero probabilities.

Why the GIG fit lands on Gamma¶

The Gamma MLE has shape s^=3.1513\hat s=3.1513s^=3.1513 and scale ϑ^=0.3964\hat\vartheta=0.3964ϑ^=0.3964. At its GIG representation (p,a,b)=(s^,2/ϑ^,0)(p,a,b)=(\hat s,2/\hat\vartheta,0)(p,a,b)=(s^,2/ϑ^,0), the inward derivative of log likelihood per observation is

1n∂ℓ∂b∣b=0+=12(1(s^−1)ϑ^−1n∑i=1n1vi)<0.\left.\frac1n\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial b}\right|_{b=0+} =\frac12\left( \frac1{(\hat s-1)\hat\vartheta} -\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{v_i}\right)<0.n1​∂b∂ℓ​∣∣​b=0+​=21​((s^−1)ϑ^1​−n1​i=1∑n​vi​1​)<0.
(18)

This expression is finite because s^>1\hat s>1s^>1. Differentiating the normalizing integral supplies the model reciprocal mean in the first term. The other two score components vanish at the Gamma MLE. Log likelihood is concave in the natural coordinates (p−1,−a/2,−b/2)(p-1,-a/2,-b/2)(p−1,−a/2,−b/2): the Hessian of its log normalizer is the covariance of (log⁡V,V,1/V)(\log V,V,1/V)(logV,V,1/V) in the interior. The supporting-plane inequality therefore certifies a maximum on the Gamma boundary. For strict a,b>0a,b>0a,b>0, this value is a supremum as b↓0b\downarrow0b↓0.

The helper uses that boundary law directly for its density and CDF. It does not present tiny positive GIG parameters as a distinct distribution. The table retains GIG’s nominal K=3K=3K=3, hence a score exactly 2 above Gamma’s. Ordinary regular-model AIC theory does not apply unchanged at this boundary; the score here is a stated comparison convention.

Quantiles and the squared-speed check¶

The squared-speed test compares against the specified exponential mean 2, without estimating it. Its KS statistic is 0.0058 and its p-value is 0.0696, so it does not reject this simple null at the 5% level. This p-value has the usual iid continuous-null calibration. It is not the probability that the exponential model is true. The different Rayleigh p-value in the table uses a fitted scale.
