Regular Variation

Overview¶

An exact Pareto tail has the same probability ratio whenever we multiply a threshold by a fixed amount. Regular variation asks for this behavior only in the limit as the threshold grows. It allows slow corrections to a power law while retaining a limiting tail exponent.

Read the Pareto survival example first for a concrete example. This page uses limits and positive measurable functions; the proof below establishes the power-times-slowly-varying characterization directly from the definition.

Definition and characterization¶

A positive measurable function fff is regularly varying at infinity with index ρ\rhoρ, written f∈RVρf\in RV_\rhof∈RVρ​, if, for every fixed t>0t>0t>0,

lim⁡x→∞f(tx)f(x)=tρ.\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(tx)}{f(x)}=t^\rho.x→∞lim​f(x)f(tx)​=tρ.
(1)

f(x)=xρL(x)f(x)=x^\rho L(x)f(x)=xρL(x)
(2)

with LLL slowly varying. A survival function Fˉ\bar FFˉ has a regularly varying right tail with exponent α>0\alpha>0α>0, written Fˉ∈RV−α\bar F\in RV_{-\alpha}Fˉ∈RV−α​, when

lim⁡x→∞Fˉ(tx)Fˉ(x)=t−α,t>0.\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\bar F(tx)}{\bar F(x)}=t^{-\alpha}, \qquad t>0.x→∞lim​Fˉ(x)Fˉ(tx)​=t−α,t>0.

Here Fˉ(x)=P(X>x)\bar F(x)=\mathbb P(X>x)Fˉ(x)=P(X>x), LLL is the slowly varying factor, α\alphaα is the positive tail exponent, and ttt is a fixed multiplier.

What the ratio means¶

Regular variation is the mathematical version of Pareto-style scale invariance. At high thresholds, multiplying the threshold by ttt has an asymptotically stable effect on exceedance probabilities. The slowly varying factor LLL allows departures from an exact Pareto law while preserving the same tail exponent.

The ratio statement is stronger than saying that large observations are more frequent than under a Gaussian baseline. It says that the relative penalty for raising a large threshold settles to a power t−αt^{-\alpha}t−α.

  • Constant functions are slowly varying. If L(x)=c>0L(x)=c>0L(x)=c>0, then L(tx)/L(x)=1L(tx)/L(x)=1L(tx)/L(x)=1 for every xxx and t>0t>0t>0.

  • Logarithmic corrections are slowly varying. For fixed β∈R\beta\in\mathbb Rβ∈R, L(x)=(log⁡x)βL(x)=(\log x)^\betaL(x)=(logx)β on x>1x>1x>1 satisfies L(tx)/L(x)→1L(tx)/L(x)\to1L(tx)/L(x)→1.

  • A nonzero power is not slowly varying. If L(x)=xβL(x)=x^\betaL(x)=xβ, then L(tx)/L(x)=tβL(tx)/L(x)=t^\betaL(tx)/L(x)=tβ, which equals 1 for all ttt only when β=0\beta=0β=0.

  • Lognormal right tails are heavy-tailed and subexponential, but not regularly varying: their fixed-multiplier survival ratios do not settle to t−αt^{-\alpha}t−α for any finite α\alphaα.

  • Exponential tails are neither regularly varying nor subexponential. For a multiplier t>1t>1t>1, their survival ratios decay exponentially in xxx.

Proof of the characterization¶

We prove the algebraic characterization used throughout these notes: a regularly varying function is a power times a slowly varying function. The proof is only an unwinding of the ratio definition. The Karamata note develops the related integral asymptotics and their moment consequences.

What the representation theorem adds¶

The elementary equivalence above defines LLL; it does not prove Karamata’s representation theorem. For positive measurable slowly varying LLL, that theorem gives, eventually,

Ratio diagnostics¶

The fixed-multiplier ratio can be compared algebraically for an exact Pareto tail and a power law with a slowly varying logarithmic correction.

For an exact Pareto tail and a logarithmically corrected tail, respectively,

Fˉ(2x)Fˉ(x)=2−α,(2x)−α(log⁡(2x))βx−α(log⁡x)β=2−α(1+log⁡2log⁡x)β.\frac{\bar F(2x)}{\bar F(x)}=2^{-\alpha}, \qquad \frac{(2x)^{-\alpha}(\log(2x))^\beta} {x^{-\alpha}(\log x)^\beta} =2^{-\alpha}\left(1+\frac{\log 2}{\log x}\right)^\beta.Fˉ(x)Fˉ(2x)​=2−α,x−α(logx)β(2x)−α(log(2x))β​=2−α(1+logxlog2​)β.
(9)

For α=1.5\alpha=1.5α=1.5 and β=1\beta=1β=1, the correction multiplier is 2 at x=2x=2x=2, 3/23/23/2 at x=4x=4x=4, and 5/45/45/4 at x=16x=16x=16. The second expression is used only sufficiently far into the tail, where it is decreasing and at most one before taking the ratio; a full distribution also needs a body below that range.

The exact Pareto ratio is constant at 2−α2^{-\alpha}2−α; the log-corrected ratio approaches the same target slowly. A finite diagnostic curve can suggest regular variation, but the definition is about the asymptotic limit.

Caveats¶

  • Regular variation is an asymptotic property. It does not say that every moderate observation follows a power law.

  • Estimating α\alphaα from finite samples is threshold-sensitive; the Hill estimator and log-log plots are diagnostics, not certificates.

  • A nonnegative law with a regularly varying right tail and α>0\alpha>0α>0 is subexponential (Foss, Korshunov, and Zachary, 2nd ed., §3.5, Theorem 3.29 and following paragraph, p. 57); see Subexponentiality for the one-big-jump principle.

  • Moment implications require assumptions on the full tail and should point to Karamata’s theorem or to a Pareto-specific proof.
