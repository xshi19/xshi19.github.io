Ruin and repeated exposure

An absorbing state changes which future payoffs a path can receive. Let TTT be the first ruin time, taking values in {1,2,…}∪{∞}\{1,2,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}{1,2,…}∪{∞}; the process stays ruined after TTT. The probability law PPP and expectation E\mathbb EE follow the shared notation.

Independent repeated exposure¶

Suppose trials are independent and each causes ruin with the same probability q∈(0,1]q\in(0,1]q∈(0,1] if reached. For every integer N≥0N\ge0N≥0,

P(T>N)=(1−q)N,P(T≤N)=1−(1−q)N,P(T>N)=(1-q)^N,\qquad P(T\le N)=1-(1-q)^N,P(T>N)=(1−q)N,P(T≤N)=1−(1−q)N,
(1)

with survival at N=0N=0N=0 defined as one. We prove both the finite formula and the limit: survival through NNN is the intersection of NNN independent nonruin events, hence the product. The events {T>N}\{T>N\}{T>N} decrease to {T=∞}\{T=\infty\}{T=∞}, so continuity of probability and (1−q)N→0(1-q)^N\to0(1−q)N→0 give P(T<∞)=1P(T<\infty)=1P(T<∞)=1. At q=0q=0q=0, survival is one at every horizon instead.

For q=0.02q=0.02q=0.02, ruin probabilities at horizons 10, 100 and 1000 are approximately 0.182927, 0.867380 and 0.9999999983. No heavy-tail assumption enters this calculation.

What the dependence assumption can be replaced by¶

We prove the equivalence. If the sum diverges, log⁡(1−hk)≤−hk\log(1-h_k)\le-h_klog(1−hk​)≤−hk​ forces the product to zero. If the sum converges, eventually hk≤1/2h_k\le1/2hk​≤1/2 and log⁡(1−hk)≥−2hk\log(1-h_k)\ge-2h_klog(1−hk​)≥−2hk​. If some reached hazard is one, ruin is certain by that step separately; all later survival probabilities are zero, and no later hazards are needed.

Growing ensemble expectation and a terminating path¶

Take q∈(0,1)q\in(0,1)q∈(0,1), a>1a>1a>1, and starting resource W0>0W_0>0W0​>0. Here WkW_kWk​ denotes resources at step kkk, not a mixing variable. Let

The proof is pathwise: after the finite ruin time the sum has only finitely many nonzero, finite terms, while its denominator grows. There is no contradiction with the expectation, which is carried by the increasingly rare surviving paths. With W0=1W_0=1W0​=1, q=0.02q=0.02q=0.02 and a=1.04a=1.04a=1.04, the ensemble factor per step is 1.0192. This example proves a particular failure of interchanging a path limit and expectation; it is not a general ergodic theorem.

An absorbing barrier can also make the order of additive payoffs matter. Starting at 3, the increments (−4,4)(-4,4)(−4,4) lead to permanent ruin at the first step. Reversing them gives resources (3,7,3)(3,7,3)(3,7,3) with no ruin. Both increment sums are zero. The rule here sets resources to zero on first reaching a nonpositive value and ignores all later increments.

Worked calculation and source scope¶

Use survival_from_hazards, absorbing_resource_path.

from xmath.incerto.lecture_risk import survival_from_hazards, absorbing_resource_path

survival = survival_from_hazards([0.02] * 3)
print("Survival through step 3: [" + ", ".join(f"{v:.4g}" for v in survival) + "]")
for increments in ([-4, 4], [4, -4]):
    path = absorbing_resource_path(3, increments)
    print(f"{increments}: [" + ", ".join(f"{v:.4g}" for v in path) + "]")

Output
