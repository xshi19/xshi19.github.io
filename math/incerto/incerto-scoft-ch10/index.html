Chapter 10 — Diagnosing returns

Chapter 10 brings the earlier moment and tail tools to a return series. Our S&P diagnostics page separates aggregation, drawdowns, moment concentration, records, and gain/loss comparisons. The statistical-pipeline page connects those questions to risk estimation and function extrapolation.

The source is SCoFT, third edition (2025), arXiv:2001.10488v4: chapter 10, printed pp. 193–205; Appendix F, pp. 207–212; and Appendix G, pp. 213–216 Taleb, 2025. Locators below refer to that version. The notes supply original arguments and code. They do not reproduce the source’s historical S&P study.

Quantities and source qualifications¶

We keep α\alphaα for the positive tail exponent, uuu for a threshold, and e(u)e(u)e(u) for mean excess. The source’s threshold KKK in chapter 10 becomes uuu; KjK_jKj​ remains a local hinge knot in the Appendix G treatment. For u>0u>0u>0 and a finite conditional mean,

ϕ(u)=E[X∣X>u]u,ϕ(u)=1+e(u)u.\begin{gathered} \phi(u)=\frac{\mathbb E[X\mid X>u]}u,\\ \phi(u)=1+\frac{e(u)}u. \end{gathered}ϕ(u)=uE[X∣X>u]​,ϕ(u)=1+ue(u)​.​
(1)

For an exact Pareto tail with α>1\alpha>1α>1, this is α/(α−1)\alpha/(\alpha-1)α/(α−1); for a unit exponential it tends to one. The ratio is dimensionless. It cannot have limit zero or require a second division by uuu.

The source’s empirical interpretations require care. Moment instability does not prove an infinite moment, and an infinite moment does not imply regular variation. The kurtosis aggregation identity needs iid observations and a finite fourth moment. The harmonic record benchmark needs iid continuous sampling; agreement with it proves neither independence nor extreme-value attraction. A finite sum of hinges represents a continuous piecewise-linear function; arbitrary nonlinear functions need a separate approximation statement. Hinge weights are slope differences, with slopes given by tangents of angles under specified axis units.

A small diagnostic¶

Use moment_share_path.

from xmath.incerto.diagnostics import moment_share_path

for sample in ([1, 1, 1, 1], [1, 1, 1, 5]):
    print(f"Largest fourth-power share: {moment_share_path(sample)[-1]:.4f}")

Output

Largest fourth-power share: 0.2500
Largest fourth-power share: 0.9952

The larger share concerns four invented observations. Both lists could come from bounded distributions with every moment finite. The numerical contrast is not a test of infinite moments.
