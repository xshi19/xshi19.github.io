Chapter 11 — Calibration and payoffs

Chapter 11 compares forecasts of events with forecasts of their consequences. The calibration and payoff page develops that distinction through exact models, conditional calibration, and score sampling laws. The point-forecast page explains which loss functions preserve or change an amount’s tail behavior.

The source is SCoFT, third edition (2025), arXiv:2001.10488v4, chapter 11, printed pp. 219–240 Taleb, 2025. All locators below refer to that version. The notes use original derivations, examples, code, and figures. They do not reproduce the book’s empirical or psychological claims as established facts.

Source qualifications¶

The chapter’s core payoff distinction does not establish that probability calibration is useless or that experimental findings in psychology are spurious. Such conclusions require the experiment’s estimand, instructions, observations, and alternative model. Our examples establish mathematical nonidentification and sensitivity under stated laws only.

The notes also correct several mathematical overextensions. A tail-amount multiplier is not necessarily a probability; the exact Pareto multiplier has denominator α−1\alpha-1α−1 for α>1\alpha>1α>1. A specified threshold event remains measurable. Jensen needs convexity in the chosen parameter or payoff argument. A beta forecast marginal and a Bernoulli event marginal need a joint-law assumption before their squared-error distribution is determined. A bounded Brier score has every positive moment, but can have kurtosis above three. Our binomial and iid-average derivations assume independence; normal limits require centering and scaling. Finally, sMAPE is bounded, and conditioning a cumulative payoff on survival does not generally preserve the underlying payoff distribution.

A finite score law¶

Use brier_score_distribution. With p=q=0.2p=q=0.2p=q=0.2, the expected Brier score is p(1−p)=0.16p(1-p)=0.16p(1−p)=0.16; increasing sample size reduces its variance but supplies no missing severity information.

from xmath.incerto.calibration import brier_score_distribution

for n in (1, 10, 100):
    score, mass = brier_score_distribution(n, .2, .2)
    mean = mass @ score
    variance = mass @ (score - mean)**2
    print(f"n={n}: mean={mean:.3f}, variance={variance:.5f}")

Output

n=10: mean=0.160, variance=0.00576
