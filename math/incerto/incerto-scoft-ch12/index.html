Chapter 12 — Point forecasts and tail risk

A forecast of an amount needs a target, a predictive distribution, and a decision context. A single mean or median cannot supply all three. The point-forecast note develops this distinction through stable scaling, repeated evaluation of forecast losses, quantiles, and synthetic bounded losses.

The source is SCoFT, third edition (2025), arXiv:2001.10488v4, chapter 12, printed pp. 241–246 Taleb, 2025. The chapter is coauthored with Yaneer Bar-Yam and Pasquale Cirillo. The attached paper is arXiv:2007.16096v1 Taleb et al., 2020; its main statements and commentary occupy pp. 1–3, technical discussion pp. 3–5, and further pandemic discussion pp. 5–8. These are separate locators, not an assertion that the paper and book contain identical claims.

The concept note identifies the third-party historical compilation without copying its data or treating its fitted tail as a universal law.

A rate with a precise meaning¶

For iid symmetric stable errors with index 0<α≤20<\alpha\le20<α≤2 and unit characteristic-function scale, the sample average has scale

n1/α−1.n^{1/\alpha-1}.n1/α−1.
(1)

This is an exact distributional identity, proved in the note. It describes an interquantile-width multiplier even when a mean absolute error does not exist. For positive Pareto samples with 1<α<21<\alpha<21<α<2, the analogous mean-error power is asymptotic; a general regularly varying law may add a slowly varying factor. A future single outcome retains its own dispersion.

Use stable_average_scale. The calculation compares averaging within each law, not risk across laws with a common variance, which need not exist.

from xmath.incerto.forecasting import stable_average_scale

for alpha in (.8, 1, 1.5, 2):
    ratio = stable_average_scale(alpha, 100) / stable_average_scale(alpha, 10)
    print(f"alpha={alpha:g}: tenfold-sample scale ratio={ratio:.3f}")

Qualifications to carry through the chapter¶

The chapter’s warning about an isolated number does not imply that every point forecast violates probability theory. A median, quantile, or bounded event probability is a defined object. Its usefulness depends on the loss and decision. A strong law is also a convergence statement, not a universal finite-sample convergence rate.

The note supplies precise replacements for several broad source claims:

  • Exact Pareto means diverge at α≤1\alpha\le1α≤1; a fixed bounded fatality variable has all positive moments. Moment boundaries for general regular variation depend on the slowly varying factor.

  • For nnn iid observations at a fixed threshold uuu, the survival fraction has MSE Fˉ(u){1−Fˉ(u)}/n\bar F(u)\{1-\bar F(u)\}/nFˉ(u){1−Fˉ(u)}/n, where Fˉ(u)=P(X>u)\bar F(u)=P(X>u)Fˉ(u)=P(X>u). The complete survival curve determines expected excess; one probability does not.

  • Infinite variance of an amount does not make every score or paired comparison unstable. Bounded scores and bounded paired differences retain finite-variance iid guarantees.

  • Multiplication or exponential growth alone proves neither a power law nor chaos. Tail extrapolation, threshold choice, and dependence remain assumptions to assess; more information need not preserve a tail exponent.

  • Precaution needs an exposure, action costs, and effectiveness assumptions. A changed action regime requires a conditional forecast and a causal comparison, not an unconditional accuracy verdict.
