Chapter 13 — Probability and payoff risk

Chapter 13 of Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails, third edition, arXiv:2001.10488v4, is titled “The probability conflation,” printed pp. 253–260 Taleb, 2025. The mathematical question is whether accuracy about an event transfers to accuracy about its consequences. The answer depends on what is known about severity and how uncertainty enters the model.

Read calibration and payoffs for the event/amount distinction, probability conflation for nonidentification and probability error, and VaR and expected shortfall for quantiles and tail-index uncertainty. The point-forecast page supplies the loss-function and moment background from chapter 12.

Scope to retain¶

  • A fixed event has a fixed probability under a fixed law. A random report needs a stated joint or uncertainty model; neither a uniform nor a beta law follows automatically from boundedness or the probability integral transform. Bounded scores also do not guarantee independent observations.

  • Zero regret for one binary event leaves conditional severity open. The complete distribution does determine payoff expectations, and a common cap or uniform tail control can turn probability error into a mean bound.

  • Chapter 13 randomizes a probability at fixed exponent. The separate exponent-uncertainty results hold for exact Pareto laws at a common cutoff and fixed tail mass. Averaging model quantiles and taking the quantile of a mixture are different operations.

  • The Pareto inverse survival is convex. In the beta-report calculation, the positive moment of order rrr exists exactly when r<aαr<a\alphar<aα. These conclusions are not formulas for every heavy-tailed distribution.

  • An unconditional tail-level expectation needs division by the tail mass to become ES in a continuous law; quantile atoms require fractional mass. VaR can fail subadditivity, and ES can be infinite.

  • The limit of unrooted high moments is not an expected maximum. For a fixed finite sample, the rooted power mean tends to the sample maximum, as proved in the moment-estimation note. This is not an identity for the expectation of a random maximum.
