Chapter 14 — Forecasts through time

Chapter 14 of Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails, third edition, arXiv:2001.10488v4, is titled “Election predictions as martingales: an arbitrage approach,” pp. 261–273 Taleb, 2025. Its mathematical question is how forecasts for the same binary event should fit together as information arrives. Our martingale note contains the proofs, models, and synthetic experiments.

Read calibration and payoffs for conditional quadratic scoring, and probability conflation for the limits of what a binary event forecast says about an amount. These remain the canonical homes of the chapter-11 and chapter-13 material.

The organizing identity is short. For a fixed event AAA resolved at the terminal time and nested information sets (Ft)(\mathcal F_t)(Ft​) under one law, define

pt=E[1A∣Ft],E[pt∣Fs]=ps,s≤t.\begin{gathered} p_t=\mathbb E[\mathbf1_A\mid\mathcal F_t],\\ \mathbb E[p_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=p_s, \qquad s\le t. \end{gathered}pt​=E[1A​∣Ft​],E[pt​∣Fs​]=ps​,s≤t.​
(1)

The note proves this with the tower property. It does not require a diffusion, continuous paths, a normal distribution, or a tradable vote share.

Notation and source qualifications¶

Here Φ\PhiΦ is the standard normal CDF, as in the shared notation. Our YYY is the bounded share and ptp_tpt​ the binary forecast, corresponding to the source’s YYY and BBB. We use uuu only for the share threshold and k=Φ−1(u)k=\Phi^{-1}(u)k=Φ−1(u) for its latent threshold. In §14.1, p. 267, the threshold must be transformed consistently; a share threshold of 1/21/21/2 becomes latent threshold zero. The source’s sigmoid is Φ(2x)\Phi(\sqrt2 x)Φ(2​x), so our latent coordinate is z=2xz=\sqrt2 xz=2​x and our latent volatility is 2\sqrt22​ times its volatility. Our σ\sigmaσ is never an unqualified estimate of vote-share volatility.

  • The bounded model’s terminal value is derived from its exact Gaussian latent transition. We do not use the delta approximation to share variance in §14.0.2, pp. 265–266 as an exact volatility calibration.

  • In §14.1, p. 267, the valuation must use the survival orientation: increasing the current level increases the value of the strict upper event. The time derivative in the backward equation is generally nonzero; the total drift, including that derivative, cancels.

  • Proposition 14.1, p. 268 requires a conditional qualification: a midpoint start can preserve the unconditional mean of a sigmoid of Brownian motion, but does not make that process a martingale. The note gives the drift cancellation and a direct conditional-expectation proof.

  • A probability tends to 1/21/21/2 under increasing centered Gaussian noise at a fixed signal. The chapter’s bounded martingale construction instead tends to the current share at fixed interior threshold. Neither result is a consequence of boundedness or no-arbitrage alone. The sensitivity to the underlying level also differs from forecast-path volatility.

  • Risk-neutral pricing does not identify subjective or physical probabilities without extra assumptions. The finite betting argument proves no arbitrage under its specified law and execution rules; a positive expected trading gain alone is not a sure profit.

  • Expected total squared revisions equal the initial Bernoulli variance when the final report resolves the event. A realized path can exceed that expectation. The addendum’s constant-volatility Brownian example cannot simultaneously remain bounded without changing its dynamics.

For a numerical comparison use gaussian_binary_probability and probit_binary_probability. The two scales have different units, so these calls illustrate their respective limits, not a volatility-matched model comparison.

from xmath.incerto.martingales import (
    gaussian_binary_probability, probit_binary_probability,
)

for scale in (1, 10):
    normal = gaussian_binary_probability(.54, scale, 1)
    bounded = probit_binary_probability(.54, scale, 1)
    print(f"scale={scale}: Gaussian={normal:.4f}, bounded={bounded:.4f}")

Output

scale=1: Gaussian=0.5160, bounded=0.5503
scale=10: Gaussian=0.5016, bounded=0.5400

The figures compare uncertainty limits, the two martingales and their latent coordinate, and coherent versus deliberately altered revisions. The multiple-candidate heuristic and commentary about individual forecasters are not used as mathematical results. All experiments use simulated data.
