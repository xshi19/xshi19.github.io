Chapter 4 — Finite-moment fat tails

Chapter 4 asks how a distribution can depart sharply from a Gaussian model while retaining finite moments. Its first mechanism is to randomize a Gaussian’s variance. Its second question is statistical: which moments must exist for a chosen estimate to have a finite error variance?

We use SCoFT’s 2025 third edition, arXiv:2001.10488v4, ch. 4, printed pp. 69–89. The chapter’s broad use of “fat tails” includes comparisons with a Gaussian at fixed variance. That usage does not assert regular variation or subexponentiality.

Start with a conditional model¶

Let X=YZX=\sqrt YZX=Y​Z, with Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim N(0,1)Z∼N(0,1) independent of positive variance YYY. The shared mixing symbol is YYY; see the notation canon and Normix foundation. With EY=1\mathbb EY=1EY=1 and finite EY2\mathbb EY^2EY2,

EX=0,Var⁡(X)=1,EX4=3EY2.\mathbb EX=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X)=1,\qquad \mathbb EX^4=3\mathbb EY^2.EX=0,Var(X)=1,EX4=3EY2.
(1)

The mixture note proves these identities. Equal probabilities on variances 0.4 and 1.6 give fourth moment 4.08; probabilities 0.99 and 0.01 on variances 0.5 and 50.5 give 77.25. Both mixtures have a finite MGF everywhere. A sharper peak and larger far-tail probabilities relative to a unit normal do not make either mixture a power law.

Specify what is perturbed¶

The source’s Gaussian boundaries in §4.3.1 concern differentiation with respect to scale. Our body/shoulder note also computes variance curvature. For a unit normal the positive density boundaries are

variance perturbation:3±6,scale perturbation:(5±17)/2.\begin{aligned} \text{variance perturbation:}&\quad \sqrt{3\pm\sqrt6},\\ \text{scale perturbation:}&\quad \sqrt{(5\pm\sqrt{17})/2}. \end{aligned}variance perturbation:scale perturbation:​3±6​​,(5±17​)/2​.​
(2)

Neither pair is a threshold for fitting a Pareto tail. A small-perturbation expansion is pointwise, and its zero-curvature points need not equal the crossings of a finite mixture. Tail probabilities have their own curvature: convexity in variance holds only above a threshold relative to that variance, not for every tail event and every mixing interval.

Separate a moment from its estimator¶

For iid Pareto observations, the moment note proves

EXq<∞  ⟺  q<α,Var⁡(m^q,n)<∞  ⟺  2q<αwhen q<α.\mathbb E X^q<\infty\iff q<\alpha,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\widehat m_{q,n})<\infty\iff 2q<\alpha \quad\text{when }q<\alpha.EXq<∞⟺q<α,Var(mq,n​)<∞⟺2q<αwhen q<α.
(3)

At exponent 3 the second moment exists, its sample average is unbiased and consistent, and that average has infinite variance. Its square root has a finite second moment. This last distinction matters when reading the standard-deviation comparison around §4.4.3, p. 83: conclusions about an average of squares do not transfer unchanged to its square root.

Even with every moment finite, a rare high-variance component can be absent from most short samples. The repeated-sample demo shows both mechanisms. Neither mechanism implies that every sample mean is downward biased. The plug-in singularity adds a third issue: fitting a model may create divergent estimator moments near a parameter boundary.

Notation and source qualifications¶

Source usageConvention used here
Normal scale parameterN(μ,v)N(\mu,v)N(μ,v) uses variance as its second argument; write v=σ2v=\sigma^2v=σ2 explicitly.
Stochastic variance or reciprocal varianceYYY is positive variance; local Q=1/YQ=1/YQ=1/Y is precision.
Student tail parameterν\nuν is degrees of freedom; the power-tail exponent is α=ν\alpha=\nuα=ν. Unit Student scale is not unit standard deviation.
Mean deviation / MADState the center and whether the deviation is a mean or a median; our dispersion demo applies Gaussian calibration.
Rising normsApA_pAp​ averages powers; geometric ℓp\ell_pℓp​ norms sum them. Their monotonicities differ.

Zero mean does not identify an MGF with a characteristic function; the finite-mixture formulas show their opposite signs in the exponent. Missing a power moment also does not by itself establish a power law. The explicit mixture and Pareto calculations provide narrower, checkable statements than either shortcut.

The chapter’s efficiency discussion is conditional on a model and an estimand. A Gaussian calibration does not make mean absolute deviation an unbiased estimator of standard deviation under every other law. We do not adopt a universal preference for one dispersion statistic. The bounded Cauchy experiment is reserved for chapter 7; recursive mixing for chapter 20.
