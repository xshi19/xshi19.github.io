Chapter 7 — Averages and limit laws

An average can converge while its fluctuations remain far from Gaussian. It can also fail to estimate any finite expectation. This chapter separates the target, the mode of convergence, and the scale of the error. Start with weak and strong laws, then compare normalized sums with the Cauchy average.

This guide uses Taleb (2025), third edition (2025), arXiv:2001.10488v4, chapter 7, printed pp. 137–152, with attached appendices C (pp. 183–186) and D (pp. 187–188). The concept pages contain original proofs at the stated scope and identify cited general theorems.

Qualifications to carry into the source¶

  • A CLT requires a parent law in a domain of attraction. There is no unconditional claim that all iid sums admit a nondegenerate normalized limit.

  • The exponent-two Pareto example has infinite variance. A Gaussian basin does not always imply square-root normalization. A central bell shape, a fourth-moment plot, and uniform CDF error are different diagnostics.

  • Cumulants are evaluated at the origin and standardized by the matching power of variance. Skewness scales as n−1/2n^{-1/2}n−1/2 and excess kurtosis as n−1n^{-1}n−1 when those moments exist. Neither a zero fourth cumulant nor a finite empirical value identifies the parent law.

  • Our §7.7 calculation covers symmetric stable laws and checks the Gaussian endpoint explicitly. We do not adopt the source’s general skew-stable expression or its Fourier-transform constants without proof.

  • Convergence of a sample mean and ergodicity are distinct questions. A multiplicative growth example compares different observables; it does not invalidate the ergodic theorem.

  • A right-tail specification cannot determine an entire two-sided rate density. Appendix C’s transformation is proved under an exact model; it supplies no independent empirical evidence about epidemics.

  • Appendix D’s exponential expression is a bound or a logarithmic-rate statement, depending on hypotheses. It is not generally an exact probability. Independence of summands supplies MGF factorization.

Worked values and figures¶

Use pareto_capped_mean and symmetric_stable_mean_abs.

from xmath.incerto.limits import pareto_capped_mean, symmetric_stable_mean_abs

print(f"Capped Pareto(1) at 100: {pareto_capped_mean(100, 1):.4g}")
for n in (1, 100, 10000):
    print(f"Stable alpha=1.5, n={n}: {symmetric_stable_mean_abs(1.5, n=n):.4g}")
