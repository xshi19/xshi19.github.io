Chapter 9 — Maxima and hidden tails

A maximum records the largest observed value. A tail model describes what may exceed it and how much those outcomes contribute to a payoff. This chapter connects the GEV family, threshold excesses, and hidden moments. Appendix E supplies the exact Pareto exponent calibration.

The source is SCoFT, third edition (2025), arXiv:2001.10488v4, chapter 9, printed pp. 171–181, and Appendix E, pp. 189–191 Taleb, 2025. All locators below use that edition. The concept notes give original derivations and distinguish exact-model identities, cited general theorems, and numerical comparisons.

Notation and qualifications¶

Use xmx_mxm​ for the Pareto minimum, α\alphaα for its positive exponent, ξ\xiξ for EVI, and MnM_nMn​ for the iid sample maximum. The first quantity decreases at order 1/n1/n1/n; the second at order n−rn^{-r}n−r. Both are population calculations averaged over repeated samples. Neither is the bias of the empirical sample mean.

Several distinctions matter when reading the source:

  • A slowly varying factor need not settle to a constant. Fréchet attraction uses tail ratios and a suitable normalization.

  • The Gumbel CDF for maxima is exp⁡(−e−z)\exp(-e^{-z})exp(−e−z); no minima convention is silently substituted. Finite-block accuracy is checked against Φn\Phi^nΦn.

  • PBDH needs the domain-of-attraction assumption. Its uniform CDF limit does not by itself imply convergence of means.

  • A hidden pppth Pareto moment is finite only for p<αp<\alphap<α. The exact missing probability is Beta(1,n); the exponential law is the limit after multiplying that probability by nnn.

  • An empirical distribution is a legitimate probability measure. CDF accuracy alone does not control unbounded-payoff accuracy. A continuous population is not absolutely continuous with respect to the finite atomic empirical measure, so a population/empirical density ratio cannot reconstruct the missing tail.

  • The inverse-gamma law needs exact Pareto sampling and a specified fixed cutoff or fixed retained count. Debiasing keeps the shape parameter; conditioning or clipping changes the sampling experiment.

A finite-sample comparison¶

Use expected_pareto_hidden_fraction.

from xmath.incerto.extremes import expected_pareto_hidden_fraction

for n in (100, 1000, 10000):
    mass = expected_pareto_hidden_fraction(n, 1.5, p=0)
    mean = expected_pareto_hidden_fraction(n, 1.5)
    print(f"n={n}: missing probability={mass:.4g}, hidden mean fraction={mean:.4g}")

Output

n=100: missing probability=0.009901, hidden mean fraction=0.192
n=1000: missing probability=0.000999, hidden mean fraction=0.08928
n=10000: missing probability=9.999e-05, hidden mean fraction=0.04145
