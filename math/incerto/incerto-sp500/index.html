S&P 500 — what return diagnostics can establish

Return diagnostics ask different questions. Aggregation examines changes with the holding period; a drawdown uses the order of prices; a tail fit uses large observations. Agreement among them can motivate a model comparison, but no finite collection of these plots proves that a population moment is infinite.

This page develops the diagnostics in SCoFT v4, chapter 10, §§10.1–10.3, printed pp. 193–205 Taleb, 2025. The existing S&P tail page owns data access and the Hill/mean-excess workflow. Here we add chronological and aggregation checks. The pipeline note examines what these checks mean for a risk estimator.

Data, clock, and estimands¶

We use Yahoo Finance ^GSPC daily Close, fetched locally on 2026-10-30, with auto_adjust=False Yahoo Finance, 2026. This is a price index, not a dividend-reinvested total-return series. The default return window is 1928-01-03 through 2026-10-29: 24,824 close-to-close log returns from 24,825 closes. The 1927-12-30 close anchors the first return. We exclude the supplied 2026-10-30 intraday row.

The full available history includes early crises and 1987; it also pools different market regimes and historical index coverage. We do not assume a homogeneous iid law. The tail page gives the fetch and local-import commands. The cache remains ignored by Git; under the Yahoo source-use terms we do not redistribute prices Yahoo, 2026. Only derived statistics and diagnostic plots appear here. FRED remains an alternate provider S&P Dow Jones Indices LLC, 2026, with a shorter available history; it is not the input to these outputs. The examples read the local cache with fetching disabled and an inclusive 2026-10-29 cutoff.

For strictly positive, chronologically ordered closes PtP_tPt​, define

rt=log⁡Pt−log⁡Pt−1,ℓt=−rt.\begin{gathered} r_t=\log P_t-\log P_{t-1},\\ \ell_t=-r_t. \end{gathered}rt​=logPt​−logPt−1​,ℓt​=−rt​.​
(1)

Here PtP_tPt​ is an index level; PPP without a time subscript denotes probability, as in the notation canon. All returns, including zero returns, remain in aggregation and record counts. Only the conditional gain/loss fits select rt>0r_t>0rt​>0 or ℓt>0\ell_t>0ℓt​>0. Joining loss days before aggregation would change the time interval.

The loader’s cleaned cache omits unavailable closes. We do not fill gaps: one return joins consecutive available closes, which can span a weekend, holiday, or missing observation. The report includes the largest calendar gap and rejects unordered or duplicated dates. A gap count alone cannot identify missing trading sessions. No total-return adjustment, intraday sampling, or volatility standardization is performed.

Kurtosis under aggregation¶

For a nonconstant finite sample x1,…,xnx_1,\ldots,x_nx1​,…,xn​, let m^q\widehat m_qmq​ be its central moment with divisor nnn, for q=2,4q=2,4q=2,4. We use excess kurtosis

m^q=1n∑j=1n(xj−xˉ)q,γ^2=m^4m^22−3.\begin{gathered} \widehat m_q=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n(x_j-\bar x)^q,\\ \widehat\gamma_2=\frac{\widehat m_4}{\widehat m_2^2}-3. \end{gathered}mq​=n1​j=1∑n​(xj​−xˉ)q,γ​2​=m22​m4​​−3.​
(2)

There is no finite-sample bias correction. Pearson kurtosis is this number plus three. An undefined population fourth moment does not prevent the sample statistic from returning a finite number.

Aggregation identity. Suppose XiX_iXi​ are iid, have variance 0<σ2<∞0<\sigma^2<\infty0<σ2<∞, and have a finite fourth central moment μ4\mu_4μ4​. For Sh=∑i=1hXiS_h=\sum_{i=1}^hX_iSh​=∑i=1h​Xi​, the population excess kurtosis satisfies

γ2(Sh)=γ2(X1)h.\gamma_2(S_h)=\frac{\gamma_2(X_1)}h.γ2​(Sh​)=hγ2​(X1​)​.
(3)

We prove this identity, not a convergence claim about a particular market sample. Center Xi←Xi−E[Xi]X_i\leftarrow X_i-\mathbb E[X_i]Xi​←Xi​−E[Xi​] for this proof; XiX_iXi​ and ShS_hSh​ now denote the centered variables and their sum. Centering preserves variance and kurtosis. In the expansion of the fourth power of the sum, independence and zero means eliminate terms with a singleton index. The surviving terms give

E[Sh4]=hμ4+3h(h−1)σ4,Var⁡(Sh)=hσ2.\begin{gathered} \mathbb E[S_h^4]\\ =h\mu_4+3h(h-1)\sigma^4,\\ \operatorname{Var}(S_h)=h\sigma^2. \end{gathered}E[Sh4​]=hμ4​+3h(h−1)σ4,Var(Sh​)=hσ2.​
(4)

Dividing by h2σ4h^2\sigma^4h2σ4 and subtracting three proves the formula. Dependence can introduce additional terms. Finite samples also lose blocks as hhh increases. A slowly falling or rising sample curve therefore does not, by itself, distinguish infinite fourth moments from dependence, changing volatility, or sampling variation.

The kappa note compares mean absolute deviations of sums. Our report plugs the daily and aggregated sample mean absolute deviations into kappa_from_mads. Each horizon uses its own sample mean; the daily baseline uses all returns. The result is descriptive. It neither estimates a unique tail exponent nor carries an iid confidence interval for dependent market returns.

In this sample, disjoint-block excess kurtosis is 18.77, 7.66, 12.04 at horizons 1, 5, and 21. It falls and then rises. The overlapping values are 18.77, 9.77, 9.91; these windows reuse observations. Both curves remain far above zero, but neither identifies a population fourth moment. Disjoint 5- and 21-return blocks give plug-in kappa 0.12 and 0.10. Splitting the returns in half changes those values to 0.22, 0.20 in the first half and 0.03, -0.04 in the second. Kappa here is sensitive to the sample window and need not lie between zero and one.

Drawdowns and conditional tail means¶

At date jjj, the running price peak is max⁡0≤i≤jPi\max_{0\le i\le j}P_imax0≤i≤j​Pi​. The maximum fractional drawdown is

Dn=max⁡0≤j≤n(1−Pjmax⁡0≤i≤jPi).D_n=\max_{0\le j\le n}\left(1-\frac{P_j}{\max_{0\le i\le j}P_i}\right).Dn​=0≤j≤nmax​(1−max0≤i≤j​Pi​Pj​​).
(5)

This equals the largest fractional decline across ordered peak/trough pairs: for fixed jjj, the largest previous PiP_iPi​ maximizes the decline. Including i=ji=ji=j makes the statistic nonnegative. This positive fractional convention differs from a negative log-drawdown convention. A distribution of drawdowns also depends on the window and overlap rules; a whole-series value is one path statistic, not a sample of iid losses.

For a positive threshold uuu with Fˉ(u)=P(X>u)>0\bar F(u)=P(X>u)>0Fˉ(u)=P(X>u)>0, finite conditional mean, and mean excess e(u)e(u)e(u), define

ϕ(u)=E[X∣X>u]u,ϕ(u)=1+e(u)u.\begin{gathered} \phi(u)=\frac{\mathbb E[X\mid X>u]}u,\\ \phi(u)=1+\frac{e(u)}u. \end{gathered}ϕ(u)=uE[X∣X>u]​,ϕ(u)=1+ue(u)​.​
(6)

The second line follows by subtracting uuu inside the conditional expectation. Thus ϕ(u)>1\phi(u)>1ϕ(u)>1; it cannot tend to zero. For an exact Pareto law, u≥xmu\ge x_mu≥xm​ and α>1\alpha>1α>1 give ϕ(u)=α/(α−1)\phi(u)=\alpha/(\alpha-1)ϕ(u)=α/(α−1). A unit exponential instead gives ϕ(u)=1+1/u→1\phi(u)=1+1/u\to1ϕ(u)=1+1/u→1. These are model identities, not a characterization of every possible tail class. For the left tail, apply the same definition to ℓt=−rt\ell_t=-r_tℓt​=−rt​ and condition on ℓt>u\ell_t>uℓt​>u.

Fourth-moment concentration and the MS plot¶

For p>0p>0p>0, the raw absolute-power maximum-to-sum share is

Rn(p)=max⁡i≤n∣Xi∣p∑i=1n∣Xi∣p.R_n^{(p)}=\frac{\max_{i\le n}|X_i|^p} {\sum_{i=1}^n|X_i|^p}.Rn(p)​=∑i=1n​∣Xi​∣pmaxi≤n​∣Xi​∣p​.
(7)

The finite-mean max-to-sum argument proves Rn(p)→0R_n^{(p)}\to0Rn(p)​→0 almost surely under iid sampling and 0<E∣X∣p<∞0<\mathbb E|X|^p<\infty0<E∣X∣p<∞. Apply that argument to ∣X∣p|X|^p∣X∣p; its nonnegativity and finite positive mean are exactly the needed assumptions. An all-zero finite prefix has no defined ratio.

A high Rn(4)R_n^{(4)}Rn(4)​ says one observation dominates the raw fourth-power total. It is not literally that observation’s share of central kurtosis: recentering and the variance denominator change when an observation is removed. The report keeps those quantities separate and repeats them on the first and second halves of the available series.

For the full sample, the largest absolute daily return contributes 25.22% of the raw fourth-power total. It is the 1987-10-19 loss; removing that observation changes daily sample excess kurtosis from 18.77 to 13.76. The corresponding fourth-power shares in the two halves are 9.67% and 53.54%. The same event dominates the shorter second-half total more strongly. These are concentration and deletion diagnostics, not tests that a fourth moment is infinite.

Finite-path instability cannot identify an infinite moment. It also cannot establish regular variation. Even at the population level, an infinite moment need not imply regular variation: the survival

Fˉ(x)=x−1(1+14sin⁡(log⁡x)),x≥1\begin{gathered} \bar F(x)=x^{-1}\left(1+\tfrac14\sin(\log x)\right),\\ x\ge1 \end{gathered}Fˉ(x)=x−1(1+41​sin(logx)),x≥1​
(8)

is decreasing, since its derivative has the sign of −1+(cos⁡(log⁡x)−sin⁡(log⁡x))/4<0-1+(\cos(\log x)-\sin(\log x))/4<0−1+(cos(logx)−sin(logx))/4<0. It starts at one and is bounded below by 3/(4x)3/(4x)3/(4x), so its mean is infinite by survival integration. But Fˉ(eπx)/Fˉ(x)\bar F(e^\pi x)/\bar F(x)Fˉ(eπx)/Fˉ(x) oscillates without a limit, ruling out regular variation. This is an explicit counterexample to treating missing moments and power tails as equivalent classes.

Records, extrema, and the two tails¶

A strict upper record occurs when XjX_jXj​ exceeds every earlier observation. The first observation counts as a record. Let Nn+N_n^+Nn+​ denote the cumulative count; define Nn−N_n^-Nn−​ using strict new minima. For iid observations from a continuous distribution, each side separately satisfies

Hn=∑j=1n1j,E[Nn+]=Hn,Var⁡(Nn+)=∑j=1n1j(1−1j).\begin{gathered} H_n=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1j,\\ \mathbb E[N_n^+]=H_n,\\ \operatorname{Var}(N_n^+) =\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1j\left(1-\frac1j\right). \end{gathered}Hn​=j=1∑n​j1​,E[Nn+​]=Hn​,Var(Nn+​)=j=1∑n​j1​(1−j1​).​
(9)

Here is a proof of the expectation and variance. All n!n!n! rank orders are equally likely. The sequence of insertion ranks has jjj choices at step jjj and gives a bijection to those orders. Its components are therefore independent and uniform on {1,…,j}\{1,\ldots,j\}{1,…,j}. An upper record occurs at rank jjj, with probability 1/j1/j1/j. Adding independent Bernoulli means and variances proves both formulas. Lower records use rank one and the same argument; upper and lower counts are not mutually independent.

Rounded data can contain ties, which do not create strict records. The continuous-iid benchmark then need not apply. Volatility clustering and nonstationarity also change the reference experiment. Agreement with HnH_nHn​ cannot establish independence, and independence alone does not establish a maximum domain of attraction.

For gain/loss asymmetry, compare positive rtr_trt​ with positive ℓt\ell_tℓt​, using the same threshold rules and showing each side’s retained counts. Thus these choices do not support a threshold-independent ordering of the two population tails. The loss-tail study also examines smaller fixed counts of extremes.

Reproduce the observed results¶

Use load_sp500_prices and sp500_diagnostics. The last helper reuses PriceSeries.log_returns.

Code: inspect a local return series
import numpy as np
from xmath.incerto.datasets import load_sp500_prices
from xmath.incerto.diagnostics import sp500_diagnostics, sample_excess_kurtosis

prices = load_sp500_prices(fetch=False, end_date="2026-10-29")
dates, returns = prices.log_returns()
result = sp500_diagnostics(prices)
print(f"Return window: {dates[0]} to {dates[-1]}; returns={len(returns)}")
print(f"Zero returns: {result['zero_returns']}; largest calendar gap: {result['max_calendar_gap_days']} days")
for name, window in result["windows"].items():
    print(f"{name}: price anchors {window['start']} to {window['end']}; returns={window['n']}")
    print(f"  Maximum drawdown: {100 * window['drawdown']:.2f}%")
    for row in window["aggregation"]:
        print(f"  h={row['horizon']}: blocks={row['blocks']}, dropped={row['dropped']}, excess kurtosis={row['excess_kurtosis']:.2f}, kappa={row['kappa']:.2f}")
    print(f"  Fourth-power maximum share: {100 * window['moment_shares']['4']:.2f}%")
full = result["windows"]["full"]
for row in full["overlapping"]:
    print(f"Overlapping h={row['horizon']}: blocks={row['blocks']}, excess kurtosis={row['excess_kurtosis']:.2f}, kappa={row['kappa']:.2f}")
rec = full["records"]
print(f"Records upper/lower: {rec['upper']:.0f}/{rec['lower']:.0f}; iid mean={rec['iid_mean']:.2f}, variance={rec['iid_variance']:.2f}")
print(f"Log-return extrema (%): {100 * rec['minimum']:.2f}, {100 * rec['maximum']:.2f}")
largest = int(np.argmax(np.abs(returns)))
reduced = np.delete(returns, largest)
print(f"Largest absolute return date: {dates[largest]}; excess kurtosis without it: {sample_excess_kurtosis(reduced):.2f}")
trough = int(np.argmax(1 - prices.close / np.maximum.accumulate(prices.close)))
peak = int(np.argmax(prices.close[:trough + 1]))
print(f"Maximum drawdown peak/trough dates: {prices.date[peak]}, {prices.date[trough]}")
for side, tail in full["tails"].items():
    print(f"{side}: {tail['n']} positive observations")
    for point in tail["hill"]:
        print(f"  k={point['k']}: alpha={point['alpha']:.2f}")
    for point in tail["conditional"]:
        print(f"  threshold={100 * point['threshold']:.2f}% log, count={point['exceedances']:.0f}, conditional mean / threshold={point['ratio']:.2f}")

uv run python demos/incerto/sp500_diagnostics.py regenerates the synthetic figures and the derived observed figure, and prints the complete local report. It uses disjoint horizons 1, 5, and 21, then repeats with overlapping windows; incomplete disjoint suffixes are dropped and counted. It also reports subwindow dates, drawdowns, extrema, record counts, MS shares for powers 1–4, both tail fits, and the frozen holdout comparison. The helper requires at least 44 closes. Such a small sample is sufficient to execute the code, not to support a tail conclusion.

Both halves contain 12,412 returns. The first ends on 1977-08-03; the second begins on 1977-08-04, using the previous close as its anchor. The full sample’s longest gap between closes is 12 calendar days. We retain that close-to-close change; a return observation is therefore not always one calendar day. The disjoint 5- and 21-return calculations discard respectively four and two returns at the end.

Choose windows and thresholds before interpreting results. Preserve the data source, retrieval date, checksum, and excluded observations with each private run. Examine dependence and time-window sensitivity before attaching uncertainty intervals. These diagnostics are exploratory; testing many thresholds and reporting only the most striking one changes the inferential question. No market model or tail exponent is selected by this page.
