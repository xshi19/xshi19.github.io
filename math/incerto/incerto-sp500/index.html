S&P 500 — what return diagnostics can establish

Return diagnostics ask different questions. Aggregation examines changes with the holding period; a drawdown uses the order of prices; a tail fit uses large observations. Agreement among them can motivate a model comparison, but no finite collection of these plots proves that a population moment is infinite.

This page develops the diagnostics in SCoFT v4, chapter 10, §§10.1–10.3, printed pp. 193–205 Taleb, 2025. The existing S&P tail page owns data access and the Hill/mean-excess workflow. Here we add chronological and aggregation checks. The pipeline note examines what these checks mean for a risk estimator.

Data, clock, and estimands¶

No observed S&P sample or empirical result is distributed with this page. The code reads the same authorized local cache as the existing tail page, with fetching disabled. FRED’s SP500 series is a daily closing price index, without dividends; its redistribution restrictions remain applicable S&P Dow Jones Indices LLC, 2026. The currently documented FRED window contains ten years of history, so it cannot reproduce the source’s much longer historical experiment or its discussion of 1987.

For strictly positive, chronologically ordered closes PtP_tPt​, define

rt=log⁡Pt−log⁡Pt−1,ℓt=−rt.\begin{gathered} r_t=\log P_t-\log P_{t-1},\\ \ell_t=-r_t. \end{gathered}rt​=logPt​−logPt−1​,ℓt​=−rt​.​
(1)

Here PtP_tPt​ is an index level; PPP without a time subscript denotes probability, as in the notation canon. All returns, including zero returns, remain in aggregation and record counts. Only the conditional gain/loss fits select rt>0r_t>0rt​>0 or ℓt>0\ell_t>0ℓt​>0. Joining loss days before aggregation would change the time interval.

The loader’s cleaned cache omits unavailable closes. We do not fill gaps: one return joins consecutive available closes, which can span a weekend, holiday, or missing observation. The report includes the largest calendar gap and rejects unordered or duplicated dates. A gap count alone cannot identify missing trading sessions. No total-return adjustment, intraday sampling, or volatility standardization is performed.

Kurtosis under aggregation¶

For a nonconstant finite sample x1,…,xNx_1,\ldots,x_Nx1​,…,xN​, let m^q\widehat m_qmq​ be its central moment with divisor NNN. We use excess kurtosis

m^q=1N∑j=1N(xj−xˉ)q,γ^2=m^4m^22−3.\begin{gathered} \widehat m_q=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N(x_j-\bar x)^q,\\ \widehat\gamma_2=\frac{\widehat m_4}{\widehat m_2^2}-3. \end{gathered}mq​=N1​j=1∑N​(xj​−xˉ)q,γ​2​=m22​m4​​−3.​
(2)

There is no finite-sample bias correction. Pearson kurtosis is this number plus three. An undefined population fourth moment does not prevent the sample statistic from returning a finite number.

Aggregation identity. Suppose XiX_iXi​ are iid, have variance 0<σ2<∞0<\sigma^2<\infty0<σ2<∞, and have a finite fourth central moment μ4\mu_4μ4​. For Sh=∑i=1hXiS_h=\sum_{i=1}^hX_iSh​=∑i=1h​Xi​, the population excess kurtosis satisfies

γ2(Sh)=γ2(X1)h.\gamma_2(S_h)=\frac{\gamma_2(X_1)}h.γ2​(Sh​)=hγ2​(X1​)​.
(3)

We prove this identity, not a convergence claim about a particular market sample. Set Zi=Xi−E[Xi]Z_i=X_i-\mathbb E[X_i]Zi​=Xi​−E[Xi​]. In the expansion of the fourth power of the sum, independence and zero means eliminate terms with a singleton index. The surviving terms give

E ⁣[(∑i=1hZi)4]=hμ4+3h(h−1)σ4,Var⁡(Sh)=hσ2.\begin{gathered} \mathbb E\!\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^h Z_i\right)^4\right]\\ =h\mu_4+3h(h-1)\sigma^4,\\ \operatorname{Var}(S_h)=h\sigma^2. \end{gathered}E⎣⎡​(i=1∑h​Zi​)4⎦⎤​=hμ4​+3h(h−1)σ4,Var(Sh​)=hσ2.​
(4)

Dividing by h2σ4h^2\sigma^4h2σ4 and subtracting three proves the formula. Dependence can introduce additional terms. Finite samples also lose blocks as hhh increases. A slowly falling or rising sample curve therefore does not, by itself, distinguish infinite fourth moments from dependence, changing volatility, or sampling variation.

Drawdowns and conditional tail means¶

Define the running price peak Pj⋆P_j^\starPj⋆​ and maximum fractional drawdown DnD_nDn​ by

Pj⋆=max⁡0≤i≤jPi,Dn=max⁡0≤j≤n(1−PjPj⋆).\begin{gathered} P_j^\star=\max_{0\le i\le j}P_i,\\ D_n=\max_{0\le j\le n}\left(1-\frac{P_j}{P_j^\star}\right). \end{gathered}Pj⋆​=0≤i≤jmax​Pi​,Dn​=0≤j≤nmax​(1−Pj⋆​Pj​​).​
(5)

This equals the largest fractional decline across ordered peak/trough pairs: for fixed jjj, the largest previous PiP_iPi​ maximizes the decline. Including i=ji=ji=j makes the statistic nonnegative. This positive fractional convention differs from a negative log-drawdown convention. A distribution of drawdowns also depends on the window and overlap rules; a whole-series value is one path statistic, not a sample of iid losses.

For a positive threshold uuu with P(X>u)>0P(X>u)>0P(X>u)>0, finite conditional mean, and mean excess e(u)e(u)e(u), define

ϕ(u)=E[X∣X>u]u,ϕ(u)=1+e(u)u.\begin{gathered} \phi(u)=\frac{\mathbb E[X\mid X>u]}u,\\ \phi(u)=1+\frac{e(u)}u. \end{gathered}ϕ(u)=uE[X∣X>u]​,ϕ(u)=1+ue(u)​.​
(6)

The second line follows by subtracting uuu inside the conditional expectation. Thus ϕ(u)>1\phi(u)>1ϕ(u)>1; it cannot tend to zero. For an exact Pareto law, u≥xmu\ge x_mu≥xm​ and α>1\alpha>1α>1 give ϕ(u)=α/(α−1)\phi(u)=\alpha/(\alpha-1)ϕ(u)=α/(α−1). A unit exponential instead gives ϕ(u)=1+1/u→1\phi(u)=1+1/u\to1ϕ(u)=1+1/u→1. These are model identities, not a characterization of every possible tail class. For the left tail, apply the same definition to −rt-r_t−rt​ and condition on −rt>u-r_t>u−rt​>u.

Fourth-moment concentration and the MS plot¶

For p>0p>0p>0, the raw absolute-power maximum-to-sum share is

Qn(p)=max⁡i≤n∣Xi∣p∑i=1n∣Xi∣p.Q_n^{(p)}=\frac{\max_{i\le n}|X_i|^p} {\sum_{i=1}^n|X_i|^p}.Qn(p)​=∑i=1n​∣Xi​∣pmaxi≤n​∣Xi​∣p​.
(7)

The finite-mean max-to-sum argument proves Qn(p)→0Q_n^{(p)}\to0Qn(p)​→0 almost surely under iid sampling and 0<E∣X∣p<∞0<\mathbb E|X|^p<\infty0<E∣X∣p<∞. Apply that argument to ∣X∣p|X|^p∣X∣p; its nonnegativity and finite positive mean are exactly the needed assumptions. An all-zero finite prefix has no defined ratio.

A high Qn(4)Q_n^{(4)}Qn(4)​ says one observation dominates the raw fourth-power total. It is not literally that observation’s share of central kurtosis: recentering and the variance denominator change when an observation is removed. The report keeps those quantities separate and repeats them on the first and second halves of the available series.

Finite-path instability cannot identify an infinite moment. It also cannot establish regular variation. Even at the population level, an infinite moment need not imply regular variation: the survival

Fˉ(x)=x−1(1+14sin⁡(log⁡x)),x≥1\begin{gathered} \bar F(x)=x^{-1}\left(1+\tfrac14\sin(\log x)\right),\\ x\ge1 \end{gathered}Fˉ(x)=x−1(1+41​sin(logx)),x≥1​
(8)

is decreasing, since its derivative has the sign of −1+(cos⁡(log⁡x)−sin⁡(log⁡x))/4<0-1+(\cos(\log x)-\sin(\log x))/4<0−1+(cos(logx)−sin(logx))/4<0. It starts at one and is bounded below by 3/(4x)3/(4x)3/(4x), so its mean is infinite by survival integration. But Fˉ(eπx)/Fˉ(x)\bar F(e^\pi x)/\bar F(x)Fˉ(eπx)/Fˉ(x) oscillates without a limit, ruling out regular variation. This is an explicit counterexample to treating missing moments and power tails as equivalent classes.

Records, extrema, and the two tails¶

A strict upper record occurs when XjX_jXj​ exceeds every earlier observation. The first observation counts as a record. Let Nn+N_n^+Nn+​ denote the cumulative count; define Nn−N_n^-Nn−​ using strict new minima. For iid observations from a continuous distribution, each side separately satisfies

Hn=∑j=1n1j,E[Nn+]=Hn,Var⁡(Nn+)=∑j=1n1j(1−1j).\begin{gathered} H_n=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1j,\\ \mathbb E[N_n^+]=H_n,\\ \operatorname{Var}(N_n^+) =\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1j\left(1-\frac1j\right). \end{gathered}Hn​=j=1∑n​j1​,E[Nn+​]=Hn​,Var(Nn+​)=j=1∑n​j1​(1−j1​).​
(9)

Here is a proof of the expectation and variance. All n!n!n! rank orders are equally likely. The sequence of insertion ranks has jjj choices at step jjj and gives a bijection to those orders. Its components are therefore independent and uniform on {1,…,j}\{1,\ldots,j\}{1,…,j}. An upper record occurs at rank jjj, with probability 1/j1/j1/j. Adding independent Bernoulli means and variances proves both formulas. Lower records use rank one and the same argument; upper and lower counts are not mutually independent.

Rounded data can contain ties, which do not create strict records. The continuous-iid benchmark then need not apply. Volatility clustering and nonstationarity also change the reference experiment. Agreement with HnH_nHn​ cannot establish independence, and independence alone does not establish a maximum domain of attraction.

For gain/loss asymmetry, compare positive rtr_trt​ with positive −rt-r_t−rt​, using the same threshold rules and showing each side’s retained counts. Run on the existing local cache¶

Use DatasetNotAvailable, load_sp500_prices, and sp500_diagnostics. The last helper reuses PriceSeries.log_returns.

Code: inspect a local return series
from xmath.incerto.datasets import DatasetNotAvailable, load_sp500_prices
from xmath.incerto.diagnostics import sp500_diagnostics

try:
    prices = load_sp500_prices(fetch=False)
except DatasetNotAvailable:
    print("No local S&P cache; empirical diagnostics remain uncomputed.")
else:
    result = sp500_diagnostics(prices)
    print(f"Window: {result['start']} to {result['end']}; returns={result['returns']}")
    full = result["windows"]["full"]
    print(f"Maximum drawdown: {full['drawdown']:.4f}")
    for row in full["aggregation"]:
        print(f"h={row['horizon']}: blocks={row['blocks']}, kurtosis={row['excess_kurtosis']:.4g}, kappa={row['kappa']:.4g}")
    for side, tail in full["tails"].items():
        print(f"{side}: {tail['n']} positive observations")
        for point in tail["hill"]:
            print(f"  k={point['k']}: alpha={point['alpha']:.4g}")

Output

No local S&P cache; empirical diagnostics remain uncomputed.

uv run python demos/incerto/sp500_diagnostics.py regenerates the synthetic figures and prints the complete local-cache report when data are present. It uses disjoint horizons 1, 5, and 21, then repeats with overlapping windows; incomplete disjoint suffixes are dropped and counted. It also reports subwindow dates, drawdowns, extrema, record counts, MS shares for powers 1–4, both tail fits, and the frozen holdout comparison. At least 44 closes are required to form a 21-return block in each half. Such a small sample is sufficient to execute the code, not to support a tail conclusion.

Choose windows and thresholds before interpreting results. Preserve the data source, retrieval date, checksum, and excluded observations with each private run. Examine dependence and time-window sensitivity before attaching uncertainty intervals. These diagnostics are exploratory; testing many thresholds and reporting only the most striking one changes the inferential question. No market model or tail exponent is selected by this page.

Source scope¶

SCoFT v4 §§10.1–10.3, pp. 193–205 Taleb, 2025 motivates these diagnostics. The aggregation and record identities above are proved under their stated assumptions; the figures are synthetic or analytic examples. We do not reproduce the book’s empirical figures, numerical verdicts, or claim that moment instability establishes a power law. Definition 10.1 on p. 200 is replaced by the conditional-mean identity stated here, with the correct sign and conditioning event for each tail.
