Survival Tail Ratio

Statement¶

For a right-tail survival function Fˉ(x)=P(X>x)\bar F(x)=\mathbb P(X>x)Fˉ(x)=P(X>x), the survival tail-ratio diagnostic is

QF(x)=Fˉ(x)2Fˉ(2x),Q_F(x)=\frac{\bar F(x)^2}{\bar F(2x)},QF​(x)=Fˉ(2x)Fˉ(x)2​,
(1)

where Fˉ(2x)>0\bar F(2x)>0Fˉ(2x)>0. With iid copies X1X_1X1​ and X2X_2X2​, the numerator is

Fˉ(x)2=P(X1>x, X2>x).\bar F(x)^2=\mathbb P(X_1>x,\ X_2>x).Fˉ(x)2=P(X1​>x, X2​>x).
(2)

Thus QF(x)Q_F(x)QF​(x) compares a two-coordinate moderate-extreme event with a one-coordinate double-threshold event. It is a tail-geometry diagnostic, not an estimator by itself.

Here x>0x>0x>0 is the comparison threshold, FFF is the CDF, and α>0\alpha>0α>0, xm>0x_m>0xm​>0 are the Pareto exponent and lower cutoff. QFQ_FQF​ is distinct from the regular-variation ratio Fˉ(tx)/Fˉ(x)\bar F(tx)/\bar F(x)Fˉ(tx)/Fˉ(x).

What a borderline ratio does and does not establish¶

SCoFT v4 §§5.0.2–5.0.3, pp. 93–95 Taleb, 2025, motivates this event comparison with exponential and sigmoid survivals. We derive their values here and keep them separate from a convolution-tail criterion.

For centered logistic survival Fˉ(x)=1/(1+e2kx)\bar F(x)=1/(1+e^{2kx})Fˉ(x)=1/(1+e2kx), k>0k>0k>0,

QF(x)=1+e4kx(1+e2kx)2⟶1.Q_F(x)=\frac{1+e^{4kx}}{(1+e^{2kx})^2}\longrightarrow1.QF​(x)=(1+e2kx)21+e4kx​⟶1.
(6)

This law is light-tailed. Its density is bounded by a constant times e−2k∣x∣e^{-2k|x|}e−2k∣x∣, so its MGF is finite for ∣θ∣<2k|\theta|<2k∣θ∣<2k by comparison with an exponential integral on each half-line. The density is k2sech⁡2(kx)\tfrac{k}{2}\operatorname{sech}^2(kx)2k​sech2(kx), the hypsecant2 helper’s law with loc=0, scale=1/k. The name identifies this squared-secant convention, not the ordinary hyperbolic-secant distribution.

For a centered Laplace law with scale b>0b>0b>0, the right survival is 12e−x/b\tfrac12e^{-x/b}21​e−x/b on x≥0x\ge0x≥0, giving QF(x)=1/2Q_F(x)=1/2QF​(x)=1/2. Caveats¶

  • QF(x)Q_F(x)QF​(x) is a distribution-level diagnostic. A finite empirical estimate can be dominated by sampling noise because both the numerator and denominator involve rare events.

  • The iid interpretation of Fˉ(x)2\bar F(x)^2Fˉ(x)2 fails under dependence. Clustered extremes need their own dependence model before this ratio can be read as a joint-event probability.

  • The ratio uses right tails. Two-sided returns, losses, and absolute values require an explicit transformation before the diagnostic is applied.

  • QF(x)→0Q_F(x)\to0QF​(x)→0 is compatible with one-big-jump geometry, but it is not a replacement for the convolution-tail definition of Subexponentiality.
