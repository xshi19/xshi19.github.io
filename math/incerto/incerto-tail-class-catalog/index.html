Tail Class Catalog

Main facts¶

The SCoFT Prologue guide separates Gaussian-relative kurtosis from asymptotic tail classes. This catalog compares nonnegative right-tail models by subexponentiality and regular variation, then checks which moments exist. The definitions concern a specified population law.

The positive classifications are cited, not proved here: Foss, Korshunov, and Zachary, second edition (2013), §3.5, Theorem 3.29 and its following paragraph, p. 57 (regular variation, including Pareto); Theorem 3.30 and its following examples, p. 58 (lognormal and Weibull with shape below one). See the book. We derive the elementary tail ratios and moment contrasts below; the appendix proves the convolution decomposition.

Let Fˉ(x)=P(X>x)\bar F(x)=\mathbb P(X>x)Fˉ(x)=P(X>x) be the survival function. In the table, “regularly varying” means Fˉ∈RV−α\bar F\in RV_{-\alpha}Fˉ∈RV−α​ for some α>0\alpha>0α>0, so Fˉ(tx)/Fˉ(x)→t−α\bar F(tx)/\bar F(x)\to t^{-\alpha}Fˉ(tx)/Fˉ(x)→t−α for fixed t>0t>0t>0. Here FFF is the CDF, xxx is the threshold, and α\alphaα is a positive tail exponent. All examples below have nonnegative support, with positive scale parameters, positive gamma shape and rate, and nonzero lognormal log-variance. See Shared notation.

Moment gates are different questions¶

The Cramér condition asks whether EeθX<∞\mathbb E e^{\theta X}<\inftyEeθX<∞ for at least one θ>0\theta>0θ>0. Ordinary moments ask about EXq\mathbb E X^qEXq, with q>0q>0q>0. For the following nonnegative models:

Proof of the lognormal moment contrast¶

We prove both entries in the lognormal row. Let Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim N(0,1)Z∼N(0,1) and X=eZX=e^ZX=eZ. Completing the square gives, for every real qqq,

EXq=12π∫−∞∞eqz−z2/2 dz=eq2/212π∫−∞∞e−(z−q)2/2 dz=eq2/2.\mathbb E X^q =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{qz-z^2/2}\,dz =e^{q^2/2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-(z-q)^2/2}\,dz =e^{q^2/2}.EXq=2π​1​∫−∞∞​eqz−z2/2dz=eq2/22π​1​∫−∞∞​e−(z−q)2/2dz=eq2/2.
(3)

In contrast, for any fixed θ>0\theta>0θ>0,

EeθX=12π∫−∞∞exp⁡(θez−z2/2) dz=∞.\mathbb E e^{\theta X} =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \exp(\theta e^z-z^2/2)\,dz=\infty.EeθX=2π​1​∫−∞∞​exp(θez−z2/2)dz=∞.
(4)

Indeed, ez/z2→∞e^z/z^2\to\inftyez/z2→∞ as z→∞z\to\inftyz→∞, so the integrand is at least 1 for all sufficiently large zzz. The integral over that half-line diverges. This establishes failure of the Cramér condition despite all positive power moments being finite. Subexponentiality is a separate, cited result; it does not follow just from this failure.

For a Pareto model with α=5/2\alpha=5/2α=5/2 and xm=1x_m=1xm​=1, the mean is 5/35/35/3 and the variance is 20/920/920/9, but the fourth moment diverges. The formulas follow from the linked integral: EX2=5\mathbb E X^2=5EX2=5 and 5−(5/3)2=20/95-(5/3)^2=20/95−(5/3)2=20/9. Pearson kurtosis is therefore not a finite summary for this model. Having a finite variance does not imply a finite fourth or positive exponential moment.

Exact light-tail checks¶

We prove the two negative classifications in the table. Write Gamma⁡(k,λ)\operatorname{Gamma}(k,\lambda)Gamma(k,λ) in shape–rate notation, with k,λ>0k,\lambda>0k,λ>0. At rate one, convolution of the densities with shapes kkk and ℓ>0\ell>0ℓ>0 gives

A finite model comparison¶

Run uv run python demos/incerto/prologue_tail_classes.py from the repository root. It evaluates log⁡A2(u)\log A_2(u)logA2​(u) for Pareto (α=5/2,xm=1)(\alpha=5/2,x_m=1)(α=5/2,xm​=1), standard lognormal, Weibull (β=1/2)(\beta=1/2)(β=1/2), exponential (rate 1), and gamma (shape 2, rate 1). The fixed grid has 241 logarithmically spaced thresholds from 1 to 104; all quantities are dimensionless population calculations. There is no sampling, data fitting, truncation of a distribution, or uncertainty interval.

The script also prints exact exponential and gamma two-sum checks at u=2,10,100u=2,10,100u=2,10,100. The helper uses log survival values for the multiplier figure; non-finite values cause an error. In particular, it does not divide zeros when an ordinary survival function underflows. The figure is numerical illustration of the formulas above, not evidence of an observed tail class.

Caveats¶

  • Regular variation and subexponentiality concern limits, not the shape of a fitted curve on a finite range.

  • Very small survival probabilities can underflow in numerical calculations. Logarithmic ratios help preserve information; rounded zeros do not establish exact zero probability.

  • The subexponential definition used here is the right-tail iid version for nonnegative summands. Two-sided and dependent settings need additional assumptions.

  • The finite exact examples above check formulas, not the accuracy of an upstream sampler, survival-function implementation, or quadrature routine.

Appendix: convolution calculation¶

This appendix proves the elementary two-summand convolution decomposition used by the subexponential diagnostic. We work with independent continuous variables, then specialize to an iid lower-bounded distribution. Discrete, dependent, and two-sided variants require separate assumptions.

For independent continuous random variables X1X_1X1​ and X2X_2X2​ with densities f1f_1f1​, f2f_2f2​ and survival functions Fˉ1\bar F_1Fˉ1​, Fˉ2\bar F_2Fˉ2​,

P(X1+X2>x)=∫−∞∞f1(y)Fˉ2(x−y) dy.\mathbb P(X_1+X_2>x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_1(y)\bar F_2(x-y)\,dy.P(X1​+X2​>x)=∫−∞∞​f1​(y)Fˉ2​(x−y)dy.
(11)

To see the iid reduction, take a common density fff, survival function Fˉ\bar FFˉ, and lower support endpoint aaa, with x>2ax>2ax>2a. Split the event {X1+X2>x}\{X_1+X_2>x\}{X1​+X2​>x} into three cases, ignoring probability-zero boundary points: first X2≤x/2X_2\le x/2X2​≤x/2, second X1≤x/2X_1\le x/2X1​≤x/2, and third both variables exceed x/2x/2x/2. In the first case, conditioning on X2=yX_2=yX2​=y with a≤y≤x/2a\le y\le x/2a≤y≤x/2 leaves the requirement X1>x−yX_1>x-yX1​>x−y, so this part contributes ∫ax/2f(y)Fˉ(x−y) dy\int_a^{x/2} f(y)\bar F(x-y)\,dy∫ax/2​f(y)Fˉ(x−y)dy. The second case contributes the same quantity by iid symmetry. The remaining upper-right square has probability Fˉ(x/2)2\bar F(x/2)^2Fˉ(x/2)2 by independence.

For a=0a=0a=0, the partition can be recorded as disjoint regions rather than a contour plot. Boundaries have zero probability under the continuous-law assumption.
