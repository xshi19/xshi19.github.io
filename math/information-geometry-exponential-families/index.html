Exponential families and sufficient statistics

An exponential family separates the data-dependent quantities from their parameter-dependent coefficients in the log density. This structure makes sufficiency, likelihood, and the local geometry accessible through one function.

Begin with a sufficient statistic¶

Let X1,…,XnX_1,\ldots,X_nX1​,…,Xn​ be independent Bernoulli variables with success probability q∈(0,1)q\in(0,1)q∈(0,1). Their joint mass function is

∏i=1nqxi(1−q)1−xi=exp⁡ ⁣{log⁡q1−q∑i=1nxi+nlog⁡(1−q)}.\prod_{i=1}^n q^{x_i}(1-q)^{1-x_i} =\exp\!\left\{ \log\frac{q}{1-q}\sum_{i=1}^n x_i+n\log(1-q) \right\}.i=1∏n​qxi​(1−q)1−xi​=exp{log1−qq​i=1∑n​xi​+nlog(1−q)}.
(1)

The sample enters the parameter-dependent part only through the count Sn=∑ixiS_n=\sum_i x_iSn​=∑i​xi​. Conditional on Sn=sS_n=sSn​=s, all binary sequences with sss successes have equal probability, independent of qqq. This directly shows that SnS_nSn​ is sufficient: the conditional distribution of the full data given the statistic contains no further dependence on the parameter.

Set θ=log⁡(q/(1−q))\theta=\log(q/(1-q))θ=log(q/(1−q)). For a single observation,

pθ(x)=exp⁡{θx−ψ(θ)},ψ(θ)=log⁡(1+eθ),x∈{0,1}.p_\theta(x)=\exp\{\theta x-\psi(\theta)\},\qquad \psi(\theta)=\log(1+e^\theta),\qquad x\in\{0,1\}.pθ​(x)=exp{θx−ψ(θ)},ψ(θ)=log(1+eθ),x∈{0,1}.
(2)

The subtraction of ψ\psiψ normalizes the two masses. This calculation motivates the general form; it does not derive every exponential family from an arbitrary sufficient statistic.

The general form and its domain¶

Fix a measure ν\nuν, a nonnegative carrier hhh, and a vector-valued statistic T:X→RdT:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^dT:X→Rd. Define

pθ(x)=h(x)exp⁡{θTT(x)−ψ(θ)},ψ(θ)=log⁡∫Xh(x)eθTT(x) dν(x).\begin{aligned} p_\theta(x)&=h(x)\exp\{\theta^\mathsf{T}T(x)-\psi(\theta)\},\\ \psi(\theta)&=\log\int_{\mathcal X} h(x)e^{\theta^\mathsf{T}T(x)}\,d\nu(x). \end{aligned}pθ​(x)ψ(θ)​=h(x)exp{θTT(x)−ψ(θ)},=log∫X​h(x)eθTT(x)dν(x).​
(3)

The natural parameter domain consists of parameters where the integral is positive and finite. We work on its nonempty open interior Θ\ThetaΘ and assume the support {h>0}\{h>0\}{h>0} is fixed. On this interior the integral admits local differentiation of all orders; exponential integrability in a neighborhood controls the moments of TTT. This is the regular setting used below.

The domain is convex: Hölder’s inequality bounds the normalizing integral at a convex combination of two parameters by the corresponding geometric mean of their integrals. In particular, ψ\psiψ is convex.

For independent observations from this family, the density factors as

(∏i=1nh(xi))exp⁡ ⁣{θT∑i=1nT(xi)−nψ(θ)}.\left(\prod_{i=1}^n h(x_i)\right) \exp\!\left\{\theta^\mathsf{T}\sum_{i=1}^nT(x_i)-n\psi(\theta)\right\}.(i=1∏n​h(xi​))exp{θTi=1∑n​T(xi​)−nψ(θ)}.
(4)

The factorization criterion for dominated models therefore makes ∑iT(Xi)\sum_i T(X_i)∑i​T(Xi​) sufficient. Its dimension stays fixed as nnn grows. Sufficiency alone is a broader concept: the full data are always sufficient, and converse characterizations of exponential families require additional hypotheses.

Differentiating the normalizer¶

Write Z(θ)=eψ(θ)Z(\theta)=e^{\psi(\theta)}Z(θ)=eψ(θ). Differentiation under the integral yields

∂iψ(θ)=∂iZ(θ)Z(θ)=∫Ti(x)pθ(x) dν(x)=Eθ[Ti(X)].\partial_i\psi(\theta) =\frac{\partial_iZ(\theta)}{Z(\theta)} =\int T_i(x)p_\theta(x)\,d\nu(x) =\mathbb E_\theta[T_i(X)].∂i​ψ(θ)=Z(θ)∂i​Z(θ)​=∫Ti​(x)pθ​(x)dν(x)=Eθ​[Ti​(X)].
(5)

Define the expectation parameter η(θ)=Eθ[T(X)]\eta(\theta)=\mathbb E_\theta[T(X)]η(θ)=Eθ​[T(X)]. Since ∂jpθ=pθ(Tj−ηj)\partial_jp_\theta=p_\theta(T_j-\eta_j)∂j​pθ​=pθ​(Tj​−ηj​), a second derivative gives

η=∇ψ,∂i∂jψ=Eθ[TiTj]−Eθ[Ti]Eθ[Tj].\eta=\nabla\psi,\qquad \partial_i\partial_j\psi =\mathbb E_\theta[T_iT_j]-\mathbb E_\theta[T_i]\mathbb E_\theta[T_j].η=∇ψ,∂i​∂j​ψ=Eθ​[Ti​Tj​]−Eθ​[Ti​]Eθ​[Tj​].
(6)

Thus ∇2ψ=Cov⁡θ(T)\nabla^2\psi=\operatorname{Cov}_\theta(T)∇2ψ=Covθ​(T) is positive semidefinite. For any vector aaa,

aT∇2ψ a=Var⁡θ(aTT).a^\mathsf{T}\nabla^2\psi\,a =\operatorname{Var}_\theta(a^\mathsf{T}T).aT∇2ψa=Varθ​(aTT).
(7)

The representation is minimal if no nonzero aaa makes aTTa^\mathsf{T}TaTT constant almost everywhere on the support. Under minimality the variance above is positive for every a≠0a\ne0a=0, so ψ\psiψ is strictly convex and its gradient has an invertible derivative. Natural and expectation parameters are then smooth local coordinates for the same family. Strict convexity also makes ∇ψ\nabla\psi∇ψ injective on Θ\ThetaΘ; its image must still be distinguished from boundary moments or the entire set of conceivable moment vectors.

The score, the derivative of the log density, is

sθ(x)=∇θlog⁡pθ(x)=T(x)−η(θ).s_\theta(x)=\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(x)=T(x)-\eta(\theta).sθ​(x)=∇θ​logpθ​(x)=T(x)−η(θ).

Its covariance is the Fisher information matrix in natural coordinates: Iθ=∇2ψI_\theta=\nabla^2\psiIθ​=∇2ψ. The Fisher note derives this metric for general regular models.

Two examples¶

For Bernoulli observations, direct differentiation gives

η=ψ′(θ)=eθ1+eθ=q,ψ′′(θ)=q(1−q).\eta=\psi'(\theta)=\frac{e^\theta}{1+e^\theta}=q,\qquad \psi''(\theta)=q(1-q).η=ψ′(θ)=1+eθeθ​=q,ψ′′(θ)=q(1−q).

The expectation coordinate is the success probability. Natural coordinates range over R\mathbb RR, while expectation coordinates range over (0,1)(0,1)(0,1).

The ambient potential still exists, but the restricted model does not automatically inherit the full family’s affine coordinates.

Likelihood becomes moment matching¶

For observed data, let T‾=n−1∑iT(xi)\overline T=n^{-1}\sum_iT(x_i)T=n−1∑i​T(xi​). Up to a parameter-independent term, the average log likelihood is

ℓn(θ)=θTT‾−ψ(θ)+constant.\ell_n(\theta)=\theta^\mathsf{T}\overline T-\psi(\theta)+\text{constant}.ℓn​(θ)=θTT−ψ(θ)+constant.
(11)

An interior optimum of a full family must satisfy

∇ψ(θ^)=T‾.\nabla\psi(\widehat\theta)=\overline T.∇ψ(θ)=T.
(12)

Its Hessian is −∇2ψ-\nabla^2\psi−∇2ψ, so in a minimal family an interior solution is the unique maximum. Existence is a separate question. If all Bernoulli observations are successes, then T‾=1\overline T=1T=1 lies outside (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) and the likelihood supremum is approached as θ→+∞\theta\to+\inftyθ→+∞; no finite natural parameter attains it.

For a constrained parameterization θ=θ(ϕ)\theta=\theta(\phi)θ=θ(ϕ) with Jacobian JJJ, the stationary equation is instead JT(T‾−η)=0J^\mathsf{T}(\overline T-\eta)=0JT(T−η)=0. Matching every ambient sufficient-statistic moment is generally too strong. This distinction will matter when an EM M-step fits a constrained joint model.

For further reading, Michael I. Jordan’s The Exponential Family: Basics, §§8.3–8.7, treats moment derivatives, sufficiency, and likelihood. That reference uses different symbols for natural and expectation parameters.
