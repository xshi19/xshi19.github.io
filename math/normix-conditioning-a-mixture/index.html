Conditioning a mixture

Fix the model first¶

Let ZZZ be standard normal and let W≥0W\geq0W≥0 be independent of ZZZ. For constants μ,β∈R\mu,\beta\in\mathbb Rμ,β∈R and σ>0\sigma>0σ>0, define

X=μ+βW+σW Z.X=\mu+\beta W+\sigma\sqrt W\,Z.X=μ+βW+σW​Z.
(1)

Conditional on W=w>0W=w>0W=w>0, this is normal with mean μ+βw\mu+\beta wμ+βw and variance σ2w\sigma^2wσ2w. At w=0w=0w=0 it is a point mass at μ\muμ. This conditional description does not require choosing a particular distribution for WWW.

One exact calculation¶

Assume in addition that E[W2]<∞\mathbb E[W^2]<\inftyE[W2]<∞. Conditional expectation gives

E[X]=μ+βE[W].\mathbb E[X]=\mu+\beta\mathbb E[W].E[X]=μ+βE[W].
(2)

The law of total variance separates variation inside each conditional normal from variation of its mean:

Var⁡(X)=E[Var⁡(X∣W)]+Var⁡(E[X∣W])=σ2E[W]+β2Var⁡(W).\operatorname{Var}(X) =\mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(X\mid W)] +\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[X\mid W]) =\sigma^2\mathbb E[W]+\beta^2\operatorname{Var}(W).Var(X)=E[Var(X∣W)]+Var(E[X∣W])=σ2E[W]+β2Var(W).
(3)

For the limiting case W=1W=1W=1 almost surely, this reduces to a normal variable with mean μ+β\mu+\betaμ+β and variance σ2\sigma^2σ2. The second-moment assumption is a sufficient condition for the displayed calculation, not a definition of every possible normal mixture.

The normal-mixtures note extends this calculation to vectors. The GH note uses the literature symbols Y=WY=WY=W, γ=β\gamma=\betaγ=β, and Σ=σ2\Sigma=\sigma^2Σ=σ2 in one dimension, while keeping ZZZ standard normal and independent. Choosing a GIG law for the mixing variable leads to the posterior calculations in EM for GH.
