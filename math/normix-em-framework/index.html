EM as sufficient-statistic updates

EM separates posterior inference from maximization. A further separation is useful when observations arrive in batches or when estimation is regularized: aggregate posterior statistics, transform that aggregate, then recover model parameters. This mathematical extract of the upstream design develops general and affine update rules and their composition with Bregman shrinkage.

The prerequisites are batch EM, exponential-family coordinates, and mixture architecture. The full upstream framework owns the software interfaces, fitting loops, and product options.

From a batch to a model¶

For a complete-data exponential family with statistic t(X,Y)t(X,Y)t(X,Y), observation batch Bt\mathcal B_tBt​, and current classical parameter ut−1u_{t-1}ut−1​, compute

η^t=1∣Bt∣∑x∈BtEut−1[t(X,Y)∣X=x].\widehat\eta_t=\frac1{|\mathcal B_t|}\sum_{x\in\mathcal B_t} \mathbb E_{u_{t-1}}[t(X,Y)\mid X=x].η​t​=∣Bt​∣1​x∈Bt​∑​Eut−1​​[t(X,Y)∣X=x].
(1)

The aggregate contains only the statistic blocks needed by the M-step. The GH convention orders them as t=(log⁡Y,Y−1,Y,X,X/Y,XX⊤/Y)t=(\log Y,Y^{-1},Y,X,X/Y,XX^\top/Y)t=(logY,Y−1,Y,X,X/Y,XX⊤/Y). YYY is the mixing variable WWW in the conditioning note; ZZZ remains standard-normal noise. Factor analysis adds latent-factor cross moments to these six blocks.

After an update rule produces a statistic vector sss, recover parameters by

M(s)∈arg max⁡u{⟨θ(u),s⟩−ψ(θ(u))}.\mathcal M(s)\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_u \{\langle\theta(u),s\rangle-\psi(\theta(u))\}.M(s)∈uargmax​{⟨θ(u),s⟩−ψ(θ(u))}.
(2)

Here θ(u)\theta(u)θ(u) is the natural-coordinate embedding. In an unconstrained minimal family with an attained interior solution, θ(M(s))=∇ϕ(s)\theta(\mathcal M(s))=\nabla\phi(s)θ(M(s))=∇ϕ(s). In a curved family, M\mathcal MM solves the constrained problem; sss is not generally the expectation vector of the recovered model. We use the name η\etaη for the running statistic in the update rules, without asserting ambient moment matching in that case.

Two layers of update rules¶

The general layer is a map with optional state qtq_tqt​:

(ηt,qt)=Ut(ηt−1,η^t,qt−1).(\eta_t,q_t)=\mathcal U_t(\eta_{t-1},\widehat\eta_t,q_{t-1}).(ηt​,qt​)=Ut​(ηt−1​,η​t​,qt−1​).

ηt=at+Btηt−1+Ctη^t,\eta_t=a_t+B_t\eta_{t-1}+C_t\widehat\eta_t,ηt​=at​+Bt​ηt−1​+Ct​η​t​,

A scalar convex combination of feasible statistics preserves their convex moment constraints. Arbitrary offsets, operators, or independent block weights need not preserve positive-definiteness, moment inequalities, or existence of an interior maximizing map. The general update notation alone provides no likelihood-ascent or convergence theorem.

Scalar shrinkage as a Bregman penalty¶

Let η0=∇ψ(θ0)\eta_0=\nabla\psi(\theta_0)η0​=∇ψ(θ0​) be a reference expectation vector and τ≥0\tau\geq0τ≥0. The penalized EM derivation maximizes

⟨θ,η^t⟩−ψ(θ)−τDψ(θ∥θ0)=⟨θ,η^t+τη0⟩−(1+τ)ψ(θ)+constant.\langle\theta,\widehat\eta_t\rangle-\psi(\theta) -\tau D_\psi(\theta\Vert\theta_0) =\langle\theta,\widehat\eta_t+\tau\eta_0\rangle -(1+\tau)\psi(\theta)+\text{constant}.⟨θ,η​t​⟩−ψ(θ)−τDψ​(θ∥θ0​)=⟨θ,η​t​+τη0​⟩−(1+τ)ψ(θ)+constant.
(5)

Dividing by 1+τ1+\tau1+τ makes it ordinary parameter recovery at Sτ(η^t)S_\tau(\widehat\eta_t)Sτ​(η​t​), where

Sτ(v)=v+τη01+τ.S_\tau(v)=\frac{v+\tau\eta_0}{1+\tau}.Sτ​(v)=1+τv+τη0​​.

With a fresh full-data E-step and exact or improving penalized maximization, this is EM for the observed likelihood minus the fixed complete-data KL penalty. Composing shrinkage with a running rule¶

Apply the base rule first, then shrink its statistic output:

(vt,qt)=Ut(ηt−1,η^t,qt−1),ηt=Sτ(vt),ut=M(ηt).\begin{aligned} (v_t,q_t)&=\mathcal U_t(\eta_{t-1},\widehat\eta_t,q_{t-1}),\\ \eta_t&=S_\tau(v_t),\qquad u_t=\mathcal M(\eta_t). \end{aligned}(vt​,qt​)ηt​​=Ut​(ηt−1​,η​t​,qt−1​),=Sτ​(vt​),ut​=M(ηt​).​
(7)

For an affine base, the composed coefficients are explicit:

ηt=at+τη01+τ+Bt1+τηt−1+Ct1+τη^t.\eta_t=\frac{a_t+\tau\eta_0}{1+\tau} +\frac{B_t}{1+\tau}\eta_{t-1} +\frac{C_t}{1+\tau}\widehat\eta_t.ηt​=1+τat​+τη0​​+1+τBt​​ηt−1​+1+τCt​​η​t​.
(8)

Thus one composition handles ordinary EM, forgetting, and sample-weighted updates. If the base is nonlinear, SτS_\tauSτ​ is still affine in its own argument, but the complete composed rule need not be affine in its inputs. The base rule’s auxiliary state passes through this construction.

The interpretation depends on the base. With ordinary batch EM, the penalty proof applies to the current observed-data objective. With running statistics, the same algebra describes a regularized surrogate, but does not establish ascent of a fixed observed-data likelihood.

Composition order also matters. Shrinking a running average gives

ηt=(1−ρt)ηt−1+ρtη^t+τη01+τ.\eta_t=\frac{(1-\rho_t)\eta_{t-1}+\rho_t\widehat\eta_t+\tau\eta_0} {1+\tau}.ηt​=1+τ(1−ρt​)ηt−1​+ρt​η​t​+τη0​​.
(9)

Updating toward a shrunk new aggregate instead gives

ηt=(1−ρt)ηt−1+ρtη^t+τη01+τ.\eta_t=(1-\rho_t)\eta_{t-1} +\rho_t\frac{\widehat\eta_t+\tau\eta_0}{1+\tau}.ηt​=(1−ρt​)ηt−1​+ρt​1+τη​t​+τη0​​.
(10)

The reference weights are respectively τ/(1+τ)\tau/(1+\tau)τ/(1+τ) and ρtτ/(1+τ)\rho_t\tau/(1+\tau)ρt​τ/(1+τ). Repeated shrinkage of the whole running statistic can dominate a vanishing data step: for fixed τ>0\tau>0τ>0, bounded new statistics, and ρt→0\rho_t\to0ρt​→0, the first recurrence tends to η0\eta_0η0​. These schemes therefore need separate choices of schedule and objective.

Blockwise rules and algorithmic limits¶

For nonnegative block weights τj\tau_jτj​, one can write

ηt,j=vt,j+τjη0,j1+τj.\eta_{t,j}=\frac{v_{t,j}+\tau_j\eta_{0,j}}{1+\tau_j}.ηt,j​=1+τj​vt,j​+τj​η0,j​​.
(11)

This defines a blockwise statistic transformation. It is not generally equivalent to the scalar joint-KL penalty, because ψ\psiψ couples statistic blocks. A separable potential or another explicit penalty derivation would be needed for such a claim. Moment feasibility must also be checked. The covariance-only example explains when changing the sixth GH block alone gives a direct dispersion shrinkage formula.

Batch EM refreshes posterior statistics for all observations at each step. Incremental or mini-batch schemes refresh only the selected observations, retain a running aggregate, and apply M\mathcal MM to that aggregate. Their fixed points, numerical cost, and convergence depend on the update schedule and on whether old posterior contributions are revisited. Factor constraints, approximate solvers, and post-update transformations must each be assessed against the actual optimization problem.

For numerical inversion and implementation details, see the upstream Bessel and solver design, full EM framework, and package API.
