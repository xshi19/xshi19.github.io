Normal variance–mean mixtures

Variance gamma, normal-inverse gamma, normal-inverse Gaussian, and generalized hyperbolic distributions share a conditional Gaussian construction. This note extracts its moment and probability identities from the upstream tutorial. Begin with conditioning a mixture for the scalar example, or the family tour for the choice of positive mixing law.

The model and notation¶

Let Z∼Nd(0,Id)Z\sim\mathcal N_d(0,I_d)Z∼Nd​(0,Id​) be independent of a nonnegative random variable YYY. Given μ,γ∈Rd\mu,\gamma\in\mathbb R^dμ,γ∈Rd and LL⊤=Σ≻0LL^\top=\Sigma\succ0LL⊤=Σ≻0, define

X=μ+γY+Y LZ.X=\mu+\gamma Y+\sqrt Y\,LZ.X=μ+γY+Y​LZ.
(1)

For d=1d=1d=1, use Y=WY=WY=W, γ=β\gamma=\betaγ=β, and L=σL=\sigmaL=σ, so this is exactly X=μ+βW+σW ZX=\mu+\beta W+\sigma\sqrt W\,ZX=μ+βW+σW​Z from the conditioning note. Literature that writes a noise vector with covariance Σ\SigmaΣ has absorbed LLL into it. For y>0y>0y>0, the conditional law is normal with mean μ+γy\mu+\gamma yμ+γy and covariance yΣy\SigmayΣ; at y=0y=0y=0 it is a point mass at μ\muμ.

The four named families in the source use strictly positive mixing variables. They are not the entire set of normal mixtures. In particular, taking YYY constant or discrete also fits (1).

Mean and covariance by conditioning¶

Assume E[Y2]<∞\mathbb E[Y^2]<\inftyE[Y2]<∞, a sufficient condition for all terms below. The laws of total expectation and total covariance give

E[X]=E[μ+γY]=μ+γE[Y],Cov⁡(X)=E[Cov⁡(X∣Y)]+Cov⁡(E[X∣Y])=E[Y]Σ+Var⁡(Y)γγ⊤.\begin{aligned} \mathbb E[X]&=\mathbb E[\mu+\gamma Y]=\mu+\gamma\mathbb E[Y],\\ \operatorname{Cov}(X) &=\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(X\mid Y)] +\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbb E[X\mid Y])\\ &=\mathbb E[Y]\Sigma+\operatorname{Var}(Y)\gamma\gamma^\top. \end{aligned}E[X]Cov(X)​=E[μ+γY]=μ+γE[Y],=E[Cov(X∣Y)]+Cov(E[X∣Y])=E[Y]Σ+Var(Y)γγ⊤.​
(2)

The first term averages variation within conditional normals. The second is a positive-semidefinite rank-at-most-one contribution from variation in their means. Thus μ\muμ is a location parameter and Σ\SigmaΣ a conditional scale matrix; neither is generally the corresponding marginal moment.

If γ=0\gamma=0γ=0, only E[Y]<∞\mathbb E[Y]<\inftyE[Y]<∞ is needed for the covariance formula Cov⁡(X)=E[Y]Σ\operatorname{Cov}(X)=\mathbb E[Y]\SigmaCov(X)=E[Y]Σ. The stronger second-moment assumption is convenient for the general mean-coupled construction, not part of the definition of a mixture.

For constant mixing Y=c>0Y=c>0Y=c>0, the law reduces to Nd(μ+cγ,cΣ)\mathcal N_d(\mu+c\gamma,c\Sigma)Nd​(μ+cγ,cΣ). This also shows why a model with almost constant mixing can have difficulty separating location from mean coupling.

An exact two-dimensional calculation¶

Take

μ=(00),γ=(3/10−2/5),Σ=(13/103/101).\mu=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad \gamma=\begin{pmatrix}3/10\\-2/5\end{pmatrix},\quad \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&3/10\\3/10&1\end{pmatrix}.μ=(00​),γ=(3/10−2/5​),Σ=(13/10​3/101​).
(3)

For gamma mixing with shape and rate both 3/23/23/2, E[Y]=1\mathbb E[Y]=1E[Y]=1 and Var⁡(Y)=2/3\operatorname{Var}(Y)=2/3Var(Y)=2/3. Therefore

E[X]=(3/10−2/5),Cov⁡(X)=(53/5011/5011/5083/75).\mathbb E[X]=\begin{pmatrix}3/10\\-2/5\end{pmatrix},\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(X)= \begin{pmatrix}53/50&11/50\\11/50&83/75\end{pmatrix}.E[X]=(3/10−2/5​),Cov(X)=(53/5011/50​11/5083/75​).
(4)

The positive conditional off-diagonal entry decreases from 3/103/103/10 to 11/5011/5011/50 because γ1γ2<0\gamma_1\gamma_2<0γ1​γ2​<0. The additional covariance matrix is still positive semidefinite: its off-diagonal entries need not be positive. This is an algebraic example, not an empirical fit or simulation result.

Joint draws, marginal density, and the posterior¶

If the positive mixing law has density ggg, then

f(x,y)=φd(x;μ+γy,yΣ)g(y),fX(x)=∫0∞f(x,y) dy.f(x,y)=\varphi_d(x;\mu+\gamma y,y\Sigma)g(y),\qquad f_X(x)=\int_0^\infty f(x,y)\,dy.f(x,y)=φd​(x;μ+γy,yΣ)g(y),fX​(x)=∫0∞​f(x,y)dy.
(5)

Here φd\varphi_dφd​ is a multivariate normal density. Sampling (X,Y)(X,Y)(X,Y) and retaining XXX has exactly the marginal law. Observing XXX alone, however, does not reveal the latent draw; its uncertainty is represented by

f(y∣x)=φd(x;μ+γy,yΣ)g(y)fX(x)f(y\mid x)=\frac{\varphi_d(x;\mu+\gamma y,y\Sigma)g(y)}{f_X(x)}f(y∣x)=fX​(x)φd​(x;μ+γy,yΣ)g(y)​
(6)

where the denominator is positive and finite. For GIG mixing the posterior is another GIG, as derived in EM. The mixture architecture explains why the joint law provides useful sufficient statistics for estimation of the marginal.

These density formulas assume Y>0Y>0Y>0. If YYY has an atom at zero, the marginal has the corresponding atom at μ\muμ in addition to its positive-mixing part; a Lebesgue density alone does not describe the entire law.

Scalar distribution functions and quantiles¶

In one dimension, for strictly positive YYY, conditioning gives

FX(x)=E ⁣[Φ ⁣(x−μ−γYσY)],σ2=Σ,F_X(x)=\mathbb E\!\left[ \Phi\!\left(\frac{x-\mu-\gamma Y}{\sigma\sqrt Y}\right)\right], \qquad \sigma^2=\Sigma,FX​(x)=E[Φ(σY​x−μ−γY​)],σ2=Σ,
(7)

where Φ\PhiΦ is the standard normal CDF. This identity requires no finite moment of YYY, since the integrand is bounded. A quantile is the generalized inverse

qτ=inf⁡{x:FX(x)≥τ},0<τ<1.q_\tau=\inf\{x:F_X(x)\geq\tau\},\qquad 0<\tau<1.qτ​=inf{x:FX​(x)≥τ},0<τ<1.
(8)

An atom at Y=0Y=0Y=0 would add Pr⁡(Y=0)1x≥μ\Pr(Y=0)\mathbf1_{x\geq\mu}Pr(Y=0)1x≥μ​ to the positive-mixing contribution. The formula explains the scalar probability calculation without prescribing an integration algorithm or package interface.

Estimation and further reading¶

For the four named mixing families, GH theory and its limits give density and higher-moment formulas. EM replaces missing mixing statistics by posterior expectations before maximizing the joint likelihood. Its monotonicity and limitations follow the ordinary EM entry. A fitted location or scale should still be interpreted through (2), not as an automatic estimate of a raw marginal moment.

The upstream normal-mixtures tutorial retains constructors, plots, sampling, CDF/quantile operations, and fitted examples. The package API remains authoritative for implementation. No fitter, executable cell, or notebook image is imported into this note.
