Exponential-family core

A log-partition function connects three descriptions of an exponential family: classical distribution parameters, natural coordinates, and expected sufficient statistics. This note develops that connection for normal and GIG laws, then explains its role in GH estimation. The IG entry on exponential families provides the foundational definitions and regularity assumptions.

One potential, two derivative identities¶

For a fixed carrier hhh and statistic ttt, write

fθ(x)=h(x)e⟨θ,t(x)⟩−ψ(θ),ψ(θ)=log⁡∫h(x)e⟨θ,t(x)⟩ dx.f_\theta(x)=h(x)e^{\langle\theta,t(x)\rangle-\psi(\theta)},\qquad \psi(\theta)=\log\int h(x)e^{\langle\theta,t(x)\rangle}\,dx.fθ​(x)=h(x)e⟨θ,t(x)⟩−ψ(θ),ψ(θ)=log∫h(x)e⟨θ,t(x)⟩dx.
(1)

Work on the nonempty open interior Θ\ThetaΘ of the finite natural-parameter domain. Under these conditions differentiation of the normalizer gives

η=∇ψ(θ)=Eθ[t(X)],Iθ=∇2ψ(θ)=Cov⁡θ(t(X)).\eta=\nabla\psi(\theta)=\mathbb E_\theta[t(X)],\qquad I_\theta=\nabla^2\psi(\theta)=\operatorname{Cov}_\theta(t(X)).η=∇ψ(θ)=Eθ​[t(X)],Iθ​=∇2ψ(θ)=Covθ​(t(X)).
(2)

The score is t(X)−ηt(X)-\etat(X)−η. Thus density normalization, expectation coordinates, and Fisher information come from the same potential. Minimality makes IθI_\thetaIθ​ positive definite; a redundant statistic vector instead gives a singular covariance. These identities do not claim that every distribution, or every marginal of an exponential family, is itself such a family.

Classical parameters such as (p,a,b)(p,a,b)(p,a,b) or (μ,Σ)(\mu,\Sigma)(μ,Σ) express features of the model directly. Their map to natural coordinates is family-specific. Expectation coordinates express moments; their map back to the model depends on whether those moments lie in ∇ψ(Θ)\nabla\psi(\Theta)∇ψ(Θ).

Likelihood and Bregman inversion¶

For independent observations, reduce the data to η^=n−1∑it(xi)\widehat\eta=n^{-1}\sum_i t(x_i)η​=n−1∑i​t(xi​). Maximizing average log likelihood is then equivalent to minimizing

F(θ)=ψ(θ)−⟨θ,η^⟩,θ∈Θ.F(\theta)=\psi(\theta)-\langle\theta,\widehat\eta\rangle, \qquad \theta\in\Theta.F(θ)=ψ(θ)−⟨θ,η​⟩,θ∈Θ.
(3)

If an interior solution θ^\widehat\thetaθ exists, its gradient equation is ∇ψ(θ^)=η^\nabla\psi(\widehat\theta)=\widehat\eta∇ψ(θ)=η​. For a minimal family it is the unique solution. Define the Bregman divergence

Dψ(θ∥θ′)=ψ(θ)−ψ(θ′)−⟨∇ψ(θ′),θ−θ′⟩.D_\psi(\theta\Vert\theta') =\psi(\theta)-\psi(\theta') -\langle\nabla\psi(\theta'),\theta-\theta'\rangle.Dψ​(θ∥θ′)=ψ(θ)−ψ(θ′)−⟨∇ψ(θ′),θ−θ′⟩.
(4)

With θ′=θ^\theta'=\widehat\thetaθ′=θ, F(θ)−F(θ^)=Dψ(θ∥θ^)F(\theta)-F(\widehat\theta)=D_\psi(\theta\Vert\widehat\theta)F(θ)−F(θ)=Dψ​(θ∥θ). This explains the term Bregman inversion. It does not guarantee existence: boundary empirical moments can place the likelihood supremum outside the finite natural-parameter domain.

Where the dual map exists, the Legendre conjugate obeys

ψ∗(η)=⟨θ,η⟩−ψ(θ),∇ψ∗(η)=θ,∇2ψ∗(η)=Iθ−1.\psi^*(\eta)=\langle\theta,\eta\rangle-\psi(\theta),\qquad \nabla\psi^*(\eta)=\theta,\qquad \nabla^2\psi^*(\eta)=I_\theta^{-1}.ψ∗(η)=⟨θ,η⟩−ψ(θ),∇ψ∗(η)=θ,∇2ψ∗(η)=Iθ−1​.
(5)

The duality entry develops the geometric interpretation and its domain limits. For a constrained subfamily θ=θ(ϕ)\theta=\theta(\phi)θ=θ(ϕ), stationarity is instead Jθ(ϕ)⊤(∇ψ−η^)=0J_\theta(\phi)^\top(\nabla\psi-\widehat\eta)=0Jθ​(ϕ)⊤(∇ψ−η​)=0; matching all ambient moments is usually too strong.

Multivariate normal example¶

For X∼Nd(μ,Σ)X\sim\mathcal N_d(\mu,\Sigma)X∼Nd​(μ,Σ) with precision Λ=Σ−1\Lambda=\Sigma^{-1}Λ=Σ−1, use t(x)=(x,xx⊤)t(x)=(x,xx^\top)t(x)=(x,xx⊤) and the vector/trace inner product. Then

θ1=Λμ,θ2=−12Λ,h(x)=1,\theta_1=\Lambda\mu,\qquad \theta_2=-\tfrac12\Lambda,\qquad h(x)=1,θ1​=Λμ,θ2​=−21​Λ,h(x)=1,

η1=μ,η2=Σ+μμ⊤,μ=η1,Σ=η2−η1η1⊤≻0.\eta_1=\mu,\quad \eta_2=\Sigma+\mu\mu^\top,\qquad \mu=\eta_1,\quad \Sigma=\eta_2-\eta_1\eta_1^\top\succ0.η1​=μ,η2​=Σ+μμ⊤,μ=η1​,Σ=η2​−η1​η1⊤​≻0.
(8)

GIG example¶

For the GIG density,

t(y)=(log⁡y,y−1,y)⊤,θ=(p−1,−b/2,−a/2)⊤,t(y)=(\log y,y^{-1},y)^\top,\qquad \theta=(p-1,-b/2,-a/2)^\top,t(y)=(logy,y−1,y)⊤,θ=(p−1,−b/2,−a/2)⊤,
(9)

and

ψ(θ)=log⁡2+log⁡Kp(ab)+p2log⁡(b/a).\psi(\theta)=\log2+\log K_p(\sqrt{ab})+\tfrac p2\log(b/a).ψ(θ)=log2+logKp​(ab​)+2p​log(b/a).
(10)

Its gradient is the log, inverse, and first moment of YYY; its Hessian is their covariance matrix. The inversion is a three-parameter convex problem in natural coordinates after a single reduction of the data. Evaluation of the potential and its derivatives still involves Bessel functions. Strict convexity does not imply a well-conditioned Hessian near a degenerating law.

Reparameterizing constrained coordinates can help maintain feasibility but can alter convexity. If θ=θ(ϕ)\theta=\theta(\phi)θ=θ(ϕ) with Jacobian JJJ, then

∇ϕ2F=J⊤(∇θ2F)J+∑j(∂θjF)∇ϕ2θj.\nabla_\phi^2F =J^\top(\nabla_\theta^2F)J +\sum_j(\partial_{\theta_j}F)\nabla_\phi^2\theta_j.∇ϕ2​F=J⊤(∇θ2​F)J+j∑​(∂θj​​F)∇ϕ2​θj​.
(11)

The extra term vanishes at a stationary point, but matters during optimization. This is one reason the gradient-descent comparison must distinguish the objective from the coordinates and numerical method.

From complete data to EM¶

For a joint exponential family fθ(x,y)f_\theta(x,y)fθ​(x,y), replace the empirical statistic by the posterior average

η^k=1n∑iEθk[t(xi,Y)∣X=xi].\widehat\eta_k=\frac1n\sum_i \mathbb E_{\theta_k}[t(x_i,Y)\mid X=x_i].η​k​=n1​i∑​Eθk​​[t(xi​,Y)∣X=xi​].
(12)

The M-step again minimizes (3), now with η^=η^k\widehat\eta=\widehat\eta_kη​=η​k​. For GH, the normal block can be recovered analytically and the GIG block by moment inversion. The mixture architecture describes why the complete-data and observed-data laws play different roles; the EM note supplies the actual posterior formulas.
