Generalized hyperbolic distribution

The generalized hyperbolic (GH) distribution combines a conditional Gaussian law with a GIG mixing variable. Barndorff-Nielsen introduced the family in Exponentially decreasing distributions for the logarithm of particle size (1977), Proceedings of the Royal Society A 353, 401–419.

Definition as a normal mixture¶

Let Y∼GIG⁡(p,a,b)Y\sim\operatorname{GIG}(p,a,b)Y∼GIG(p,a,b) with a,b>0a,b>0a,b>0, and let Z∼Nd(0,Id)Z\sim\mathcal N_d(0,I_d)Z∼Nd​(0,Id​) be independent of YYY. For μ,γ∈Rd\mu,\gamma\in\mathbb R^dμ,γ∈Rd and LL⊤=Σ≻0LL^\top=\Sigma\succ0LL⊤=Σ≻0, define

X=μ+γY+Y LZ,X∣Y=y∼Nd(μ+γy,yΣ).\begin{aligned} X&=\mu+\gamma Y+\sqrt Y\,LZ,\\ X\mid Y=y&\sim\mathcal N_d(\mu+\gamma y,y\Sigma). \end{aligned}XX∣Y=y​=μ+γY+Y​LZ,∼Nd​(μ+γy,yΣ).​
(1)

This uses standard normal noise, as in conditioning a mixture: in one dimension Y=WY=WY=W, γ=β\gamma=\betaγ=β, L=σL=\sigmaL=σ, and Σ=σ2\Sigma=\sigma^2Σ=σ2. The upstream convention absorbs LLL into the noise and writes Zup=LZ∼Nd(0,Σ)Z_{\mathrm{up}}=LZ\sim\mathcal N_d(0,\Sigma)Zup​=LZ∼Nd​(0,Σ).

The location μ\muμ need not be the marginal mean, and Σ\SigmaΣ need not be the marginal covariance. The vector γ\gammaγ couples the conditional mean to the mixing variable. The parameters (p,a,b)(p,a,b)(p,a,b) control the mixing law. A general positive mixing distribution gives a normal variance–mean mixture; the GIG choice selects GH. This is distinct from restricting attention to finite mixtures with a discrete component label.

Model identifiability¶

For any c>0c>0c>0, replace the latent variable by Y′=cYY'=cYY′=cY. Since Y′∼GIG⁡(p,a/c,bc)Y'\sim\operatorname{GIG}(p,a/c,bc)Y′∼GIG(p,a/c,bc), the transformation

Tc:(μ,γ,Σ,p,a,b)⟼(μ,γ/c,Σ/c,p,a/c,bc)T_c:(\mu,\gamma,\Sigma,p,a,b) \longmapsto(\mu,\gamma/c,\Sigma/c,p,a/c,bc)Tc​:(μ,γ,Σ,p,a,b)⟼(μ,γ/c,Σ/c,p,a/c,bc)
(7)

leaves the distribution of XXX unchanged. Equivalently, (μ,γ/c,Σ/c,p,cδ,ω)(\mu,\gamma/c,\Sigma/c,p,c\delta,\omega)(μ,γ/c,Σ/c,p,cδ,ω) has the same marginal law. The joint law on a fixed coordinate pair (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) changes: its latent coordinate has been rescaled.

The unconstrained marginal parameterization is therefore nonidentifiable. Where the score and Fisher information exist, differentiating along this constant-density curve gives a null score direction and a singular Fisher matrix. This argument identifies a scale redundancy; it does not compute marginal curvature or prove that every remaining parameterization is regular.

One can choose a representative by imposing δ=1\delta=1δ=1, b=1b=1b=1, or ∣Σ∣=1|\Sigma|=1∣Σ∣=1 in the interior. For the determinant convention, take c=∣Σ∣1/dc=|\Sigma|^{1/d}c=∣Σ∣1/d in (7). Such a normalization must transform all coupled parameters. Rescaling Σ\SigmaΣ alone changes the model. Fixing the determinant also does not control the matrix condition number: diag⁡(ε,ε−1)\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{-1})diag(ε,ε−1) has determinant one and becomes arbitrarily ill-conditioned.

Protassov (2004), EM-based maximum likelihood parameter estimation for multivariate generalized hyperbolic distributions, and Hu (2005), Calibration of multivariate generalized hyperbolic distributions using the EM algorithm, discuss constrained estimation. The normalization here follows directly from the displayed latent-variable transformation.

Here μk\mu_kμk​ denotes a central moment, and E[(Y−m1)2Y]=m3−2m1m2+m13\mathbb E[(Y-m_1)^2Y]=m_3-2m_1m_2+m_1^3E[(Y−m1​)2Y]=m3​−2m1​m2​+m13​. For γ=0\gamma=0γ=0, symmetry gives zero skewness, and excess kurtosis is 3E[Y2]/E[Y]2−33\mathbb E[Y^2]/\mathbb E[Y]^2-33E[Y2]/E[Y]2−3. Joint exponential-family form¶

The complete-data density (2) has the form fθ(x,y)=h(x,y)exp⁡{⟨θ,t(x,y)⟩−ψ(θ)}f_\theta(x,y)=h(x,y)\exp\{\langle\theta,t(x,y)\rangle-\psi(\theta)\}fθ​(x,y)=h(x,y)exp{⟨θ,t(x,y)⟩−ψ(θ)}, with

t(x,y)=(log⁡y, y−1, y, x, x/y, xx⊤/y).t(x,y)=(\log y,\ y^{-1},\ y,\ x,\ x/y,\ xx^\top/y).t(x,y)=(logy, y−1, y, x, x/y, xx⊤/y).
(10)

The first three blocks are scalars, the next two are vectors, and the last is a symmetric matrix. Use the trace inner product for matrix blocks. Symmetric matrices have d(d+1)/2d(d+1)/2d(d+1)/2 independent coordinates; treating duplicate off-diagonal entries as independent would introduce an artificial nonminimal representation.

Expanding the quadratic in (2) identifies

θ1=p−1−d/2,θ2=−12(b+μ⊤Σ−1μ),θ3=−12(a+γ⊤Σ−1γ),θ4=Σ−1γ,θ5=Σ−1μ,θ6=−12Σ−1.\begin{aligned} \theta_1&=p-1-d/2,& \theta_2&=-\tfrac12(b+\mu^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mu),\\ \theta_3&=-\tfrac12(a+\gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}\gamma),& \theta_4&=\Sigma^{-1}\gamma,\\ \theta_5&=\Sigma^{-1}\mu,& \theta_6&=-\tfrac12\Sigma^{-1}. \end{aligned}θ1​θ3​θ5​​=p−1−d/2,=−21​(a+γ⊤Σ−1γ),=Σ−1μ,​θ2​θ4​θ6​​=−21​(b+μ⊤Σ−1μ),=Σ−1γ,=−21​Σ−1.​
(11)

With h(x,y)=(2π)−d/21y>0h(x,y)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\mathbf1_{y>0}h(x,y)=(2π)−d/21y>0​ the log-partition is

ψ(θ)=12log⁡∣Σ∣+log⁡2+log⁡Kp(ab)+p2log⁡(b/a)+μ⊤Σ−1γ.\psi(\theta)=\tfrac12\log|\Sigma|+\log2+\log K_p(\sqrt{ab}) +\tfrac p2\log(b/a)+\mu^\top\Sigma^{-1}\gamma.ψ(θ)=21​log∣Σ∣+log2+logKp​(ab​)+2p​log(b/a)+μ⊤Σ−1γ.

This parameterization is defined where the recovered Σ≻0\Sigma\succ0Σ≻0 and a,b>0a,b>0a,b>0. The natural parameters determine the classical parameters uniquely for this joint family. The observable GH family, with all parameters free, does not inherit this complete-data exponential-family representation after integration; see the core note.

Numerical limits and special cases¶

The upstream source reports small distributional errors despite larger errors in some recovered GIG parameters. Those experiments are not rerun here, and do not guarantee stability for every parameter regime. Moment inversion can suffer from cancellation, Bessel overflow, and poorly conditioned covariance matrices. The optimization note separates these issues from likelihood monotonicity.
