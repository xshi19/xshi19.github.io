Entropy, varentropy, and Rényi entropy

This note derives formulas for exponential-family components and joint normal variance–mean mixtures. Entropy is the mean of the surprisal, and varentropy is its variance. For a wider structural account, the upstream source cites Stankyavichyus (2026), Varentropy: Overview, Computational Routes, and Structural Decomposition, a preprint. The derivations below concern the stated model families.

Read the exponential-family core, Fisher geometry, and the joint/marginal distinction first. Monte Carlo examples stay in the upstream varentropy tutorial.

Definitions¶

Let XXX have density p(x)p(x)p(x) with respect to a reference measure μ(dx)\mu(dx)μ(dx). The information content (surprisal) is I(X)=−log⁡p(X)\mathcal{I}(X) = -\log p(X)I(X)=−logp(X). Its mean is the entropy and its variance is the varentropy:

H=E[−log⁡p(X)],VH=Var⁡[−log⁡p(X)].H = \mathbb{E}[-\log p(X)], \qquad V_H = \operatorname{Var}[-\log p(X)].H=E[−logp(X)],VH​=Var[−logp(X)].
(1)

Entropy measures the average surprisal; varentropy measures how much the surprisal fluctuates around that average. Unlike the kurtosis, the varentropy requires only I(X)∈L2\mathcal{I}(X) \in L^2I(X)∈L2, so it stays finite for many heavy-tailed laws whose fourth moment diverges.

The Rényi entropy of order α>0\alpha > 0α>0, α≠1\alpha \neq 1α=1 (Rényi, 1961, On Measures of Entropy and Information), is

Hα=11−αlog⁡∫p(x)α μ(dx),H_\alpha = \frac{1}{1-\alpha} \log \int p(x)^\alpha \, \mu(dx),Hα​=1−α1​log∫p(x)αμ(dx),
(2)

For Lebesgue measure HHH is differential entropy; for a general reference measure it is entropy relative to that measure. Logs are natural. Fix the reference measure throughout. Where the density-power integral is finite in an open interval around 1 and differentiation under the integral is justified, Hα→HH_\alpha\to HHα​→H as α→1\alpha\to1α→1. These regularity assumptions also justify the derivatives and local expansion below.

Density-Power Route¶

All three quantities are governed by the log density-power integral

R(α)=log⁡∫p(x)α μ(dx),R(1)=0.R(\alpha) = \log \int p(x)^\alpha \, \mu(dx), \qquad R(1) = 0.R(α)=log∫p(x)αμ(dx),R(1)=0.
(3)

Writing I=−log⁡p(X)\mathcal{I} = -\log p(X)I=−logp(X), the function R(1−s)R(1-s)R(1−s) is the cumulant-generating function of I\mathcal{I}I, so differentiating at α=1\alpha = 1α=1 gives

H=−R′(1),VH=R′′(1),H = -R'(1), \qquad V_H = R''(1),H=−R′(1),VH​=R′′(1),
(4)

while the Rényi entropy is Hα=R(α)/(1−α)H_\alpha = R(\alpha)/(1-\alpha)Hα​=R(α)/(1−α). The first-order expansion

Hα=H−12VH (α−1)+O ⁣((α−1)2)H_\alpha = H - \tfrac{1}{2} V_H\,(\alpha - 1) + \mathcal{O}\!\left((\alpha-1)^2\right)Hα​=H−21​VH​(α−1)+O((α−1)2)
(5)

shows that the varentropy is (twice the negative of) the slope of the Rényi spectrum at α=1\alpha = 1α=1.

Exponential Family¶

For an exponential family p(x∣θ)=h(x)exp⁡{θ⊤t(x)−ψ(θ)}p(x\mid\theta) = h(x)\exp\{\theta^\top t(x) - \psi(\theta)\}p(x∣θ)=h(x)exp{θ⊤t(x)−ψ(θ)} whose carrier is constant on its support, log⁡h(x)≡b0\log h(x) \equiv b_0logh(x)≡b0​, the normalized density power stays in the family with natural parameter αθ\alpha\thetaαθ, provided this parameter belongs to the natural domain:

R(α)=(α−1) b0+ψ(αθ)−α ψ(θ).R(\alpha) = (\alpha - 1)\,b_0 + \psi(\alpha\theta) - \alpha\,\psi(\theta).R(α)=(α−1)b0​+ψ(αθ)−αψ(θ).
(6)

Substituting into (4) and using η=∇ψ(θ)\eta = \nabla\psi(\theta)η=∇ψ(θ) and the Fisher information I(θ)=∇2ψ(θ)I(\theta) = \nabla^2\psi(\theta)I(θ)=∇2ψ(θ) gives closed forms in terms of the log-partition triad:

H=ψ(θ)−θ⊤η−b0,VH=θ⊤I(θ) θ,Hα=(α−1) b0+ψ(αθ)−α ψ(θ)1−α.\begin{aligned} H &= \psi(\theta) - \theta^\top \eta - b_0, \\ V_H &= \theta^\top I(\theta)\,\theta, \\ H_\alpha &= \frac{(\alpha-1)\,b_0 + \psi(\alpha\theta) - \alpha\,\psi(\theta)}{1-\alpha}. \end{aligned}HVH​Hα​​=ψ(θ)−θ⊤η−b0​,=θ⊤I(θ)θ,=1−α(α−1)b0​+ψ(αθ)−αψ(θ)​.​
(7)

The varentropy identity VH=θ⊤I(θ) θV_H = \theta^\top I(\theta)\,\thetaVH​=θ⊤I(θ)θ holds because the centered information content I(X)−H=−θ⊤{t(X)−η}\mathcal{I}(X) - H = -\theta^\top\{t(X) - \eta\}I(X)−H=−θ⊤{t(X)−η} lies exactly in the span of the score. This covers gamma, inverse gamma, GIG, and multivariate normal families when their carrier is chosen as h=1h=1h=1 on their fixed supports. For a ddd-dimensional normal law, VH=d/2V_H=d/2VH​=d/2.

When log⁡h\log hlogh is not constant, the general variance formula is

VH=θ⊤I(θ)θ+2θ⊤Cov⁡(t(X),log⁡h(X))+Var⁡(log⁡h(X)).V_H=\theta^\top I(\theta)\theta +2\theta^\top\operatorname{Cov}(t(X),\log h(X)) +\operatorname{Var}(\log h(X)).VH​=θ⊤I(θ)θ+2θ⊤Cov(t(X),logh(X))+Var(logh(X)).
(8)

For example, the two-parameter inverse-Gaussian representation has log⁡h(y)=−12log⁡(2π)−32log⁡y\log h(y)=-\tfrac12\log(2\pi)-\tfrac32\log ylogh(y)=−21​log(2π)−23​logy. One can instead use its exact embedding GIG⁡(−1/2,λ/m2,λ)\operatorname{GIG}(-1/2,\lambda/m^2,\lambda)GIG(−1/2,λ/m2,λ) with h=1h=1h=1 and statistic log⁡y\log ylogy. Along the density-power path, the GIG order then varies; holding it at −1/2-1/2−1/2 would omit part of the surprisal.

Varentropy of the GIG Distribution¶

Let Y∼GIG(p,a,b)Y \sim \mathrm{GIG}(p, a, b)Y∼GIG(p,a,b) with density (1) and natural parameters θ=[p−1, −b/2, −a/2]\theta = [p-1,\,-b/2,\,-a/2]θ=[p−1,−b/2,−a/2]. Raising the density to the power α\alphaα keeps it proportional to a GIG density with parameters (1+α(p−1), αa, αb)(1 + \alpha(p-1),\, \alpha a,\, \alpha b)(1+α(p−1),αa,αb) — the escort-closure property. Using the Bessel integral ∫0∞yq−1e−(uy+v/y)/2 dy=2 (v/u)q/2Kq(uv)\int_0^\infty y^{q-1} e^{-(uy + v/y)/2}\,dy = 2\,(v/u)^{q/2} K_q(\sqrt{uv})∫0∞​yq−1e−(uy+v/y)/2dy=2(v/u)q/2Kq​(uv​) (NIST DLMF, 10.32.10, after a change of variable) gives, with z=abz = \sqrt{ab}z=ab​,

R(α)=(1−α)log⁡2+1−α2log⁡ ⁣ba+log⁡K1+α(p−1)(αz)−αlog⁡Kp(z).R(\alpha) = (1-\alpha)\log 2 + \frac{1-\alpha}{2}\log\!\frac{b}{a} + \log K_{1 + \alpha(p-1)}(\alpha z) - \alpha \log K_p(z).R(α)=(1−α)log2+21−α​logab​+logK1+α(p−1)​(αz)−αlogKp​(z).
(9)

Define F(p,z)=log⁡Kp(z)F(p, z) = \log K_p(z)F(p,z)=logKp​(z) and the first-order differential operator

L=(p−1) ∂p+z ∂z.L = (p-1)\,\partial_p + z\,\partial_z.L=(p−1)∂p​+z∂z​.
(10)

Along the escort path α↦(1+α(p−1), αz)\alpha \mapsto (1 + \alpha(p-1),\, \alpha z)α↦(1+α(p−1),αz) the derivatives at α=1\alpha=1α=1 are ddαF=LF\frac{d}{d\alpha}F=LFdαd​F=LF and d2dα2F=(L2−L)F\frac{d^2}{d\alpha^2}F=(L^2-L)Fdα2d2​F=(L2−L)F. The subtraction accounts for the variable coefficients of LLL. All remaining terms of (9) are linear in α\alphaα, so from (4),

  VH{GIG(p,a,b)}=(L2−L)log⁡Kp(z),z=ab.  \boxed{\; V_H\{\mathrm{GIG}(p,a,b)\} = (L^2 - L)\log K_p(z), \qquad z = \sqrt{ab}. \;}VH​{GIG(p,a,b)}=(L2−L)logKp​(z),z=ab​.​
(11)

Expanded, this is the pure second-order form

VH=(p−1)2Fpp+2(p−1) z Fpz+z2Fzz,V_H = (p-1)^2 F_{pp} + 2(p-1)\,z\,F_{pz} + z^2 F_{zz},VH​=(p−1)2Fpp​+2(p−1)zFpz​+z2Fzz​,
(12)

which coincides with the Fisher quadratic form θ⊤I(θ) θ\theta^\top I(\theta)\,\thetaθ⊤I(θ)θ of (7). The entropy follows from H=−R′(1)H = -R'(1)H=−R′(1):

H{GIG(p,a,b)}=log⁡{2Kp(z)}+12log⁡ ⁣ba−Llog⁡Kp(z).H\{\mathrm{GIG}(p,a,b)\} = \log\{2 K_p(z)\} + \tfrac12\log\!\frac{b}{a} - L\log K_p(z).H{GIG(p,a,b)}=log{2Kp​(z)}+21​logab​−LlogKp​(z).
(13)

For a,b>0a,b>0a,b>0, the density decays exponentially in yyy at infinity and in 1/y1/y1/y near zero, so the required logarithmic and power statistics have finite second moments. Thus VHV_HVH​ is finite for every interior GIG law. The proper inverse-gamma boundary a=0a=0a=0, p=−k<0p=-k<0p=−k<0, b>0b>0b>0 also has finite varentropy for every k>0k>0k>0, even when its fourth moment fails (k≤4k\leq4k≤4). That boundary fact follows from the inverse-gamma density, not from substitution of z=0z=0z=0 in an interior Bessel expression. It is not a uniform bound as the shape approaches zero.

Joint Varentropy of Normal Variance-Mean Mixtures¶

Consider the joint law of (2) for a positive mixing variable,

X∣Y=y∼Nd(μ+γy, Σy),Y∼gϑ.X \mid Y = y \sim \mathcal{N}_d(\mu + \gamma y,\ \Sigma y), \qquad Y \sim g_\vartheta.X∣Y=y∼Nd​(μ+γy, Σy),Y∼gϑ​.
(14)

Conditionally on YYY, the quadratic form Q=(X−μ−γY)⊤Σ−1(X−μ−γY)/YQ = (X - \mu - \gamma Y)^\top \Sigma^{-1}(X - \mu - \gamma Y)/YQ=(X−μ−γY)⊤Σ−1(X−μ−γY)/Y is χd2\chi^2_dχd2​ and independent of YYY, so

−log⁡p(X∣Y)=CΣ+d2log⁡Y+12Q,CΣ=d2log⁡(2π)+12log⁡∣Σ∣.-\log p(X \mid Y) = C_\Sigma + \tfrac{d}{2}\log Y + \tfrac12 Q, \qquad C_\Sigma = \tfrac{d}{2}\log(2\pi) + \tfrac12\log|\Sigma|.−logp(X∣Y)=CΣ​+2d​logY+21​Q,CΣ​=2d​log(2π)+21​log∣Σ∣.
(15)

Writing IY=−log⁡gϑ(Y)\mathcal{I}_Y = -\log g_\vartheta(Y)IY​=−loggϑ​(Y) for the mixing-law surprisal, the joint information content is IX,Y=CΣ+IY+d2log⁡Y+12Q\mathcal{I}_{X,Y} = C_\Sigma + \mathcal{I}_Y + \tfrac{d}{2}\log Y + \tfrac12 QIX,Y​=CΣ​+IY​+2d​logY+21​Q. Since Var⁡(12Q)=d/2\operatorname{Var}(\tfrac12 Q) = d/2Var(21​Q)=d/2 and Q⊥YQ \perp YQ⊥Y,

  VH(X,Y)=d2+Var⁡ ⁣[IY+d2log⁡Y].  \boxed{\; V_H(X, Y) = \frac{d}{2} + \operatorname{Var}\!\left[\mathcal{I}_Y + \frac{d}{2}\log Y\right]. \;}VH​(X,Y)=2d​+Var[IY​+2d​logY].​
(16)

The representation is X=μ+γY+YLZX=\mu+\gamma Y+\sqrt Y LZX=μ+γY+Y​LZ, with LL⊤=ΣLL^\top=\SigmaLL⊤=Σ, Z∼Nd(0,Id)Z\sim\mathcal N_d(0,I_d)Z∼Nd​(0,Id​) independent of YYY, and Σ\SigmaΣ positive definite. Thus Q=Z⊤ZQ=Z^\top ZQ=Z⊤Z is independent of YYY. The notation maps to W=YW=YW=Y, β=γ\beta=\gammaβ=γ, σ2=Σ\sigma^2=\Sigmaσ2=Σ in the scalar conditioning note.

The Gaussian layer contributes exactly d/2d/2d/2 to joint varentropy. For a fixed mixing law, μ\muμ, γ\gammaγ, and Σ\SigmaΣ all drop out of this variance. The joint entropy is

H(X,Y)=H(Y)+d2log⁡(2πe)+12log⁡∣Σ∣+d2E[log⁡Y],H(X,Y)=H(Y)+\frac d2\log(2\pi e)+\frac12\log|\Sigma| +\frac d2\mathbb E[\log Y],H(X,Y)=H(Y)+2d​log(2πe)+21​log∣Σ∣+2d​E[logY],
(17)

when the terms are finite. In particular, μ\muμ and γ\gammaγ also drop out of joint entropy; only Σ\SigmaΣ contributes among the normal parameters. More generally, Gaussian integration gives the joint density-power route

RX,Y(α)=(1−α)CΣ−d2log⁡α+log⁡∫0∞gϑ(y)αy(1−α)d/2 dy.\begin{aligned} R_{X,Y}(\alpha) &=(1-\alpha)C_\Sigma-\frac d2\log\alpha\\ &\quad+\log\int_0^\infty g_\vartheta(y)^\alpha y^{(1-\alpha)d/2}\,dy. \end{aligned}RX,Y​(α)​=(1−α)CΣ​−2d​logα+log∫0∞​gϑ​(y)αy(1−α)d/2dy.​

For GIG mixing, IY=ψGIG(θ)−θ⊤t(Y)\mathcal{I}_Y = \psi_{\mathrm{GIG}}(\theta) - \theta^\top t(Y)IY​=ψGIG​(θ)−θ⊤t(Y) with t(Y)=[log⁡Y, Y−1, Y]t(Y) = [\log Y,\, Y^{-1},\, Y]t(Y)=[logY,Y−1,Y], so adding d2log⁡Y\tfrac{d}{2}\log Y2d​logY shifts only the coefficient of log⁡Y\log YlogY:

(19)

Its variance is the shifted Fisher quadratic form, and translating to classical coordinates gives the operator LdL_dLd​ of (10) with the order shifted by −d/2-d/2−d/2:

  VH(X,Y)=d2+(Ld2−Ld)log⁡Kp(z),Ld=(p−1−d2)∂p+z ∂z.  \boxed{\; V_H(X, Y) = \frac{d}{2} + (L_d^2 - L_d)\log K_p(z), \qquad L_d = \left(p - 1 - \tfrac{d}{2}\right)\partial_p + z\,\partial_z. \;}VH​(X,Y)=2d​+(Ld2​−Ld​)logKp​(z),Ld​=(p−1−2d​)∂p​+z∂z​.​

For d=0d = 0d=0 this reduces to the GIG varentropy (11). The operator shift p−1↦p−1−d/2p - 1 \mapsto p - 1 - d/2p−1↦p−1−d/2 is exactly the effect of the conditional Gaussian volume factor Y−d/2Y^{-d/2}Y−d/2, and mirrors the natural parameter θ1=p−1−d/2\theta_1 = p - 1 - d/2θ1​=p−1−d/2 of the joint exponential family (11). The variance-gamma and normal-inverse-gamma joints arise at the proper boundaries b=0b=0b=0, p>0p>0p>0 and a=0a=0a=0, p<0p<0p<0, respectively; they require their own log-partition formulas. Normal-inverse-Gaussian mixing has p=−1/2p=-1/2p=−1/2 and can be evaluated through the GH embedding so that the order varies faithfully along the density-power path.

Coordinate and moment limits¶

Under an invertible affine change U=AX+cU=AX+cU=AX+c of a continuous vector, H(U)=H(X)+log⁡∣det⁡A∣H(U)=H(X)+\log|\det A|H(U)=H(X)+log∣detA∣ and VH(U)=VH(X)V_H(U)=V_H(X)VH​(U)=VH​(X), because the Jacobian contributes a constant. A nonlinear transformation generally adds a random log-Jacobian and can change varentropy. All formulas above use the stated Lebesgue coordinates, rather than a coordinate-free information measure.

Finiteness of surprisal variance is different from finiteness of XXX’s fourth moment. It can make varentropy useful for heavy-tailed families, but does not by itself order all distributions by tail weight or guarantee accurate finite-sample estimation. The GH family tour identifies the mixing boundaries; online EM and shrinkage concern parameter estimation rather than an estimator or convergence guarantee for these information quantities.
