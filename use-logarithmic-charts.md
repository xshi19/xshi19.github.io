## How to use it

Take Yahoo Finance as an example. Click a stock chart to enter the page shown below. Click Settings in the upper right.

Then select Logarithmic, as indicated by the red line.

## Why use a logarithmic scale?

This article does not discuss any mathematical theory. Just visually, the biggest reason to use a logarithmic scale is that the same vertical distance represents the same percentage gain or loss at any point in time.

For example, in the chart above, a stock goes from 1 to 10, then from 10 to 100. Both are tenfold increases. Buying at point A and selling at point B gives you the same return as buying at point B and selling at point C. You cannot see this at all on a regular scale (left), but the distances are consistent on a logarithmic scale (right). Conversely, the losses from C to B and from B to A are also the same.

Because of this, when stock prices are displayed on a regular scale, the historical price movements you see, such as a 10% rise or a 10% fall, are distorted. A 10% fall a year ago does not look the same as a 10% fall today.

## Value investors

Why does this matter even to value investors who do not spend much time looking at historical data? Because it helps you judge what counts as “noise,” making it easier to hold stocks for the long term.

Back to that terrifying big circle Gary drew at the beginning. On a logarithmic scale, it becomes the red circle on the left below. The pitifully small red circle on the right is the “sharp” drop after the launch event that caused such a stir recently.

The logarithmic scale shows that Tesla's real gains came in two periods, 2013–2014 and 2019–2021, both on the order of 10x. Most of the rest can be considered “noise.”
